AI Watermarks．How They Work

A source-checked guide to Unicode markers, KGW and SynthID-Text, with a recalculated probability example and clear limits on what watermark detection proves.
Source: YouTube Reviewed: 2026-10-31

TL;DR

A text watermark can be spread across token choices, with no extra invisible character. A matching detector tests whether those choices align unusually well with a keyed pattern.

The video explains the central intuition well, but its shortcuts need qualifications. A signal supports possible involvement of a particular watermarking process. A missing signal does not establish human authorship. Accuracy still requires checking the underlying claims.

1. Three mechanisms with different failure modes

U+200B is a zero-width space; U+202F is a narrow no-break space with width. Such characters have legitimate formatting uses. Their presence alone does not prove intentional vendor watermarking, and deleting format characters indiscriminately can damage text. See Unicode.

Replacing a character can remove a signal carried only by that character. A statistical watermark resides in word choices, so whitespace cleanup generally leaves it untouched. Signed provenance operates at a different layer again.

2. KGW: softly favor a changing green list

The 2023 Kirchenbauer et al. paper uses context-dependent token lists. Its soft variant adds a bias δ to green-token logits before softmax; it does not permanently ban words or add a fixed number of probability points. The green fraction γ is configurable, not necessarily one half.

Detection reconstructs the lists and tests excess green tokens under a null model. The basic demonstration can use public rules; a private key is a deployment choice. Low-entropy passages offer fewer choices. Increasing bias trades generation quality against detectability; a red token alone does not disprove a watermark.

3. Pseudorandom sampling and SynthID-Text

Read the tree: each leaf is a draw, so a token can appear more than once. In layer one, “nice” beats “good” and “great” beats “good.” Layer two computes new scores, so “nice” can now score 0. Across a passage, the winning tokens tend to score higher under the same keyed rules. The detector tests that pattern rather than looking for a hidden symbol.

Scott Aaronson’s 2022 account describes keyed pseudorandom choices and an aggregate score. It does not establish a historical product deployment. The video’s inverse-CDF analogy is illustrative: the same preceding token alone does not fix the next output, because its model distribution depends on broader context. Detection is not generally exact token-by-token replay.

The 2024 Nature paper introduces SynthID-Text. The video illustrates a tournament with eight candidates; that is not a required candidate count. Keyed scores depend on context, candidate and layer.

Non-distortion is an averaged distribution guarantee with conditions, not identical probabilities for every fixed key and context. Sequence guarantees require handling repeated contexts. The paper also reports an inter-response diversity trade-off; “no impact whatsoever” overstates the result.

The official tournament notes explain context masking. The score-update implementation reweights distributions layer by layer without materializing a huge tournament tree. Public code checked on this date does not establish commercial deployment parameters.

4. Recalculate the 155-of-200 example

For an illustrative model, assume independent trials with X ~ Binomial(200, 0.5). The relevant one-sided tail is:

P(X ≥ 155) = Σ[k=155…200] C(200,k) / 2²⁰⁰ ≈ 1.19858 × 10⁻¹⁵
Its reciprocal is about 8.34322 × 10¹⁴; the usual z score is 7.78. Four all-green trials have probability 1/16.

The automatic transcript may have omitted a Chinese large-number unit. If the speaker meant roughly eight hundred trillion, the magnitude agrees. Without replaying the exact audio, this review does not declare a spoken arithmetic error or repeat the lottery analogy.

This tail probability is not the probability that a person wrote the passage. Real detection must account for repeated contexts, dependencies and multiple testing. The idealized number is not an operational false-positive rate.

5. Practical use and complementary evidence

My engineering recommendation: report “signal under this key” or “insufficient evidence,” with detector version, passage length and threshold. Handle short passages, quotations, translation, code and mixed authorship explicitly. Consequential authorship decisions need human review and corroboration.

MethodUseful forRemaining gap
Statistical watermarkEvidence of a particular generation processMatching key/method required; editing may weaken it
Signed provenanceSigner and integrity since signingRequires trust in signer; does not establish truth
Revision history and citationsEditing history and factual verificationRecords must exist; absence alone proves little

A writing-style AI classifier is a separate inference, not keyed watermark verification. The official SynthID reference implementation explicitly says it is not intended for production use. Evaluate false positives, misses and quality on your own corpus before adoption.

6. How to read the video

It offers a useful intuition for signals distributed across sampling decisions. Building a detector or judging authorship requires more: a threat model, calibration and full algorithmic conditions. Popular explanations are not implementation specifications.

The closing discussion of artistic value is an opinion rather than a testable watermark claim. My view: provenance and quality deserve separate examination. Knowing that AI participated does not establish accuracy, usefulness or original contribution.
