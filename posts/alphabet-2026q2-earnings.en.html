TL;DR · What Actually Mattered

🎭 The "3x earnings blowout" is an accounting event, not an operating one. GAAP diluted EPS was $9.11, of which $6.26 came from $99.0B of unrealized equity gains booked in Other income — mostly Alphabet's ~14% stake in Anthropic, whose $65B raise lifted its valuation from $380B to $965B, putting the stake's fair value at $124.3B. Strip it out and core EPS ≈ $2.85 vs. $2.88 expected — a 1% miss. The stock went $341.91 → $318.34 (−6.9%) the next day. The market did the same arithmetic.

💸 Capex raised for a third straight quarter, FCF negative for the first time, and the cash cow started borrowing. FY26 capex guidance: $175-185B → $180-190B → $195-205B. Quarterly capex $44.9B (YoY +100%); free cash flow −$5.86B, the first negative print in five quarters. To fund it, Alphabet raised nearly $70B this quarter ($49.6B equity + $20.3B debt), taking long-term debt from ~$46.5B a year ago to $98.2B. That's a structural balance-sheet shift — bigger news than any single quarter's revenue line.

✂️ The two businesses' margins are diverging. Google Cloud operating margin went 20.7% (year ago) → 32.9% → 35.6%, scale economics landing on schedule. But Google Services margin fell QoQ from 45.3% to 41.8% (−3.5pp) on a 50.6% QoQ jump in G&A. Total operating margin therefore slipped 36% → 34% QoQ. The growth engine is getting cheaper to run; the cash cow is getting more expensive. That matters more than a 2.8% revenue beat.

☁️ Cloud really is accelerating — it isn't backlog window-dressing. $24.77B for the quarter, +81.8% YoY (up from +63% last quarter), lifting Cloud from 14.1% to 20.7% of total revenue. Backlog is $514B (+11.3% QoQ — growth decelerating at the margin), yet revenue growth accelerated. Management's explanation: backlog is converting fast, with 50%+ expected to be recognized over the next 24 months. That claim is falsifiable next quarter.

★ One trap worth flagging: the trailing P/E of 17.89x, 3.2nd percentile over one year looks absurdly cheap — but only because the $99B one-time gain inflated trailing EPS. The denominator was artificially enlarged; this number is not a valuation signal. Use forward P/E: 26.34x, close to the 27.81x one-year mean. Neutral.

1. Take the EPS Apart First

The $99.0B of unrealized equity gains sits in Other income and (expense), net. Two main components: the ~14% Anthropic stake (a $65B raise took Anthropic's valuation from $380B to $965B; Alphabet's stake was marked to $124.3B per the SEC disclosure of 2026-07-23) and appreciation on SpaceX. These are unrealized fair-value moves — not cash, not operating results.

The symmetry is the part to remember: the same accounting treatment produces an equal and opposite one-time loss if those valuations retrace. Some future quarter's GAAP EPS may get hit by the identical mechanism — and it won't mean operations deteriorated either.

📌 Line the core EPS up as a time series and the real picture appears: $2.67 (Q4'25) → $2.76 (Q1'26) → $2.85 (Q2'26). That's a gentle climb of roughly 3% per quarter — nothing like the explosion the headline implies.

Two things worth pulling out:

3. Capex Raised Three Quarters Running; FCF Goes Negative

Capex guidance has been raised twice in three quarters: $175-185B (given at FY25 Q4) → $180-190B (FY26 Q1) → $195-205B (this quarter). For FY27, management reiterated a "significant increase" but declined to give a range.

Where does the money come from? Alphabet did something it has never done at this scale: $49.6B of equity (common stock plus mandatory convertible preferred) and $20.3B of debt — nearly $70B combined. Long-term debt went from roughly $46.5B a year ago to $98.2B. TTM free cash flow has now fallen three periods running: $73.3B → $64.4B → $53.3B.

📌 This is the real story of the quarter. For twenty years Alphabet's identity was "money printer, cash-rich, whose only problem was how to return it." This quarter it issued both stock and bonds to build data centers. That isn't inherently wrong — if Cloud revenue growth keeps pace with the depreciation and interest coming down the pipe, the trade is a good one. But that "if" is unverified, and the verification window is years, not a quarter.

4. Cloud Earns More; Services Earns Less

The Cloud line is good news: as scale arrives, profitability keeps improving — 20.7% → 32.9% → 35.6% across three quarters, with no sign of a ceiling.

The Services line is the one to watch: 45.3% → 41.8% QoQ, 3.5 points gone in a single quarter, driven by a 50.6% QoQ increase in G&A. Total operating margin fell 36% → 34% QoQ as a result (YoY is still +2pp, so an annual comparison hides it entirely).

The question is whether this is one-time or structural. If it reflects a permanent step-up in AI R&D and headcount costs, the cash cow's output gets compressed for the long haul — and that cash cow is exactly what's funding the capex bill. Whether this line stabilizes next quarter is the single most useful number to track.

5. What Changed in the Tone of the Call

"Supply constrained" got redefined as a good thing. Management's wording: "we are supply constrained, a sign of momentum and rapid adoption." That phrase used to be an apology; this quarter it's evidence of demand. They went further, saying that even lower-margin bridge deals (short-term leased capacity) count as "highly ROI positive" multi-year investments.

Sundar Pichai: "if anything, over the past year, we've gotten more bullish on the opportunities ahead," describing the company as still in "very early innings." That's a more aggressive embrace of the high-capex narrative than prior quarters — and the shift itself is information.

6. Valuation: Ignore the Trailing P/E

⚠️ Trailing P/E 17.89x, 3.2nd percentile over one year — this number is broken right now. The $99B one-time gain inflated trailing EPS, enlarging the denominator and shrinking the multiple, which makes it look like an all-time-cheap level. It's an accounting artifact. Any "Google is historically cheap" conclusion built on trailing P/E does not hold this quarter.

Forward P/E is 26.34x against a one-year mean of 27.81x — between the mean and −1σ, not statistically extreme; neutral to reasonable. That's also where the stock sits after rebounding roughly 12% off its post-earnings low. The extreme readings noted earlier this summer (forward P/E 23.5x, 0.8th percentile) are gone.

Analyst consensus: 10 analysts, 100% buy ratings, average target $435.43 (high $475 / low $400) against a recent $333-357 range — roughly 22–31% implied upside. But 100% buy is itself a crowding signal; unanimity without a bear case usually indicates downgrade risk rather than guaranteed upside.

On cyclicality: Alphabet's core advertising business is moderately cyclical (tied to macro ad budgets, but with entrenched share), while Cloud is in a growth phase, not a cyclical one. The genuinely cyclical variable here is the capex cycle, which is plainly in its expansion leg — and the signature of an expansion leg is depreciation that lands on the income statement two to three years later. Forward P/E rarely prices that fully.

7. Peer Group Scan

Group definition: mega-cap platforms that build their own AI infrastructure and own an application-layer monetization channel (hyperscalers plus AI application layer). The shared traits: they build their own data centers, buy or design their own accelerators, run capex-to-revenue ratios that are climbing fast, and monetize AI through advertising or cloud subscriptions. These names trade as one basket in the current tape — but their valuation percentiles are wildly different.

Two observations:

① GOOGL is the only name in the basket not sitting at an extreme low percentile. AMZN at 0.8%, META at 5.2%, NVDA at 6.4% are all near one-year lows; GOOGL's 26.34x forward is near its mean. You can read that as "already corrected, already priced," or as "relatively expensive" — it depends on whether you think the capex bill eventually pencils out.

② MSFT at 23.52x / 44th percentile is the most neutral name on the table. Neither the statistical lows of AMZN and META nor visibly expensive. In a group this polarized on valuation, sitting in the middle is the least informative position.

★ AMZN's percentile is an intraday snapshot from the query date (2026-08-03), when the stock rallied hard on its own earnings. It is distorted and should not be compared directly across the row.

9. Risks and the Other Side

1. Core earnings growth is modest. Ex one-time gains, core EPS went $2.67 → $2.76 → $2.85 — a climb, not a breakout. And the accounting symmetry cuts both ways: if the Anthropic or SpaceX marks retrace, a future quarter takes an equal one-time hit to GAAP EPS.

2. The capex cycle hasn't peaked and leverage is rising. Three consecutive guidance raises, FCF negative, ~$70B raised externally. If Cloud revenue growth can't keep pace with depreciation and interest, the Services margin compression already visible this quarter (−3.5pp) continues rather than reverses.

3. Google Network is in structural decline. Negative YoY again, with the long-term trend pointing down. It's not large ($7.3B a quarter), but the direction is unambiguous and it goes largely unmentioned.

4. Antitrust is a long tail. Both sides are on appeal and resolution before 2027 is unlikely. Not a current driver — but a structural remedy like a Chrome divestiture would hit the denominator of the valuation model, not the numerator.

The bull rebuttal: the one-time gain isn't operating income, but the Anthropic stake's value is real — Alphabet bought a $124.3B position for comparatively little. And capex looks ugly now, yet Cloud is at +82% YoY and accelerating with $514B of backlog behind it: evidence that the demand is real, not that they built something nobody wants. Both points hold. The disagreement is purely about time — how many years you're willing to wait for the depreciation to run through.
