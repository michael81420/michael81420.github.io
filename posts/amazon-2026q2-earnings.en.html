1. AWS: the acceleration is real, the ranking is still third

The AWS line is the strongest signal in this print, and not just on revenue:

① Revenue of $42.23B, +37% YoY, four quarters of acceleration and an 18-quarter high. ② Operating income of $16.6B at a 39.31% margin, against 33.04% a year earlier — 627bp of expansion. ③ Backlog (RPO) of $496B, up $132B in the quarter, which Jassy described on the call as "growing triple digits year-over-year."

Point ② carries the most information. A 627bp margin expansion is far larger than revenue-driven scale effects would produce — meaning that under full capacity utilisation, pricing power and unit-cost efficiency improved simultaneously. That is a quality signal, not a volume one.

But put it back in the peer ranking: AWS +37%, Azure 43%, Google Cloud +82%. "AWS is reaccelerating" is a fact; "AWS is leading" is not. Its edge right now is the largest base and the clearest backlog visibility, not growth.

📌 One Jassy line worth recording: "Even at that amount, we will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026, and I believe this dynamic will also be true in 2027, too." Even with capex raised to $220B, 2026 capacity falls short — and he expects the same in 2027. The supply gap is a multi-year condition management concedes openly, not a single-quarter phenomenon.

2. Unpacking net income: barely a ninth of it is operating upside

GAAP net income was $62.65B, +245% YoY — a striking number. But $53.4B of it was a pre-tax one-time gain on the Anthropic investment: non-recurring and non-operating.

The restoration: $53.4B pre-tax × (1 − 22.5% effective tax rate) ÷ roughly 10.9 billion shares ≈ $3.80/share; $5.75 − $3.80 ≈ $1.95, broadly consistent with the $1.97 Adjusted EPS analysts use. Against the $1.82 consensus, the real beat is +8.2%.

★ Definition matters here: the $1.97 Adjusted EPS is an analyst convention, not an Amazon disclosure. The company did not provide a non-GAAP EPS excluding this gain. The arithmetic above is a cross-check, not a precise accounting figure.

+8.2% is a solid beat and the core business genuinely did well — operating income of $27.46B (+43.2% YoY) exceeded the top of last quarter's guidance by $3.5B, and that part is entirely real. The only thing to watch is how much of the market's 15.3% single-day move was a reaction to the $62.6B headline. This is the mirror image of Alphabet this quarter — Alphabet had a one-time gain too, the market saw through it, and the stock fell 6.9% the next day.

3. Segments: AWS expands margin, retail absorbs tariffs

① AWS margin expansion is the only large improvement in the company (+627bp). North America expanded just 33bp and International actually slipped.

② Part of why North America's expansion is limited: it is absorbing tariffs itself. The quarter included roughly $600M of tariff-related refunds, and management said on the call that Amazon "largely absorbed these costs rather than pass them on to customers" — sacrificing margin to defend share rather than passing cost to consumers. A strategic choice: near-term margin for long-term share.

③ Advertising is the underrated third engine: $19.8B, +26% YoY (up from +24% last quarter, still accelerating). It is high-margin, already large, and synergistic with both AWS and retail via AI-driven sponsored-product conversion. In the same quarter that Meta's ad impressions decelerated, Amazon's ad business accelerated — a contrast worth noting.

4. Capex raised to $220B — because memory got more expensive

Full-year capex guidance was raised from $200B to $220B (against FY2025 actual of roughly $128B, +71% YoY). This is the second raise of the fiscal year.

⚠️ The reason deserves its own paragraph: management attributed it explicitly on the call to "higher cost of memory" rather than simply building more capacity. That means cost inflation in AI infrastructure is now flowing into Amazon's own capital spending. The same dollars buy less capacity than they did.

This matters more than "spending more." If capex rises because demand is stronger and more needs building, that is good news. If it rises because the same equipment costs more, that is a direct deterioration in capital efficiency. Oracle faced the identical question on its own call this quarter (Guggenheim asked about memory and SSD price increases eroding contract margins) — this is a shared cost pressure across the group, not an Amazon-specific issue.

The result shows in cash flow: quarterly FCF of −$8.82B, and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow swinging from +$18.2B a year ago to −$7.6B. Operating cash flow actually grew well ($45.39B, +40% YoY) — capex simply grew faster.

5. Guidance: two straight quarters of beating its own range

A pattern worth recording: Amazon has exceeded the top of its own guidance for two consecutive quarters (Q1 and Q2 operating income both above the ceiling by $2.4–3.5B). Conservative guidance is a long-standing management habit.

So the +9~12% YoY growth implied in Q3 guidance, well below this quarter's actual +19.6%, should not be read directly as a deceleration signal — beyond the tougher comparison (Prime Day already fell in Q2), conservative framing is the norm. If the pattern holds, the Q3 operating income range of $22.5–26.5B may be understated again.

6. Valuation: trailing PE is broken, use forward

⚠️ Trailing PE 21.85x at the 0.8 percentile of one year — this number cannot be used right now. The $53.4B one-time gain inflated TTM EPS and mechanically depressed trailing PE, making it look like an all-time bargain when it is only an accounting effect. Alphabet has exactly the same trap this quarter; both require the same ruler.

Forward PE 28.46x, below the one-year mean of 32.0x and below the 34.4x long-run mean since 2019 (37th percentile long-term) — on a forward basis, mid-to-low within its own history, not stretched.

The cross-section is different: AMZN 28.46x / MSFT 23.5x / GOOG 26.3x / META 17.9x / ORCL 19.5x / NVDA 19.9x — AMZN carries the highest forward PE of the three major clouds, reflecting a premium for AWS acceleration and backlog visibility.

Cycle intensity (invest-playbook): AWS is in a growth phase rather than a cyclical one; retail is moderately cyclical (consumer demand and tariffs). Depreciation will press on the income statement over the next two to three years, a cost forward PE typically fails to capture in full.

9. Both sides of the argument

Three reasons to be constructive:

① $496B of AWS backlog locks in roughly three years of revenue, with margin expanding 627bp to 39.3% at the same time — this is not revenue bought with margin. Up $132B in the quarter, with Jassy citing triple-digit YoY growth.

② Advertising is an accelerating, high-margin third engine. $19.8B at +26% (from +24%), synergistic with AWS and retail, still accelerating in the same quarter Meta's impressions decelerated.

③ Guidance has been beaten decisively twice running. If the conservative-setting, consistent-overdelivery pattern holds, the Q3 range of $22.5–26.5B may again be understated.

Three reasons to be cautious:

① The large beat rests mainly on a one-time gain. The core Adjusted EPS beat is only +8.2%, far less dramatic than the GAAP headline. Some portion of the market's 15.3% single-day move may rest on the unadjusted number. Alphabet had the same mechanism in the same quarter and fell 6.9% — the market's consistency is questionable.

② The capex raise is about cost, not demand. $220B, +71% YoY, attributed to memory pricing — a direct deterioration in capital efficiency rather than good news about capacity. TTM FCF is already negative at −$7.6B.

③ AWS is still the slowest of the three clouds. +37% versus Azure at 43% and GCP at +82%. If GCP and Azure keep widening the gap, the AWS competitive narrative dilutes — and the forward PE premium the market currently grants (highest of the three) rests on exactly that narrative.

★ The disagreement: how you treat that $53.4B. Bulls say the Anthropic stake's value is real and Amazon bought a large position for relatively little. Bears say it is unrealised, it will reverse, and it obscures deteriorating capex efficiency. Both are correct — the difference is whether your holding period is one quarter or three years.
