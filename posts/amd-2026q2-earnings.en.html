AMD．Q2 2026 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of $11.5B (+50% YoY) set a company record, and Data Center revenue of $6.72B (+107% YoY) has doubled in two years — both revenue and EPS beat estimates.
Yet the stock fell nearly 9% after hours: the real story is that non-GAAP gross margin is stuck at 56%, with almost no further expansion for two straight quarters. Built from the IR release and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: AMD IR press release + earnings call summaries Period: 2026-03-29 – 2026-06-27 Reported: 2026-08-04 after close Compiled: 2026-09-26

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

📈 Data Center revenue has doubled in two years — one of the strongest growth curves on the board right now. $6.72B (+107% YoY, +16.3% QoQ), now 58.3% of total revenue. Revenue of $11.5B beat the ~$11.25B consensus; non-GAAP EPS of $1.66 beat the ~$1.62 consensus.

🧱 But gross margin has stalled, and the GAAP/non-GAAP gap didn't narrow. Non-GAAP gross margin went 55% (Q1) → 56% (Q2) — essentially flat. GAAP operating margin was just 17%, versus a non-GAAP operating margin of 27% — a 10pp gap (stock compensation and acquisition amortization) that didn't shrink at all. That's the key to the paradox of "beat on everything, stock still fell."

⏳ Helios/MI400 demand is ahead of plan, but the ramp is deliberately slow. Management said customer demand already exceeds their original forecast, but shipments are "modest in Q3, a step-up in Q4, and a further ramp in early 2027." The market had priced in a steeper slope, and that gap in timing is the direct cause of the after-hours drop.

★ On valuation: forward PE has reached 71.6x, the 84.8 percentile of its one-year range — the stock has already priced in most of what a fully-ramped Helios outlook would deliver, which leaves little room for the next step to go wrong.

1. Data center is sprinting; margin isn't keeping pace

The Data Center growth curve is textbook: $3.20B (Q2'25, which included about $800M of one-off charges tied to MI308 export controls) → $5.78B (Q1'26) → $6.72B (Q2'26, +107% YoY). EPYC server CPUs and Instinct GPUs are both driving it — this quarter's least controversial highlight.

But layer margin on top and the story changes. Non-GAAP gross margin, adjusted for one-offs, went from roughly 54% a year ago to 56% this quarter — a two-point expansion that's nowhere near the slope of revenue growth. More telling is the GAAP/non-GAAP gap: this quarter's GAAP operating margin was just 17% against a non-GAAP operating margin of 27% — a 10pp gap driven mainly by stock compensation and Xilinx/ZT Systems acquisition amortization, and that gap didn't narrow this quarter. In other words, part of AMD's profit growth still hasn't fully reached the bottom line.

2. Guidance: revenue raised again, but margin guidance didn't improve

Q3 2026 guidance calls for revenue of about $13.0B (±$300M), +41% YoY and +13% QoQ — continuing the acceleration. But non-GAAP gross margin guidance is still ~56%, flat versus this quarter. If margin guidance had also been raised, the market would likely have read this print very differently.

On the call, management said Helios/MI400 customer demand already exceeds their original forecast, but shipments are being paced deliberately conservatively: modest volume in Q3, a clear step-up in Q4, and a further ramp in early 2027. That combination — demand ahead of plan, but shipments deliberately held back — is the direct reason the stock fell nearly 9% after hours; the market had bet on a steeper ramp.

3. Valuation: forward PE of 71.6x is already pricing in most of the two-year-out story

📊 Trailing PE 161.7x, forward PE 71.6x, 1-year percentile 84.8% (current price $630.63). Trailing PE is high because trailing four-quarter earnings had a low base; forward PE has come down a lot, but an 84.8 percentile means the valuation is already near the top of its one-year range, leaving relatively little room for error if the outlook disappoints.

Analyst consensus (Futu, updated 2026-09-25): 33 analysts, 84.8% buy / 15.2% hold / 0% sell, average price target $656.63 (high $1250 / low $540), implying only about +4% from the current price — not much room left to run, and the wide target range ($540–$1250) itself shows how much analysts disagree on how fast Helios will ramp.

Cycle-intensity read (invest-playbook): AI data-center GPUs are in a high-intensity expansion cycle, but AMD is currently the "second source" — its ramp pace follows the NVIDIA ecosystem and its own customers' capex timing, not something fully within its own control. That's a risk worth flagging precisely because forward PE is already at a high percentile.

4. Peer-group scan

Group definition: AI data-center compute silicon. AMD's direct rivals are GPU leader NVDA and long-time CPU rival INTC; on the custom-ASIC/networking side there's AVGO and MRVL; on the foundry side, everyone depends on TSM. These names all compete for slices of the same AI capex cycle.

Side by side, AMD (84.8%) and MRVL (91.2%) sit at the highest valuation percentiles on the table — both are "growing fastest, but the stock has already run ahead of fundamentals" names. AVGO (2.4%) and NVDA (6.0%), by contrast, are growing just as strongly yet sit near historical valuation lows — that big a valuation split within one group is worth watching for signs that capital is crowding into a narrow set of narratives.

② Does Helios/MI400 really step up in Q4?
Management: modest Q3, clear step-up in Q4

6. Bull and bear cases

The bull case:

① Data Center is the only real second option alongside the NVIDIA ecosystem. +107% YoY, with customer demand already ahead of management's original forecast and multiple cloud providers signaling adoption intent for Helios. ② Guidance keeps getting raised. Q3's $13.0B guide implies a faster pace than the sequence embedded in the Q2 guide, with YoY growth of +41%. ③ The client (CPU) base is stable, unlike pure-GPU players that depend entirely on a single product cycle.

The bear case:

① Valuation has already priced in the story two years out. Forward PE of 71.6x at the 84.8 percentile is nearly the most expensive name on the table — any slowdown in the ramp will hit the stock directly. ② Margin expansion isn't keeping up with revenue growth. Non-GAAP margin has been essentially flat at 56% for two straight quarters, and the 10pp GAAP/non-GAAP gap hasn't narrowed either — profit quality hasn't caught up with the growth narrative. ③ Helios's real ramp doesn't land until early 2027, leaving a full two quarters of execution risk in between. If shipments don't actually "step up" in Q4 as management promised, the market's disappointment will be swift — the stock already fell nearly 9% on this print.

★ The real disagreement is whether you believe in Helios's ramp curve, or believe the stock has already run ahead of that curve. Bulls and bears largely agree on the growth story itself — the difference is whether today's price should already be paying for the second half of that story.
