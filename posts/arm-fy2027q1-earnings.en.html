TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 The numbers themselves are strong: a clean beat on both revenue and EPS, and data-center royalties doubled. Revenue of $1.29B, +22% YoY (vs. a $1.26B estimate); non-GAAP EPS of $0.45, beating the $0.40 estimate by +12.5%. Within royalties of $715M (+22% YoY), data-center royalty revenue doubled in the quarter — the single biggest highlight. Demand for the AGI CPU, launched in March 2026, has already topped $2 billion.

📉 But the full-year royalty growth guide was cut — that's the real reason the stock fell 8%. Management lowered full-year royalty growth expectations from roughly 20% to the high teens, citing memory-price inflation now spreading into mid-tier and premium phones and weighing on smartphone shipments. AI data-center growth isn't yet fast enough to fully offset smartphone softness.

💰 GAAP and non-GAAP tell completely different stories. Non-GAAP operating margin was 41.2%, actually up from 39.1% a year ago; but GAAP operating margin fell from 10.8% to 7.1% — the entire gap comes from R&D expense (GAAP $838M, +29% YoY) and stock-based compensation (SBC $343M, +42% YoY). The core business is making more money; the GAAP bottom line is being buried under investment-phase expenses.

★ On valuation: forward PE sits around 229x, at the 82.4th percentile of its one-year range — meaning even stretched over the next 12 months of earnings, Arm is pricier now than it has been for most of the past year. 20 analysts, 85% buy, average target $313.41 vs. the 2026-09-25 close of $310.32 — implying very little upside built in.

1. The core numbers: GAAP margin eaten by expenses, non-GAAP actually expanding

Royalty revenue of $715M (+22% YoY), licensing and other of $574M (+23% YoY). GAAP net income $270M (+108% YoY), non-GAAP net income $480M (+28% YoY) — the gap is mostly SBC of $343M, which isn't excluded under GAAP. Cash plus short-term investments ended at $3,888M, +8.0% QoQ; there were no buybacks or dividends this quarter.

2. Guidance: Q2 revenue steps up, but the full-year royalty target was cut

Q2 FY27 revenue guide is $1.38B (±$50M), a sequential step up from Q1's $1.29B; non-GAAP EPS guide is $0.47 (±$0.04). At the full-year level, though, management cut royalty growth expectations from about 20% to the high teens, while keeping licensing growth guidance at roughly 30% — using acceleration on the licensing side to offset the royalty slowdown.

Management also reiterated a five-year target: total revenue of roughly $25B by FY2031 ($10B from IP/CSS, $15B from the AGI CPU business). That's a long-term narrative, distinct from the actual FY27 guide, and shouldn't be treated as something to be realized in the near term.

3. Valuation: forward 229x, 82nd percentile — still not cheap

📊 At the current price of $310.32 (2026-09-25 close), trailing PE is 317x, forward PE is 229x, sitting at the 82.4th percentile of its one-year range — even stretched over the next twelve months of earnings, Arm is pricier now than it has been most of the past year. This isn't "a cheap growth stock."
Note: 229x is Futu's figure and implies EPS of about $1.35, closer to a GAAP basis; using the FY27 non-GAAP consensus EPS of about $2.22, forward PE is roughly 140x (StockAnalysis shows 130x). Both are expensive, but the gap is material.

20 analysts cover the stock: 85% buy, 15% hold, 0% sell, average target price $313.41 (high $480 / low $230) — barely above the current price. That means analysts broadly buy the story, but no one is willing to push the target much higher from here. The "AI data-center royalties doubling" narrative is already fully priced in — whether that acceleration can continue next quarter matters more than this quarter's beat itself.

4. Peer group scan

Group definition: semiconductor IP licensing and chip-design tooling — Arm doesn't make chips, it licenses CPU architecture (IP) for royalties and license fees. The group extends to licensees who build ARM IP into their own silicon, and to the EDA duopoly that chip design depends on.

AVGO and CDNS sit at the lowest valuation percentiles on the table (2.4%, 10.8%), but for different reasons: AVGO is held down by its own huge AI custom-silicon revenue base, while CDNS reflects the market pricing in a growth peak for EDA. MRVL's 91.2% percentile is the highest here — the AI networking narrative is outrunning fundamentals, and that's a bigger valuation risk than Arm itself.

6. Bull vs. bear

Three bullish points:

① AI data-center royalties are genuinely accelerating. Data-center royalty revenue doubled in the quarter, AGI CPU demand already exceeds $2 billion, cumulative Neoverse core shipments have passed 1.5 billion, and NVIDIA, Google, AWS, and Microsoft are all expanding Arm-based deployments.

② Core profitability is actually improving. Non-GAAP operating margin of 41.2% beat last year's, and licensing growth guidance held at roughly 30%, offsetting the royalty slowdown with licensing volume.

③ The balance sheet is clean. Cash plus short-term investments of $3,888M, no leverage pressure, no need to dilute shareholders or take on debt to fund AI investment.

Three bearish points:

① Smartphone royalty weakness is structural, not noise. Memory-price inflation is spreading from premium into mid-tier phones, and management itself cut the full-year royalty target — while smartphones remain the base of the royalty business.

② There's no valuation margin of safety. Forward PE of 229x at the 82.4th percentile, with an average analyst target barely above the current price. Any disappointment can trigger an outsized reaction — exactly what happened when the stock fell 8% on a beat.

③ The GAAP/non-GAAP gap keeps widening (28.3pp → 34.1pp). SBC is up 42% YoY, and if the stock pulls back, the dilution from that SBC will feel more painful than it does today.

★ The real fault line: can AI data-center royalty growth accelerate fast enough, within 1–2 fiscal quarters, to fully offset the smartphone cut? Bulls are betting AGI CPU volume ramps quickly; bears think the current valuation has already priced that acceleration in, leaving very little room for error next quarter.
