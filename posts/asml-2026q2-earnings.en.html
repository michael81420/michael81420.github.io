TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 Beat its own guidance across the board — not a case of low expectations. Revenue of €9.33B (+21.3% YoY), EPS of €7.59 (+28.6% YoY), net margin 31.3%. The real surprise was gross margin of 54.0% — the Q2 gross margin guide given at the Q1 call was only 51–52%, so the actual beat that by 2–3pp, driven mainly by stronger-than-expected Installed Base Management (service/upgrade) revenue.

🔺 FY26 revenue guidance was raised for the second time this year, and by the widest margin yet. The trajectory: €34–39B at the start of the year (2026-01-28) → €36–40B at the Q1 call (04-15) → now raised outright to €43–45B (07-15), lifting the midpoint by roughly €6B from the last update. Management sounds notably more confident about visibility into AI-driven leading-edge capacity orders.

💧 Cash flow swings are extreme and seasonal — driven by customer prepayment timing, not capex. Q1'26 free cash flow was −€2.61B (customer prepayments concentrate in Q4, so a seasonal Q1 dip is normal), and it recovered to +€1.32B in Q2'26, up 313% YoY. ASML's own capex is actually quite steady at around €0.4B per quarter — the cash-flow swings come almost entirely from working capital, not new factories.

Guidance: FY26 raised a second time, and by the widest margin yet

The FY26 revenue guidance path runs €34–39B at the start of the year → €36–40B at the Q1 call → €43–45B now — each update shifted the whole range up, and this time the midpoint rose by about €6B, the biggest jump of the three. Gross margin guidance moved in step, from 51–53% to 54–56%.

One detail worth flagging: ASML's own Q2 gross margin guide, given at the Q1 call, was only 51–52%, but the actual came in at 54.0% — the company's own guidance has now been beaten by its own results for a second straight quarter, suggesting management itself underestimated the Installed Base Management (service/upgrade) contribution. On net bookings: the last two quarterly releases no longer disclose a specific figure, describing order intake only as "remained extremely strong"; the last hard number was €13.2B in Q4 2025 (of which €7.4B was EUV).

Valuation: forward PE at 31.4x, percentile already at the top of the group

📊 Forward PE is about 31.4x (1-year average ~48.0x), sitting at the 83.6th percentile of its one-year range — the richest valuation, and closest to its own historical high, in the peer group covered below.

Analyst consensus (as of 2026-09-25): 6 analysts, 100% buy / 0% hold / 0% sell, average price target of $2,391.8 (high $2,859 / low $2,100) versus the $1,743.94 price at query time, implying roughly +37.2% upside. The sample size is only 6 names, so read this with caution.

Cycle-severity read (invest-playbook): ASML holds a near-monopoly in EUV lithography, so its revenue is essentially a derivative of the leading-edge capex plans of TSMC, Samsung, Intel and others downstream — this cycle currently reads as structural expansion rather than a classic inventory cycle (see TSMC's own capex guidance, raised three times running this year). But that also means ASML's downside is heavily concentrated in the capex decisions of a handful of large customers. Whether 31x forward PE is justified comes down to how long you believe this cohort of customers keeps expanding capacity.

Scanning the Peer Group

Group definition: Front-end semiconductor equipment (capital equipment for wafer fabs). ASML owns lithography; the others each hold a chokepoint in a different process step — deposition/etch, process control, or metrology — together forming the supplier map for fab capex. (Downstream foundries like TSMC belong to a different group and aren't included here.)

One cross-sectional observation: ASML's 83.6th percentile is the highest in the table, with AMAT, LRCX and KLAC clustered in the 70–75th percentile range — the whole equipment group is being re-rated together on this AI capex cycle. The one exception is NVMI (Nova), at only the 30th percentile — small and fast-growing, but clearly lagging the re-rating seen in the four larger leaders.

Bull & Bear

Three reasons to be bullish:

① EUV lithography has almost no substitute. Every advanced-node capacity order has nowhere else to go but ASML — the least contestable node in this whole AI capex cycle.

② The beat this quarter came from strength, not a low bar. Gross margin of 54.0% beat the company's own 51–52% guide, driven by structural Installed Base Management growth, not a one-off item.

③ FY26 guidance has now been raised two quarters running, and this raise was the biggest. Management's confidence in order visibility is building quarter over quarter, not a one-time bounce.

Three reasons to be cautious:

① Customer concentration risk. Revenue depends heavily on the capex plans of a handful of names — TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Micron — and any one of them slowing its expansion plans feeds straight through to ASML's order book.

② Valuation is already near its own historical high. Forward PE of 31.4x at the 83.6th percentile is the richest in the group, leaving less room for error than peers.

③ Cash flow is sharply seasonal and easy to misread quarter by quarter. Q1'26 free cash flow printed −€2.61B; without understanding that as prepayment timing rather than a deteriorating business, a single quarter's number can be badly misread.

★ The real fork in the road: do you think EUV's monopoly pricing power is already fully priced into a 31.4x forward PE. Bulls are betting the downstream capex cycle hasn't peaked; bears think the valuation no longer leaves much room to be wrong.
