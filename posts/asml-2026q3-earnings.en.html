ASML．2026 Q3 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of €11.66B (+55.2% YoY), gross margin 57.5% and operating margin 44.4% — the core business beat the company's own guidance across the board. But headline EPS of €12.95 includes about €0.51B from revaluing the Mistral AI stake, and the €11.35B FCF is mostly customer prepayments.
FY26 guidance was raised only at the low end (€43–45B → €44–45B), the smallest of the three moves. Built from the IR release, call summaries and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: ASML IR / SEC 6-K release + 2026-10-14 earnings call summaries Period: 2026-06-29 – 2026-09-27 Reported: 2026-10-14 Compiled: 2026-10-15

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 The core business is genuinely strong; gross margin has beaten the company's own guide again. Gross margin was 57.5%, above the top of the 55–57% Q3 guide, and operating margin was 44.4% (Q2 37.1%, year-ago 32.8%). The driver was Installed Base Management (IBM, service and upgrades) at €3.29B, which management said on the call was about €0.4B above guidance.

🧹 The headline EPS needs unpacking. Basic EPS of €12.95 (+135.9% YoY) includes about €0.51B from revaluing the first Mistral AI tranche (a separate line below operating income in the IR release). Stripping it out leaves net income of about €4.45B and a core EPS of roughly €11.62 (my own arithmetic, no tax effect) — still +53% on Q2's €7.59.

💧 FCF of €11.35B is about 97% of revenue and should not be extrapolated. Working capital contributed +€7.01B, and long-term contract liabilities (customer prepayments) rose from €3.6B in Q2 to €9.3B (secondary source). That is a leading indicator for orders, not a profitability measure, and a give-back next quarter would not be surprising.

★ On guidance: FY26 of €44–45B moved only at the low end (+€1B). Q4 is guided to €14.2–15.2B at a ~57% gross margin. Management said for the first time that 30% + 30% capacity growth (2027, 2028) is still not enough for customer demand. The stock fell 1.86% on the day (secondary source); forward PE is about 26.6x.

Segments & Geography: EUV and Upgrades Carry the Margin

Two structural changes: ① EUV systems €4.1B vs non-EUV €4.3B (call, oral; EUV includes one High-NA), with Logic 54% / Memory 46%. ② China was 23% of machine sales (secondary source; 42% a year ago) — a sharp fall in share, though the company still guides China to about 20% of FY26 total sales. FY26 growth by line (call, oral): EUV >45%, non-EUV ~20%, IBM ~50%; memory system sales ~+75%, logic ~+10% (advanced logic >25%).

Guidance: A Big Q4 Step-Up, but FY26 Moved Only at the Low End

Arithmetic: FY26 of €44–45B minus the first three quarters (€29.76B = 8.77 + 9.33 + 11.66) implies Q4 of roughly €14.2–15.2B, exactly the Q4 guide — the FY guide is simply the Q4 guide added up, not new information. The new information is Q4 itself: €14.2–15.2B is another 22–30% above Q3.

Gross margin: Q4 is guided to about 57%, slightly below Q3's 57.5% (call, oral: capacity-ramp costs); FY26 is about 56% (54–56% at Q2). Net bookings: ASML stopped giving quarterly figures after 2025; the release says only that order book momentum "continues to increase quarter on quarter", so no figure was found this quarter (—). The last hard numbers were €13.2B for Q4 2025 and a €38.8B backlog at end-2025 (secondary source; backlog is now disclosed annually).

Wording changes (before / after)

① Orders upgraded: last quarter's release said "order momentum remained extremely strong" → this quarter "an order book momentum that continues to increase quarter on quarter this year". From "stays strong" to "still rising".

② Expansion still not enough — said outright: CEO Fouquet: "30% in 2027, 30% in 2028 will not allow us to meet our customer demand. We have to do more." The IR release has low-NA EUV and DUV immersion capacity +30% for 2027, with a further 30% for 2028 under investigation; the CEO now calls both 30%s insufficient, says demand outruns supply "at least until 2028", and is studying ways to beat 30% in 2028.

③ Customers asking about 2029–2030: "some strong interest to already place orders for 2029 and sometimes even 2030"; but the company is not yet accepting 2029–2030 orders, saying it wants to check total demand against capacity and allocate fairly (Investing.com summary).

④ Price increases (value-based pricing): CFO Dassen said customer discussions are finished, the effect is "not really visible in 2026", partly visible in 2027 and fully from 2028, and it is a one-time step, not an annual plan (Investing.com summary).

Where analysts pressed

(From the digest's question topics, not verbatim.) ① Capacity levers beyond +30% (upgrades, NXE:3800F from 230 to 260 wph, 2027 immersion DUV at 330 wph). ② Gross margin drivers (volume, EUV/immersion mix, IBM upgrades) and the size of the pricing benefit. ③ Visibility beyond 2027 and High-NA timing (Intel over 1 million wafers exposed, Samsung 2028, TSMC around 2030).

Dodges and vagueness

• 2027/2028 revenue or unit numbers: "far too early", none given. • Dollar impact of pricing: not quantified. • Post-2028 capacity measures: "investigating", no size. • Net bookings/backlog: no longer given quarterly. • The "about 65 systems" shipped in 2026 is ambiguous in one digest; another reads it as low-NA EUV, about 65 in 2026 vs 44 in 2025.

Numbers given only on the call ([call, oral])

EUV system sales €4.1B (one High-NA) / non-EUV €4.3B; Logic 54% / Memory 46%; IBM about €0.4B above guidance; Q4 IBM about €3.7B; FY26 growth: EUV >45%, non-EUV ~20%, IBM ~50%, memory ~+75%, logic ~+10%; China about 20% of FY26 sales; Mistral gain about €0.5B; Q4 gross margin slightly below Q3 on ramp costs, with ramp costs continuing into 2027 and possibly weighing on margin.

Prior-Quarter Check

One note on method: my criterion for ① was too narrow — I wrote that landing outside the range meant a one-off, but the miss was above the top of the range, which reflects conservative guidance rather than a one-off. ASML's Q2 and Q3 gross margins both exceeded the top of its own guide, so the top of guidance is not a ceiling. That is a single-company habit and stays out of lessons; the cross-company takeaway is the one in ③: when FCF explodes, check prepayments.

Valuation: Forward PE of 26.6x, but the Percentile Isn't Comparable With Last Quarter's

📊 Futu's forecast (forward) PE is 26.58x; current (TTM) PE is 42.29x at the 22.4th percentile of its one-year range (1-year mean 49.20x). Last quarter this site wrote forward 31.4x / 83.6th percentile. ⚠️ The two queries came from different fields and dates, so you cannot read it as "fell from the 83.6th to the 22.4th percentile"; part of it is TTM earnings jumping (including the Mistral gain), which mechanically lowers PE.

Analyst consensus (Futu, updated 2026-10-14): 6 analysts, 100% buy, average target $2,532.1 (high $2,859 / low $2,100; $2,391.8 last quarter) versus the 10/14 close of $1,717.76 (secondary source), implying about +47%. Only 6 analysts, so read conservatively.

Cycle-severity read (invest-playbook): ASML revenue is a derivative of downstream leading-edge capex. This cycle's hallmark is customer prepayments plus orders reaching into 2028–2030, better visibility than a normal inventory cycle; but FY26 memory system sales of ~+75% means a meaningful part of growth comes from memory-maker expansion, historically the most volatile capex. Verdict: mostly structural expansion, with a cyclical memory component — "monopoly" should not be read as "no cycle".

Bull & Bear

Three reasons to be bullish:

① Core-business quality: 44.4% operating margin, gross margin above the top of its own guide, and Q4 still guided to ~57%.

② Longer demand visibility: a surge in customer prepayments, orders being discussed into 2029–2030, management saying supply trails demand through 2028, plus a one-time price rise that lands in 2027–2028.

③ High Q4 guide: €14.2–15.2B is another 22–30% above Q3, with FY26 revenue about +36% (call, oral).

Three reasons to be bearish:

① Beautiful numbers, padded headlines: EPS of €12.95 includes about €0.51B (about €1.33 a share) of Mistral revaluation; the 97% FCF conversion is prepayments. Neither repeats every quarter.

② Slight revenue miss to consensus and the stock fell: revenue of €11.66B vs Visible Alpha €11.71B (−0.4%, secondary source); the stock fell 1.86%, then another 2.26% after hours (secondary source). Good numbers are already priced in, consistent with a crowded-trade pattern.

③ Growth leans on memory and ramp costs weigh on margin: memory systems ~+75% is a heavy share; Q4 gross margin is guided slightly below Q3, and management itself says ramp costs may continue into 2027. China's share has dropped to 23%, but the long-run risk of China's domestic DUV remains.

★ The disagreement: do you read "prepayment surge plus orders to 2030" as a longer growth runway, or as one-off cash-flow and profit noise this quarter? This site's view: the former is a structural signal, but Q3's EPS and FCF should not be treated as the new normal.
