Cutting 20% of headcount did not damage business momentum — that is the single most important thing here.

1. What This Quarter Was For: Verifying the −23% Q1

On May 7, 2026, Cloudflare delivered three bad things at once: non-GAAP gross margin of 72.8% against an expected 75.1%, DBNRR slipping from 120% to 118%, and the announcement of a 20% layoff (over 1,100 people). The stock lost 23.3% over two sessions.

The market's questions were specific: is AI workload structurally eating the gross margin, and can a company that just cut a fifth of its staff still sell? Neither can be answered by management commentary — only by the next quarter's numbers. That is what makes this a verification quarter rather than an incremental one.

📌 A reusable rule: when a stock craters on "a broken metric plus organizational upheaval," the thing to watch next quarter is not whether revenue beats (it usually does) — it's whether the broken metric itself turned. Revenue beats can be carried by recognition of existing bookings. Gross margin and NRR cannot.

The stock rose 17.4% after hours (from $284.43 to $333.95), nearly recovering the two-day post-Q1 collapse. Note that this is an after-hours print — the full regular-session reaction won't be known until tonight's close.

2. The Core Numbers

Three things worth pulling out:

① Revenue of $696.1M came in 4.7% above the company's own $665M high end. Cloudflare's typical beat has run around 3.1%, so this is a clear step up. And it beat its own guidance, not just the sell-side consensus — the latter can be distorted by a low bar, the former cannot.

② The GAAP / non-GAAP gap is unusually wide this quarter. GAAP operating margin of −29.6% and EPS of −$0.48 against non-GAAP of +13.8% and $0.29. The entire difference is the $150.7M restructuring charge. You cannot read the operating business off GAAP in a quarter like this — but you shouldn't ignore GAAP either, because restructuring is real cash going out the door.

③ FCF of $56.4M (8% margin) looks like a sharp QoQ step down from $84.1M (13%), but roughly $99M of the restructuring charge was a cash hit. Adding it back, underlying FCF was around $155M (~22% margin) — that's the quarter's real cash-generating capacity.

★ Cloudflare reports a single segment with no product-level or geographic revenue breakout. Everything said on the call about Workers, Zero Trust and AI is qualitative — none of it can be verified against a revenue line. That is the biggest disclosure gap versus most SaaS peers, and every product-line discussion on this page is constrained by it.

3. Revenue Growth Has Accelerated Five Quarters Running

Putting Q1 back on this chart surfaces something easy to forget: revenue growth actually accelerated in Q1 26 (33.6% → 34%). That −23.3% had nothing to do with the growth rate; it was gross margin, DBNRR and the layoff stacking up together.

This quarter's +36% is the fastest of the five, and QoQ growth jumped from +4.1% to +8.8%. But note that Q3 guidance implies only +31% YoY — taken literally, the five-quarter acceleration streak ends next quarter (see section 6).

4. Gross Margin: A Weak Stabilization Signal, and a Changed Frame

This is the part that most deserves a discount. The non-GAAP gross-margin path:

From 76.3% in Q2 25 down to 72.8% in Q1 26 — 350bps of cumulative decline. This quarter recovered 30bps to 73.1%. The direction is right, but it has clawed back less than a tenth of the drop.

More notable is the shift in management's framing:

Q1 call (May)
Attributed to paid vs. free traffic mix — a defensive explanation
No timeline for stabilization
Q2 call (this one)
"Trend showing signs of stabilizing," plus a pivot to total unit economics rather than gross margin alone
From explaining to de-emphasizing
📌 When management starts saying "don't look at just that metric, look at the broader picture," there are usually two possibilities: the metric genuinely lost its relevance, or it won't look good in the near term, so the observation frame gets swapped first. The available information cannot distinguish between them — only next quarter's gross margin can.

The falsifiable check: if FY26 Q3 non-GAAP gross margin fails to rise for a second consecutive quarter (i.e. comes in below 73.1%), then this quarter's "stabilization" was 30bps of noise, and AI-workload margin erosion has to be repriced as a structural problem.

5. Large Customers and DBNRR: The Most Solid Part of the Print

If you could only pick one metric to prove the 20% layoff didn't damage the business, it's this one:

Net adds of 282 customers above $100K in a single quarter, and 986 on a trailing-twelve-month basis — both company records. Q1's net add was only 118. The layoff spanned late Q1 into Q2, and the result was that large-customer acquisition more than doubled.

DBNRR of 120% (+2pp QoQ, +6pp YoY) fully recovers the 2pp Q1 slip. Alongside RPO of $2.732B (+38% YoY, slightly faster than revenue's 36%) and cRPO +35% YoY, bookings visibility has not deteriorated.

Zero Trust / SASE was described as the fastest-growing product line after Workers. Two deals were named on the call: an $11M European technology customer and a $7.7M US federal agency; one of them replaced 16 legacy vendors with Cloudflare, saving over $1.3M a year.

★ But disclosure quality is uneven. The >$500K / >$1M / >$5M tiers were only described qualitatively as "record net adds" on the call — no absolute figures or growth rates. Workers AI / inference has no standalone revenue disclosure either. These are all "story without numbers" and cannot be independently verified.

6. Guidance: Raised on Both Lines — but Restructuring Costs Were Raised Too

The FY26 revenue midpoint went up 2.1% and the non-GAAP EPS midpoint 5.0% — EPS was raised more than twice as much as revenue, which reflects operating leverage from the post-layoff cost structure, not just revenue flow-through.

In the same guidance, though, total FY26 restructuring cost was raised from $140–150M to as much as $165M. Q2 alone consumed $150.7M, already at the top of the original full-year range. The cost is running higher than what was expected when it was announced in May — the growth just covered it.

Q3 guidance: revenue of $736–737M (implying +31% YoY, +5.8% QoQ) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.34. Note that +31% is materially below this quarter's +36% — taken at face value, the five-quarter acceleration streak ends next quarter. Cloudflare has a record of guiding conservatively (it beat its own high end by 4.7% this quarter), so this may not be real deceleration — but it is the only deceleration signal currently on the table, and it came from the company itself.

Other figures given only verbally on the call: Q2 network capex at 7% of revenue, with FY26 capex guided to 14–15% of revenue (clearly rising); GAAP net loss of only about −$18M excluding restructuring, with management describing the path to GAAP profitability as "pacing ahead of the 2028 target"; and channel partners contributing 31% of revenue, which management believes could reach 50%+.

7. The New Narrative: From CDN to a Tollbooth on the Agentic Internet

Management spent meaningful time on a new positioning this quarter. It's worth noting separately because none of it shows up in the revenue numbers yet:

① Non-human traffic passed 50% of total internet traffic for the first time. Machine-to-machine traffic — bots, crawlers, AI agents — is now the majority. Cloudflare's angle: if all of it has to pass through this layer, charge for it at this layer.

② New launches: Monetization Gateway, Wallets, and cloudflare.pay, aimed at commercial payments between agents. This is an entirely new business line with no disclosed monetization scale.

③ Developers passed 7.4 million, adding 2 million in the quarter — more than the 1.5 million added in all of 2025. That's the Workers platform base, not revenue.

④ CEO Prince explicitly ruled out commercial GPU compute rental, calling it a commodity race, in favor of a "work-done" agentic-internet positioning.

📌 Be clear about what this section is: an update to the valuation story, not a source of this quarter's results. The +36% came from the existing CDN, security and Workers businesses, and has nothing to do with cloudflare.pay. And because Cloudflare reports a single segment, even if this line does take off, outsiders will never be able to size it from the financials — only from whatever management chooses to say on a call.

Attributing a 31.3x forward P/S to this new narrative is the least data-supported step available right now. It may turn out to be correct, but nothing in the filing can prove or disprove it.

8. Valuation: 98.8th Percentile — a Real Positive Is Not the Same as an Entry Point

Cyclicality read: Cloudflare is a structural-growth name (subscription SaaS infrastructure), not a commodity cyclical, so P/E and P/S still carry information — but only on a forward basis. With persistent GAAP losses, trailing P/E is −1137.7x, entirely meaningless, and is not used here.

Forward P/S of 31.3x sits at the 98.8th percentile over one year and the 99.6th over three. In plain terms: over the past three years, it has been more expensive than this less than 0.4% of the time. And that's computed off the after-hours price — if tonight's regular close holds, the percentile only goes higher.

The implied non-GAAP forward P/E is roughly 263x (~$330 after hours ÷ the $1.255 FY26 EPS midpoint). It looks absurd because Cloudflare's EPS base is tiny — what it really says is that the market is pricing this entirely on growth and narrative, not on earnings.

📌 The central tension this quarter: there's very little to pick at in the print itself — the beat was clean, all four cracks were patched, guidance was raised on both lines. The problem isn't the company, it's that the price already sits at the top of its own valuation history. "The good news is real" and "this is an entry point" are two separate judgments, and keeping them separate matters unusually much here.

Sell-side consensus (context only): 26 analysts, average target of $284.65, 80.8% buy ratings. That average already sits below the ~$330 after-hours price — meaning the old targets haven't digested this beat and should be revised up, but also that the stock has already run ahead of consensus in the short term.

9. Peer-Group Scan

Group definition: the intersection of edge computing / CDN / SASE (secure access service edge). Cloudflare is simultaneously a CDN leader and a Zero Trust / SASE platform, with an AI-infrastructure narrative layered on recently. The scan covers comparable names with either a CDN or a SASE business.

Three observations:

① NET isn't the only expensive one. PANW at 99.2%, NET at 98.8% and CRWD at 98.8% are all pinned to the top of their ranges. Capital is concentrating the "AI + security + infrastructure" narrative premium in the leaders — this is not a Cloudflare-specific phenomenon. Being bearish on NET's multiple means being bearish on the whole trade's pricing logic.

② The cheap ones are cheap for a reason. AKAM's forward P/E is only 38.9x but its growth is low single digits; FSLY's forward P/S is only 4.65x but the business is structurally struggling. Neither is mispriced — the market is correctly assigning low multiples to low growth. Worth noting that FSLY's 4.65x, low as it is in absolute terms, still sits at its own 82nd percentile: even it isn't cheap relative to itself.

③ ZS is the only moderately valued name in the group (P/S at the 37th percentile) — and it's Cloudflare's direct competitor in SASE / Zero Trust. Same market, two very different pricings, the difference being that NET gets classified as "AI infrastructure" while ZS gets classified as "pure security." Whether that classification is worth 60 percentile points is the most open question on this table.

11. Risks and the Bear Case

1. The valuation percentile is at an extreme. Forward P/S of 31.3x at the 98.8th percentile over one year and the 99.6th over three. That position tolerates almost no miss — and Q3 guidance already implies deceleration to +31%.

2. The gross-margin stabilization is only 30bps. Still more than 300bps below the historical 76% range, and management has already begun swapping the observation frame from gross margin to total unit economics. If the AI-workload cost structure is structural, the gross-margin premium SaaS is supposed to carry gets compressed for a long time.

3. Disclosure quality is low. A single segment with no product-level revenue; customer tiers, Workers AI and cloudflare.pay are all qualitative only. The new narrative can be neither verified nor falsified from outside — at the 98th valuation percentile that is an added risk, not a neutral fact.

4. Restructuring costs have overshot the company's own estimate. Originally $140–150M; $150.7M in a single quarter; full-year now up to $165M. The layoff is costing more than it appeared to in May.

5. Capex intensity is rising noticeably. FY26 guided to 14–15% of revenue (7% in Q2 alone). Management says it won't do GPU rental, but AI-related network buildout is still pushing capex up, which pressures FCF margin.

What the other side says: the bear case is "31x sales and an implied 263x P/E — any single miss compresses the multiple, and Q3 guidance is already decelerating." The bull rebuttal is "five straight quarters of acceleration, record large-customer net adds after cutting 20% of staff, DBNRR recovered, guidance raised on both lines — execution is being underrated, not the multiple overrated, and the agentic-internet tollbooth hasn't contributed a dollar of revenue yet." Neither is wrong. The disagreement is whether you're willing to pay at the 98.8th percentile for a narrative that has no numbers behind it yet. This page can't settle that — it can only point out that the flaw this quarter isn't in the earnings, it's in the price.
