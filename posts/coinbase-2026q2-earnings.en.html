TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

📉 The headline numbers are ugly — a third straight quarter missing estimates. Revenue of $1.22B, −18.5% YoY (vs. a $1.3B estimate); GAAP diluted EPS of −$1.36, badly missing the analyst estimate of a $0.17 loss. Trading volume and transaction revenue both fell, reflecting a broadly cooling crypto market.

💧 But the trigger for the loss was non-cash portfolio volatility, not operations deteriorating to that degree. This quarter's loss on crypto asset investments was $209.5M, versus a $362.1M gain a year ago — a swing of $571.6M on that single line alone, larger than the entire net loss. Core profitability, Adjusted EBITDA, stayed positive at $207.8M — a 14th straight positive quarter — but down 31% QoQ and 59% YoY. The core is weakening too, just nowhere near as dramatically as the GAAP headline.

🏆 Trading market share hit a record even as the broader pie shrank. Crypto trading volume market share reached 10.3%, a record for a third straight quarter; prediction market contract revenue crossed a $100M annualized run rate, +106% QoQ; Bitcoin-related revenue is now just 12% of the total (once over 50%). Market share is climbing even as the total market is shrinking.

★ On valuation: forward PE sits around 237x, at just the 2.0th percentile of its one-year range — but that low percentile stems from a tiny earnings-estimate denominator, not genuine cheapness, and should be read with caution.

1. The core numbers: GAAP loss amplified by the portfolio; the core is weakening too

By segment: transaction revenue $599.2M (−21.6% YoY), subscription & services revenue $555.1M (−12.2% YoY). Operating loss was $113.5M, versus an operating loss of $24.7M a year ago — the core business itself is running at a loss, just at a much smaller scale than the GAAP net loss. Quarterly operating cash flow was approximately $197.3M (backed out from H1's $380.1M less Q1's $182.7M; Coinbase doesn't separately disclose quarterly capex, so true free cash flow isn't calculated here).

2. Guidance: the Q3 subscription-revenue guide midpoint sits below this quarter's actual

Q3 2026 subscription & services revenue guidance is $500–580M, with the $540M midpoint below this quarter's actual $555.1M — signaling further cooling on that line next quarter. Adjusted Expenses guidance was raised to $980–1,080M, with full-year Adjusted Expenses narrowed to $4.2–4.45B. Management gave no specific guide for transaction revenue, since it tracks market trading volume and is inherently hard to forecast.

3. Valuation: the low percentile reflects a tiny earnings base, not genuine cheapness

📊 At the current price of $195.11 (2026-09-25 close), forward PE is roughly 237x, sitting at just the 2.0th percentile of its one-year range. This "low percentile" shouldn't be read as cheap — Coinbase's earnings swing wildly with crypto prices, and once the forward earnings estimate is pushed very low, the PE denominator shrinks and the multiple gets pushed abnormally high, making the percentile look low. What actually matters is the trend in Adjusted EBITDA and trading market share, not the PE figure itself.

25 analysts cover the stock: 72% buy, 24% hold, 4% sell, average target price $209.57 (high $330 / low $95) — about +7.4% above the current price. The width of that target range ($95–$330) alone signals how much more divergence exists over Coinbase's earnings path than for a typical company.

4. Peer group scan

Group definition: crypto asset and trading infrastructure — spanning regulated exchanges, retail trading platforms, stablecoin issuers, Bitcoin balance-sheet proxies, and Bitcoin miners. They're all exposed to the crypto price and volume cycle, but their business models differ so much that valuation shouldn't be compared using the same lens across the board.

MSTR and MARA's forward PEs barely reflect real operations — MSTR is a leveraged Bitcoin proxy whose earnings are dominated by fair-value swings on its Bitcoin holdings, and MARA remains loss-making, so its negative PE carries no valuation meaning. The two names genuinely comparable to COIN are HOOD (diversified retail trading) and CRCL (COIN's key USDC revenue-share partner), and both sit at far higher valuation percentiles, showing the market pays a bigger premium for "beyond the exchange" crypto narratives.

6. Bull vs. bear

Three bullish points:

① Trading market share hit a record against the grain. Even as the overall crypto market cooled, Coinbase's market share hit a record for a third straight quarter at 10.3%, signaling improving relative competitiveness rather than simply shrinking with the market.

② Core profitability stayed positive, and diversification is progressing. Adjusted EBITDA has been positive for 14 straight quarters; prediction market contract revenue crossed a $100M annualized run rate, +106% QoQ; and Bitcoin-related revenue fell to 12% of the total, reducing hostage exposure to a single asset.

③ The headline net loss was exaggerated by non-cash portfolio swings. This quarter's loss was mainly driven by a $209.5M fair-value loss on crypto assets (versus a gain a year ago) — required mark-to-market accounting, not an actual cash outflow.

Three bearish points:

① The core business (excluding the portfolio) is also loss-making. Operating loss of $113.5M, nearly 5x wider than the $24.7M operating loss a year ago — a problem that persists even after stripping out portfolio volatility.

② A third straight quarter missing estimates. Both revenue and EPS missed, Adjusted EBITDA fell 31% QoQ and 59% YoY, and the Q3 subscription revenue guidance midpoint sits below this quarter's actual — the trend is downward.

③ Valuation is highly dependent on the crypto price cycle. Forward PE of 237x rests on an extremely small earnings base; if the crypto market keeps cooling, earnings estimates could be cut further, shrinking the PE denominator even more and amplifying downside risk to the stock.

★ The real fault line: with record market share and falling profitability happening at the same time, which one is the "true signal"? Bulls see market share as a leading indicator that profitability will follow once the crypto cycle turns; bears see the widening operating loss as the true state of the core business, and worry that rising market share built on price competition is itself a red flag. Whether Adjusted EBITDA stops falling next quarter is the most direct test.
