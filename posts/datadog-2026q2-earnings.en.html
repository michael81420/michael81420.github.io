TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

📈 Growth didn't slow — it accelerated to 35.6%. Revenue of $1.12B was up 11.4% QoQ, the strongest sequential quarter since Datadog went public. Non-GAAP operating margin rose in lockstep: 20% (a year ago) → 22% (Q1) → 23% (this quarter). $100k+ ARR customers reached 4,720 (+22.6% YoY).

⚠️ But the stock still fell 15.6% — the problem is Q3 guidance. Management disclosed on the call that its largest customer's usage started declining in Q3 (the customer renewed, but usage dropped), already reflected in Q3 and full-year guidance. The Q3 revenue guide midpoint implies YoY growth of roughly +29%, a clear step down from this quarter's 35.6%. The market is pricing in that slowdown, not the quarter that was just reported.

🛡️ But management offered a key rebuttal: growth excluding the largest customer has accelerated for five straight quarters. That suggests Datadog's moat depends less and less on any single large customer and more on a broadening customer base — the crux this post's valuation section digs into.

★ On valuation, forward PE is 101.14x; the share price of $268.13 is now almost exactly at the average analyst target of $269.34 — good news and bad news are roughly offsetting each other, with no clear directional signal yet.

1. The Core Numbers: Three Tables to Read This Quarter

★ Management did not disclose the exact percentage decline in the largest customer's usage or the customer's name — only that it had renewed and that the impact is already reflected in guidance.

2. The Growth Curve: A Peak This Quarter, a Guided Step Down Next

Revenue growth of 35.6% YoY didn't decelerate the way many cloud software companies eventually do — it actually beat Q1's 32%, and the 11.4% QoQ jump was the strongest sequential quarter since the company went public.

But the next data point tells a different story: Q3 guidance of $1,135–1,145M against last year's $886M implies YoY growth slowing to roughly 29% — because the largest customer's usage started declining in Q3, a fact management chose to bake into guidance rather than something analysts are guessing at. This is the core reason the stock fell 15.6% on the day.

📌 Falsifiable test: management said "growth excluding the largest customer has accelerated for five straight quarters." If that's true, actual Q3 growth should beat the guide midpoint, the same way this quarter beat Q1's guide midpoint. If it's just talk, Q3 should land right at or below the guide midpoint.

3. Guidance: Raised for the Year, But With a Slowdown Signal Baked In

On the surface, FY26 revenue guidance was raised from $4.30–4.34B to $4.45–4.47B, and EPS guidance from $2.36–2.44 to $2.50–2.54 — both moving up, reflecting this quarter's beat of the prior guide.

But Q3 in isolation tells a different story: the guide midpoint implies roughly 29% YoY growth — the first time in six quarters the guidance itself implies a clear step down, and the reason is a named, single-customer event rather than a vague macro factor. That combination — "raise the full year, but guide down the next quarter" — is the technical reason for the sharp post-earnings stock reaction.

4. Valuation: The Stock Is Back to Even With Analyst Targets, Direction Undecided

📊 Forward PE of 101.14x (stockanalysis.com, 2026-09-26), market cap $96.28B, share price $268.13. The stock fell −15.6% the day after earnings but has since recovered to close to its pre-earnings level over the following month and a half.

On the analyst side, a 35-analyst panel (2026-09-24) shows a Buy consensus with an average target of $269.34 — almost exactly the current price of $268.13. A separate 46-analyst panel skews more bullish (41 Buy / 3 Hold / 2 Sell) with an average target of $285.28. Both point to the same conclusion: the market hasn't reached a clear directional verdict on the largest-customer risk and is waiting for Q3's actual numbers.

Cycle-intensity read (invest-playbook): cloud observability/APM is a moderate-to-low cyclicality industry tied to enterprise IT spending and cloud migration, but customer concentration is a Datadog-specific stock risk rather than a sector-wide issue (see the peer scan below — no other peer carries this single-customer concentration story). A forward PE of 101x isn't extreme on its own, but the margin of safety depends entirely on whether "growth excluding the largest customer has accelerated for five straight quarters" holds up in Q3's actual numbers.

7. Bull vs. Bear

Three reasons to be bullish:

① Underlying growth excluding the largest customer has accelerated for five straight quarters — evidence the real moat is breadth of the customer base, not dependence on any single large account.

② Revenue growth hit 36% this quarter, a high for this stretch, while margin rose in lockstep (20%→22%→23%) — this isn't growth bought by sacrificing profitability.

③ FY26 guidance has been raised for a second straight quarter, and $100k+ ARR customers are up 22.6% YoY — the broader customer base itself shows no cracks.

Three reasons to be bearish:

① The largest customer's usage decline is now a fact baked into guidance, not a hypothetical risk; the Q3 guide midpoint implies growth slowing to roughly 29%, a clear step down from this quarter.

② The 15.6% post-earnings drop is the market pricing in exactly that slowdown — even with the full-year raise, the single-customer concentration risk has moved from theory to an actual number.

③ A forward PE of 101x still isn't cheap; the stock has already run back to essentially match the average analyst target, so if Q3 growth doesn't validate the "accelerating ex-largest-customer" thesis, there's no margin of safety.

★ The real disagreement: do you believe "growth excluding the largest customer has accelerated for five straight quarters," or do you weight more heavily the fact that the slowdown is already baked into guidance. Bulls are betting on the breadth moat; bears are betting the concentration risk has already materialized. Q3 will settle it.
