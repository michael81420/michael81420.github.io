He openly says he can't find a clear reason, and the closest analogue he offers is the nonsensical March 2025 plunge (whose cause — Trump tariff news — only became clear afterwards).

✅ All three "good earnings, still fell" cases check out: TSMC ADR at $403.41, -15.8% from its 6/30 high (forward PE just 21x); Google at $319.09, -21.1% from its 5/18 high (forward PE 23.5x, valuation percentile just 0.8%); TXN at $279.58, -16.3% off the high. 7. Objective commentary (invest-playbook framework)

What holds up

The most valuable thing here is the discipline of separating industry commentary from price commentary — he repeatedly stresses that he's only judging whether the industry trend is improving, not whether a stock is a buy today. That split is rare in KOL content. His candor about "good earnings, still falls" is also a plus: no forced narrative, just an admission that he can't explain it. This selloff really did hit TSMC, Google, and TXN — three leaders in three different industries — with good numbers, so attributing it to positioning and deleveraging holds up better than hunting for a fundamentals story (and squares with EP680's Korean leveraged-ETF blowup analysis).

Micron's "gross margin approaching 80%" is not exaggerated this time — it's understated (actual 84.6%). A useful counterexample: Gooaye numbers flagged as "exaggerated" are sometimes just ahead of the earnings, and warrant a re-check a quarter or two later.

What to discount

SOX "almost 30%" is overstated: the actual deepest drawdown is -24.0% — right direction, roughly 6 points too large.

"Taiwan PCB/laminate stocks at 10x PE" has no evidence: checking the major CCL/PCB/substrate leaders — EMC 103x, Iteq 76x, Nan Ya PCB 235x, Unimicron 123x, Zhen Ding 68x, Compeq 36x — trailing PEs are all far above 10x. If a 10x name exists, it must be an unnamed, deeply obscure small-cap; listeners applying this to the well-known large-cap PCB names would badly misjudge.

"Equipment names down to 7–8x next-year EPS": he declines to name them because they're too small — honest in intent, but it makes the segment entirely unverifiable, and easy to over-extrapolate into "any equipment stock is cheap." The DRAM contract-price cadence and Nichicon's "September" price-hike timing are similarly unsourced.

Risks and the other side

If the "indiscriminate deleveraging" read is wrong and the market is actually front-running undisclosed bad news (as in the March 2025 analogue he himself cites), then "the industry is fine, prices will come back" fails. He acknowledges this is his biggest operational uncertainty, which is why he shifted to moving-average-based stops, lower trading frequency, and staying invested only through index beta rather than buying the dip — worth noting: he isn't buying either.

Also, the whole power-semi chain sits at 27–44x forward PE — not cheap. Even if the industry call is right, if sentiment keeps deteriorating these mid-to-high multiple growth names likely have more downside than defensive leaders (TSMC at 21x forward).

Credibility verdict

On macro framing and already-reported earnings numbers this episode is solid and verifiable (TXN gross margin, Micron gross margin, TSMC/Google earnings reactions all check out). On forward predictions and unnamed tickers it's narrative without a verifiable numeric skeleton, and shouldn't be used directly as a basis for trades.
