8. An objective read (invest-playbook framing)

What holds up

The strongest thing here is discipline in attribution. He doesn't force the selloff into a single story; he names three independent, simultaneous triggers — and all three are real events that survive checking, not reasons found after the fact. That's a step beyond EP682's "I can't explain it, and I won't pretend to": this time he found reasons, and the reasons hold.

The volume-based "giving back vs blown out" test is the most useful method in the episode, and it's falsifiable — it names an observable (volume) that readers can track themselves rather than asking them to trust a gut feel.

Then there's the honesty of the self-doubt. He doesn't stop at "core AI assets are cheap, buy the dip"; he volunteers the counter-case — if even the un-expensive Korean pair can crash, the valuation center may be shifting structurally down — and uses Amazon's dot-com drawdown to separate "cheap" from "cheaper." A commentator arguing against his own position isn't common.

Words and actions line up too: leverage under 1.3x, no shorts, the big position rotated into TSMC plus TAIEX futures, an explicit "I won't bottom-fish small caps this time." He isn't buying — consistent with the defensive tone, not talking bearish while adding risk.

What to discount

A hardware spec he got wrong: he says Nvidia's and AMD's CPU:GPU ratios are "both 1:4." AMD's Helios is indeed 1:4 (18 CPUs : 72 GPUs), but Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 is actually 1:2 (36 CPUs : 72 GPUs). His skepticism toward the "1:1" marketing from Lisa Su and Lip-Bu Tan points the right way, but the number he rebuts it with isn't accurate either — spec claims from commentators need independent checking too, however technical they sound.

A lot of personal-state and emotional content: roughly 70% is psychology, position sizing, and everyday topics. That may be worth something emotionally, but the information density is well below EP682, and anyone expecting a watchlist will come away empty. Not a flaw — just worth knowing going in.

His own drawdown figure can't be verified: he says he's given back roughly 25–28% this quarter. Personal book, no way to check, take it as color only.

Risks and the other side

"Volume spike marks the bottom" is a heuristic, not a law. If the selling is mostly passive deleveraging (the Korean kind) rather than margin calls, the market can absolutely grind out a bottom with no visible spike — holding the rule too tightly means missing the whole recovery.

The deeper risk is the one he raises himself: if a structural downward re-rating is the right read, then "AI core assets are in single-digit valuation percentiles" isn't a buy signal but the start of a new normal. Micron's 5.7x forward PE looks extraordinarily cheap, but memory is a violently cyclical business — cyclicals show their lowest PEs at peak earnings, so a low multiple may be marking a cycle top rather than proving value. The episode never engages with this, which is a real gap in the analysis.

Credibility verdict

On event attribution and already-observed market data this episode is solid: all three triggers check out, the TSMC/NVDA/Micron valuation figures all match, and the "NVIDIA is the cheapest AI stock" call has now held across several episodes. On hardware spec detail there's a clear error (Nvidia's 1:2 stated as 1:4). As for breadth of stock coverage — this was never a stock-picking episode, so don't read it as one.
