5. Taiwan's Disposition Stocks: Only Insiders Know Whether They Can Get Out

A substantial chunk of the back half covers Taiwan's disposition-stock and liquidity mechanics, and it condenses to one line: for large holders, the disposition regime is a liquidity trap, not a cooling mechanism. When a stock is put under disposition (switched to periodic call auction), trade frequency drops dramatically. Small holders barely notice; anyone holding size discovers they simply cannot sell — the volume they need to move would break the price outright.

💡 The usable framework: his point is that "getting in and getting out are two different problems." When your position is small, liquidity risk barely exists and fundamentals are all that matter; once it is large, liquidity risk has to be managed ahead of fundamental risk. That is the same story as Situational Awareness in miniature — 40% leverage is easy to put on, and something else entirely to unwind under a margin call.
⚠️ Calibrate the scope yourself: this section is fundamentally a large-holder's view (Taiwan's disposition thresholds are far above what a typical retail investor trades). For smaller accounts the value is conceptual — understanding why some stocks fall off a cliff for no fundamental reason — not directly actionable.
