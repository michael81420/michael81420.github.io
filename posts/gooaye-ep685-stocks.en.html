8. Listener Q&A: Three Ideas Worth Keeping

The contrarian-indicator friend: don't ignore them, measure them

For the friend who "knows the industry fundamentals cold but always trades the wrong way," the host's advice is to watch their behavior as a read on where we are in the cycle, rather than mocking or ignoring them. In practice this treats the people around you as a small-sample sentiment gauge — when the people who understand the fundamentals start capitulating, positioning is usually at an extreme. Be honest about the limits, though: small sample, no statistical significance, useful only as a supporting signal, never as an entry or exit trigger.

Blow-up psychology: "just hold on two more days" is usually wrong

A long-squeeze shakeout does not end until every leveraged holder has been forced out. That makes "hold on two more days" mechanically backwards — the longer you hold on, the deeper you get washed, because flushing out everyone still hanging on is the endpoint of the shakeout. This is the same idea as EP684's "getting in and getting out are two different things," stated from the other side: how long a leveraged position survives is not your decision to make.

Disposition stocks: the answer depends on your position size

The host thinks Taiwan's disposition-stock regime does more good than harm (liquidity matters more), while acknowledging that retail investors get hurt more easily by it (net harmful for them). He explicitly says the answer depends on how big your position is — which is a more complete and more honest treatment than the one-sided large-position framing in EP684.

9. Assessment (invest-playbook framework)

What holds up

"Don't wait for the second leg" has evidence behind it, not just feel. The index's move from 39,933 to 44,612 involved no retest of the low whatsoever; anyone waiting for a W-bottom confirmation missed the entire leg. This is more actionable than the usual market aphorism.

The TPU delay call is independently corroborated. The cited SCMP source cannot be traced, but the direction is supported by Morgan Stanley and Fubon Research. And his conclusion on it — short-term noise, does not change the long-term big picture — is proportionate to the strength of the evidence.

The honesty on disposition stocks deserves credit. Stating outright that the answer depends on position size and that retail is net worse off, rather than arguing only from his own vantage point, is more complete than the same topic in EP684.

What to discount

"Small impact" on the China optics ban is a subjective inference. "They moved production to Thailand and Malaysia, so it routes around" sounds reasonable, but the source reporting states the draft is not final and whether origin pierces corporate nationality is unknown. This is the passage to hold most loosely — repeating a subjective rationalization as a conclusion leads listeners to underestimate the risk.

The HBM downgrade has no independent source at all. The direction is plausible (more layers, lower yield, is common sense), but the specific case is untraceable private supply-chain chatter — the same recurring issue since EP674.

The implied "still buyable" in the Palantir segment needs unpacking. The print was genuinely strong, but an 86.4x forward PE sitting at the 6.0% percentile is a technical artifact of an exploding EPS base. "Good earnings" is not "still cheap", and those two things sit uncomfortably close together in the episode's narrative.

The "SpaceX" passage is almost certainly an ASR error and is not cited here. SpaceX is private with no after-hours session; CoreWeave does not fit either (it reports 8/11, after this episode). The original word cannot be recovered reliably.

Risks and the other side

The biggest risk is reading "don't wait for the second leg" as "chase anything." The correct reading is "waiting for textbook confirmation carries a high opportunity cost," not "buying now is definitely right." In fact the host himself chose defense (staying in the index) rather than offense — the assertiveness of the spoken framing does not match his own positioning choice, and listeners should keep those separate.

Second, the bounce has already repaired the cheapest entry points. TSMC's forward-PE percentile went from EP683's 21.6% (very cheap) to 47.6% (neutral); MediaTek's trailing PER went from 58.71x to 66.06x with no new fundamental catalyst. The "AI core assets at single-digit valuation percentiles" window from EP683–684 is closing, and the margin of safety today is not the one available two weeks ago.

Third, what is being bought in the optics group right now is the imagination of a rumor. The four Taiwan names are up 23–38% over five days yet still negative on the month, with valuations already at 141x and 83x. That combination — vertical rally, high trailing PE, orders unrealized — is the worst risk/reward in the episode.

Credibility verdict

The episode is reliable on market data that has already happened (the TSMC limit-up and the Palantir reaction both verify precisely) and has real evidence behind its market-positioning framework. But on supply-chain rumors, only one of three has independent corroboration; of the other two, one has no source at all and one attaches an optimistic conclusion to an unverified premise.

Read this episode for how to think about positioning after a bounce, not as a stock list — the one name with real numbers, Palantir, is already up 31.68%, and the optics group the rumor lifted is no longer cheap.
