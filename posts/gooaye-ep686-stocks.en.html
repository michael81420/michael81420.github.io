10 · Assessment (invest-playbook framing)

What holds up

Two offhand spoken numbers verify to the decimal. "Pulled back to the quarterly line" (0.1% off) and "small-caps fell nearly 30%" (actually −28.05%). That level of precision is uncommon in feel-based commentary, and the credibility of this episode's positioning read should be revised upward.

All four specific stock narratives survive cross-checking: Delta's full order book against a lagging stock (revenue +55.4% vs −12.2% for the month), Lite-On popping post-print (+18.2%), PTI announcing a buyback the next day (the 8/7 board resolution is real), and 3653 surging after being knocked down on bad news (a continuous run from 8/4). All four line up on both timing and numbers.

The memory group's narrative verifies as conservative. King Slide's monthly revenue +355.5% YoY and Phison's +377.6% far exceed the impression the episode conveys. One of the rare cases where checking the numbers reveals the show understated things.

What to discount

The TSMC segment is directionally wrong. "Sideways through May and June, then heading back to new highs" — but the April low of 1,810 to the June high of 2,510 was a +38.7% powerful advance, not consolidation, and at 2,370 the stock is still −5.6% below that high. No new high was made.

The King Slide "knocked down to 10,000" detail doesn't match. The verification window contains no corresponding "long consolidation then a sharp drop to NT$10,000" move; more likely it briefly touched the 9,400–10,100 range on 8/4–8/6 before rebounding quickly. The direction (bad news, then up) is right, but the drama was amplified.

Leg 3 (passives/power components) is pure speculation. The episode gives no price or revenue figure for a single passive-component name; the argument is a supply-chain observation about ODM/EMS-level repricing plus a pattern inference. The evidence is an order of magnitude weaker than for the first two legs and should not carry the same position size.

Both "HBM 12Hi to 8Hi" and "800V multiplies ASPs" lack a numerical floor. The first has no official documentation; the second has no order value or guidance. Both are plausible-but-unverifiable forward narratives.

Risks and the other side

The biggest risk is reading "lagging" directly as "catch-up opportunity." Delta's divergence is a fact, but "lagging → will eventually rise" is an assumption, not a conclusion. The counterargument is simple: money may just refuse to pay 52.51x when a peer trades at 28.89x. Between strong fundamentals and a rising stock sits the question of what the market is willing to pay — and this episode never discusses it.

Second, the sectors that already ran are no longer cheap: 3653 at 121.16x, King Slide at 61.25x, Acbel at 94.30x — all post-surge levels. And when the episode mentions these names, the tone is mostly retrospective admiration of how well they ran, which reads very easily as "you can still chase." The name whose valuation actually matches its growth is the one that got no emphasis: Phison, at 19.32x against +377.6% revenue growth.

Third, the three US names sit at wildly different valuation percentiles and cannot be lumped together. Cloudflare's forward PS of 32.88x sits in the 96.8th percentile of its own past year — the most stretched figure in this write-up. Palantir's forward PE of 91.4x looks terrifying in absolute terms, yet its percentile is only 11.6% (relatively low against its own history). NVIDIA's forward PE is 22.15x at the 22.4th percentile — the cheapest of the three. The host pairs PLTR and NET as "the first leg of software," but their valuation risk is not remotely the same grade.

Credibility verdict

On market data that has already happened this episode is highly credible (two index figures precise to the decimal, four stock narratives all verified), and the first two legs of the rotation have data behind them. But the third leg, the 800V ASP outlook, and the HBM spec rumor all lack any verifiable numerical floor.

Read this one as a map of where the money went after July's selloff. But the roads already marked on it (optics, thermal, memory) are no longer cheaply priced, and the unmarked road (passives/power components) doesn't even come with a ticker. The genuinely actionable item is the Delta divergence — at minimum it comes with clear fundamental numbers you can keep tracking.
