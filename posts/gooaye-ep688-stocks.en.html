That is the general lesson for IPO lockups: they are staged schedules, not single cliffs, so anchor on the largest tranche dates.

9. Assessment (invest-playbook framework)

What holds up

The VSH/STM segment is the cleanest fact-check in recent episodes. The claim (earnings growth >> revenue growth), the numbers (+1303% vs +16.6%, +339% vs +26.1%), and the valuation (both forward multiples in the low percentile of their own range) all line up at once. Better still, the claim carries its own test — a listener can verify it in the filings without trusting anyone's interpretation.

The 800VDC roadmap matches the official document, and it explains architecture without pitching stocks. The three stages, the beneficiaries, and the driver (rack power climbing from 100kW toward 330–570kW) are all consistent with the OCP whitepaper. Naming no stock across the whole segment is a plus, not a minus — laying out the architecture and letting listeners map their own supply chain is far more responsible than calling tickers.

The disaggregated-inference explanation is precise. The compute-bound Prefill vs bandwidth-bound Decode distinction is correct, and it correctly explains why Cerebras's wafer-scale silicon and large SRAM have an edge on Decode. The official 5x tokens/sec-per-watt claim checks out.

What to discount

Cerebras's financial condition is skipped, and that is the actual risk. "Public for a while" is really three months; Q2'26 gross margin fell from 31% to 14.2%, GAAP net loss was $450.5M, and forward PE is negative. Debating lockup mechanics on a name with unproven earnings quality leaves the main risk out of the frame.

Calling the SpaceX lockup "nine or ten tranches" oversimplifies. It is a conditional schedule with pressure concentrated on the 20%, the 28%, and 12/8. Treating it as evenly distributed misreads when the supply actually lands.

Several key technical figures are unsourced. "~5% penetration" and "6.1kA → 0.4kA" are stated without attribution and no primary source was found. Physically plausible, but they should not be cited as verified.

"AMD acquired a company that etches model logic into silicon" matches no findable deal, and cannot be confirmed here.

Risks and the other side

The first risk is compressing a three-stage roadmap into a single trade. The whitepaper is credible, but it describes an upgrade path spanning 2026 to 2030. What the market currently pays for SiC/GaN (NVTS, WOLF) and power modules (VICR 43.9x, MPWR 51.2x) reflects the back half of that story — and the back half carries the highest timeline risk: a one-year spec slip compresses multiples before any downturn arrives. Same conclusion that recurs through EP686 and EP687.

The second is extrapolating VSH/STM's earnings flex. The +1303% and +339% are a low base plus operating leverage, and the comparison base normalizes a year from now. The most dangerous moment for a deep cyclical is often the quarter or two when the earnings look best, because it means utilization is already back near the high end with limited room above. Low valuation percentiles are genuinely good, but watch whether that reflects "cheap" or "the market is already pricing the second derivative of growth".

Third, the disaggregated-inference news is negative for NVDA and the episode never follows that thread. If inference workloads split across vendors and hardware types, the winner-take-all premium should compress. The host notes that Nvidia's earlier CPX effort turned out to be "all thunder, no rain," but never connects it to valuation. NVDA's 22.2x forward PE is genuinely not expensive among large AI assets, though the spread of heterogeneous architectures is a long-term de-rating factor on that multiple.

Credibility verdict

This episode has the highest verification rate in recent memory on technical and financial facts: the 800VDC whitepaper, the AMD×Cerebras partnership, and the VSH/STM results all hold, and all trace back to primary sources. That is far more substantial than EP687's "I agree with your allocation" content.

The problems cluster in a single pattern: what is said is accurate, but what is left out is what matters — Cerebras's margin collapse, the real concentration of the SpaceX unlock, and the timing gaps between 800V stages. All three omissions push in the same direction: they make the risk look smaller than it is.

Overall: the technical content is reliable and directly usable; the financial conclusions need the missing half filled in yourself. What is worth keeping is that three-stage roadmap — not as a stock list, but as a ruler for judging when a given company's revenue actually shows up. VSH and STM are the one part you can take as-is: claim, numbers and valuation all agree, and the claim comes with its own verification method.
