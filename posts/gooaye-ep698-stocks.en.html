II. The CCL downgrade scare: rumor vs. hard data

Rumor: CPO moves high-speed signals next to the chip, so the board only needs M4. Host: not everywhere — C2C, CPU-to-GPU PCIe Gen6 and CXL storage still need high-grade material.

The host compares it to the earlier SemiAnalysis bearish call on CPO: related stocks got hammered, then mostly ripped back to new highs. Memory works the same way in his view — offloading capacity to external DRAM/storage may cut stack layers, but capacity is still needed, so shortage just moves from one item to another.

III. Market tone and price hikes

The index is near highs while many turn bearish; momentum traders chase strength and get washed out the next day. He says a choppy, no-continuity tape is normal and depends on your holding period. On pricing: a ~10% TSMC hike next year is plausible (memory has risen most; TSMC is “the last cut”), and GPU street prices are already rising. He recapped holdings only — Take-Two profitable, a Polish game stock a loser — with no new recommendations.

VI. Objective take

Holds up

The event and the price damage are real, and revenue is not loosening. The “short-term panic, mid-term rebound” analogy matches earlier CPO episodes.

Discount

The host is heavily exposed to the AI supply chain, so downplaying this risk favors his own book. Monthly revenue is a lagging indicator; if the rumor proves true it shows up a quarter or two later. Memory/Rubin claims are relayed with no primary source, and valuation (~70x) went undiscussed.

Risk

If CPO penetrates beyond scale-out into scale-up, board specs really erode — a long-term risk invisible in current data. Also: rumor whipsaw, high PE plus sub-60MA charts (EMC, TUC), and TSMC/AMD hikes still being rumors.

Credibility: medium-high as a view, not as a fact. Not investment advice.
