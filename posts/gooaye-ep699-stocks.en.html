2. Agentic AI pulls demand back from GPU to CPU

Most of his numbers hold up: CPU share of workload ~15%→50% (Bernstein, June 2026: 14%→50%) and CPU:GPU converging to 1:1 (AMD’s own blog, which also raised its server-CPU CAGR to 35%). The 30M→120M core count couldn’t be sourced, but the direction matches the server-CPU TAM surge.

He also shoots down the viral “40 CPUs per GPU” claim: no institutional backing; 1:2–1:4 is the sensible consensus. Some hyperscalers with long Intel relationships and deep inventory don’t feel a CPU shortage at all.

3. DSP price hikes and the CPO/LPO debate

The rumor: 15–20% for regular customers, less for the biggest. No report confirms the exact range, but tight supply and ASPs of ~$35–50 (800G) and ~$80–100+ (1.6T) are real. He names Marvell (~60–70% of high-end PAM4 oDSP; 90%+ together with Broadcom) and MaxLinear (only ~1–2% share — a different league). Qualcomm’s Hexagon is used to explain how DSPs evolved into NPUs — background, not a stock call.

7. Take

What holds up

The numbers score well this time. Muse at #1, Bernstein’s 14%→50%, AMD’s 1:1 and the record indexes all check out, and the host proactively rejects the exaggerated 40:1 claim.

What to discount

CPU is a second-wave narrative, and the host himself says second waves tend to be weaker; the DSP hike size has no public confirmation. Dismissing Acer’s chairman as a laggard ignores that his is a first-hand contrarian read — watch the next earnings before laughing it off.

Risks

Rich valuations: MRVL 77x forward, loss-making MXL 96x, INTC 108x; Taiwan optics at 45x–243x trailing. Visual Photonics (+44% in a month) looks overheated, while GCS (-12%) and Browave (-6%) are already rolling over. CPO/LPO is the long-run structural risk to DSP.

Credibility: high on the numbers, moderate on theme durability. Not investment advice.
