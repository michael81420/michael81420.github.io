2. Why M Charm might be different

Meta’s hardware motive is clear: stop being gated and taxed by Apple’s and Google’s phone platforms. M Charm packages an AI assistant as a carry-around, dress-up trinket wired straight into retail and payments. The host said flatly “I’d buy this,” but that is a consumer reaction, not fundamentals — there is no official price, and the 2-inch OLED, 5G and front/rear cameras come from supply-chain leaks.

The VR glasses weigh about 100g (≈400g with battery pack), lighter than Vision Pro. A stretch scenario: if AR glasses ever sell “hundreds of millions,” waveguides would eat so many 12-inch wafers that even TSMC lacks capacity — but the host himself calls glasses replacing phones near-term unrealistic. He also sees Amazon blocking outside AI agents as a reasonable defense of its ad and customer relationship.

3. The whipsaw market: chasing up days vs buying down days

“Dead Mouse” was up 200%+ in H1, then got whipsawed from July: bought Unimicron and it was raided by prosecutors the next day, MPI dropped for no clear reason, Wiwynn pulled back hard, and an optics name nearly hit limit-down the same day. The host said Delta had “already turned bearish with a weak pattern” — the laggard of that leg.

Fact-check notes: the Unimicron raid was on 2026-08-28 (Taoyuan prosecutors, country-of-origin labeling dispute), −≈6% that day — yet it made a range high of NT$1,185 in September. Wiwynn did a ~1:3 split on 9/1–9/2; post-split high 2,610 → 2,115 on 9/24 (−19%), so don’t compare to pre-split prices. Delta rebounded from a July low of 1,495 to 1,910 (+27.8%); “bearish” describes the pattern at the listener’s entry, not the whole period.

7. Assessment

What holds up

The verifiable facts are right. The Unimicron raid and the fates of Humane/Rabbit/Limitless all check out; separating short-term pain from “the index keeps rising” is a healthy mindset. Meta’s route-around-the-phone motive is industry consensus.

Discount this

M Charm is unpriced and unreleased, and AI wearables have a brutal failure rate — not an investable thesis yet. The 8%/30% trust figures have no traceable source. Delta “turning bearish” is easy to misread out of context.

Risks

META at the 98.4th valuation percentile, BE at 100th, AMD forward 71.6x — the listener’s portfolio is well themed but collectively expensive, and the episode never mentions valuation. Copying the list risks buying at the top.

Credibility: facts high, information density low. Not investment advice.
