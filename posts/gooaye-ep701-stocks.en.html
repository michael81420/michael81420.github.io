1. What this episode covers

The YouTube title is just “EP701 | 🐡.” The first part is a Q3 trading review: July was “the most violent crash in years” for Taiwan; the host’s drawdown hit ~30% and he recovered by trimming in late July and rotating in Aug–Sep. In the US, a big Broadcom bet “blew up and lay flat for a long time,” delaying new highs. The middle covers two new themes (small IC design spillover, TSMC Texas). Side threads: he reserved a Tesla Roadster (reveal 10/15, ~$50k deposit — a consumer purchase, not a stock call), and speculates that closed frontier models may eventually be deployed on-prem in customer data centers (chip designers can’t send IP to cloud APIs for security reasons). The Q&A is mostly chit-chat; the one usable bit: momentum traders “only trade the first half hour,” and late-day rallies are usually big players unwilling to spend.

On macro: treat the US–China meeting as “nothing happened” — H20-class chip approvals depend on Washington, and the optics ban is unchanged; high Treasury yields are a worry but not actionable.

2. Small IC design: the spillover is real, but only into AI/IP

The host’s logic has two legs: big players like MediaTek and Broadcom have their advanced-node and EDA resources tied up by AI chips, so “what they can’t take spills to Taiwanese designers”; and AI-assisted design shortens cycles (he cites OpenAI’s claim of 8–9 months to design and ~1 year to tape-out), giving small, lean firms more operating leverage. On the second point he says he’s “assuming the official claim is true.”

Checks: MediaTek’s August revenue was NT$64.18B (+44% YoY), so the rebound is real, but no public data backs the TPU-order spillover detail. Within the group, the names with 30%+ revenue growth and moving prices are GUC, eMemory, ASPEED, M31, Faraday and AP Memory — all ASIC/IP/server-management chips; consumer and display names (Elan, Parade, FocalTech, Novatek, Realtek) are not moving together. Alchip is the flip side: August revenue +274% while the stock sits −33.7% off its high.

3. TSMC Texas rumor: facility contractors first, materials lag

The host himself calls it “very early — treat it as a rumor,” but thinks Texas makes sense (power, water, chemical and oil infrastructure). Checks: Digitimes and Electronics Weekly (9/30), citing equipment supply-chain sources, report TSMC is evaluating a second Texas campus with 6 advanced fabs, Dallas most likely, not yet approved by the board; his “front-end first or back-end first” detail has no source.

He describes facility names as “famously cheap but they never move,” with higher pricing on US projects widening the upside; he also notes analysts once cut contractor margins that later proved too pessimistic. TSMC itself closed at NT$2,480, −2.2% off its high, with August revenue of NT$514.8B (+53% YoY).

7. Assessment

What holds up

The verifiable facts check out. Index highs, Broadcom’s drawdown, the TSMC Texas rumor (which he labels as such), and the Roadster delay and deposit details are all accurate. Treating rumors as rumors and focusing on themes over macro is a consistent framework.

Discount this

“Small IC designers moving together” is overstated — the group is sharply split, same pattern as EP675–677; the MediaTek TPU spillover detail has no public data. “Recovered by early September” refers to his levered, rotated personal book, not the index. On-prem closed models is speculation he flags himself (“playing prophet again”).

Risks

GUC 206x, ASPEED 125x, eMemory 111x (trailing) — strong revenue, but priced in; facility names are rumor-driven with no board approval; the host is heavy in optics and IC design, so the themes he champions overlap his positions. Until FOMO is confirmed, “two steps forward, one back” could still be a false breakout.

Credibility: high on facts and experience, subjective on the spillover and on-prem arguments; few stock picks — use it as a theme scan, not a single-stock recommendation. Not investment advice.
