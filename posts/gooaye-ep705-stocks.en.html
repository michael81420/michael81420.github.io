2. Crowded trades: good numbers, no move

The host did not name the equipment stock, so it can't be checked. It is a personal rule of thumb: when crowded, good news can't push it, so wait for a beat or a shakeout. Counterview: "good numbers, no rally" is more often already-priced-in or high valuation than crowding.

8. Objective take

What holds up

The host gave almost no checkable numbers, so nothing was caught as exaggerated. What could be checked (Oct 13 recap, memory's relative weakness) was true; "won't add if it doesn't rise" and "beware what's priced in" are risk control, not stock-picking calls.

What to discount

"Crowded trades" and "whose money the Broadcom CEO is making" are inferences with no statistics or filings behind them. The relayed DBS content and two revenue figures are unverified, and the "BADC" ten-times-PE name is unknown.

Risks

Memory's weakness may simply be the market expecting a cycle top, not just crowding; MU at 5.3x forward is no margin of safety in a deep cycle. A single holding near 20% (self-described SpaceX position, US book near two tenths; Futu confirms SPCX at $153.53, the size itself unverifiable) is high concentration.
