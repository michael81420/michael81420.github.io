1. What this episode covers

The investing part runs: market caution and funding costs → Meta post-earnings → bottleneck trading → software rotation → Taiwan themes → the Griffin interview and the Jevons counterpoint → stop-loss and profit-taking.

The transcript is speech-to-text with many homophone errors; garbled items such as "Sy", "Hware" and "test-interface stocks" are not cited.

2. Semis weak, software strong

He argues that "own the whole capex chain" worked for two years until July; now the market is doing homework. DRAM and MLCC are still short and margins still rising, but the market asks whether they can go higher. High prices already discount three years of growth, repair is slow, and rates are gravity.

Counterpoint: "if memory and substrates cannot hold, others will struggle" is his own test, not a fact; substrates actually diverge over 20 days. Software has run fast over 5 days (NOW +12%) and may just be a bounce.

3. Griffin’s airline-seat analogy

Ken Griffin, speaking to Bloomberg at a Citadel forum, likened GPUs to airline seats, cited 2000-era fiber and GPU depreciation, and said AI is a bubble without predicting a crash (Mitrade 10/21, single secondary source). The host counters with Jevons Paradox: comparable but not identical; the gauge is compute rental pricing, which he says is still rising, but no public index exists.

4. Peer scan: shortage-driven AI supply chain

Group: the "shortage-and-price-hike" AI supply chain: memory, ABF substrates, high-end CCL / PCB, versus software and AI applications. A deep commodity cycle plus AI scarcity.

Taiwan forward PE unavailable; trailing is not substituted. Leaders: Nanya, MU (memory), Unimicron (substrates); followers: Nan Ya PCB, Kinsus, Zhen Ding, ITEQ; laggards: SNDK, TUC, Gold Circuit, ASE.
