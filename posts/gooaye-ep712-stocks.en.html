2. Stabilization authorization and VIX are not a floor

✅ Confirmed: Taiwan’s Ministry of Finance announced November 6 that the stabilization fund was authorized to deploy money as conditions require. Authorization does not establish actual purchases that day or a guaranteed index floor. This is more precise than the host’s shorthand of intervention.

⚠️ Not established: his rebound expectations and high-VIX buying heuristic have not been backtested here. Cboe defines VIX around 30-day S&P 500 implied volatility; it supplies neither direction nor a Taiwan market bottom. No quarterly moving-average trading level is calculated in this article.

3. AI buildout: follow the permitting chain

Permitting delays can transmit along the buildout chain; timing remains project-specific.

The host links household costs to electoral pressure, permit restrictions and delayed equipment demand. He expects existing construction to be harder to stop and sees more risk to 2028–29 than 2027 orders. Those years are his scenario, not company-confirmed backlog guarantees.

⚠️ Timeline correction: the Texas governor’s office announced on September 21, 2026 a directive to halt permits sought by data centers pending ERCOT audit information. Restrictions therefore cannot be attributed solely to post-election Democrats. The notice also does not establish that every existing site must stop operating; environmental permits, grid access and construction need project-level checks.

Scenario A: a delay rather than lost demand

If existing permits, financing and power access remain intact, revenue may shift rather than disappear. Check delivery plans, alternative sites and replacement customers. Valuation can adjust before revenue does.

Scenario B: genuine demand contraction

Multiple customers canceling sites, equipment failing to find replacement buyers and cancellation clauses being invoked would be stronger evidence of contraction. This is an editorial verification framework, not an observed outcome.

The host’s views about oil prices driving votes or political donations changing restrictions are interpretations; no voter-causality or regulatory model is established here.

4. SoftBank turbine rumor: verify the transaction first

❓ Unconfirmed: the host attributes a turbine-order default allegation to Elon Musk. This run could not directly retrieve the original X post and found no cancellation confirmation on the reviewed SoftBank Group news page. Counterparty, volume, value, delivery schedule and the meaning of default remain unresolved. An attributed allegation is not a verified cancellation.

His framework is that low-penalty peripheral orders may be cut before expensive core commitments. Historical analogies to 2018 or 2022 do not prove that sequence will recur, nor that all TSMC orders are irrevocable. Replacement buyers could preserve aggregate demand; repeated cancellations across customers and equipment categories would be more informative.

5. Netflix and the source of an investing edge

The host says he has not closely followed Netflix for a long time and previously sold before its eventual rally. He still likes the service and does not want to cancel his household subscription. He gives no current revenue, EPS, target price or retention data. That anecdote cannot substitute for financial analysis.

Streaming combines structural adoption with content and advertising cycles. AI infrastructure combines structural demand with capex and financing cycles. Streaming needs retention, pricing and content-cost evidence; infrastructure needs permits, delivery and cash collection. A good narrative alone supplies neither with a justified multiple.

His beginner framework is to identify the source of an edge—information, analysis or trading mechanics—and pursue expected value rather than win rate alone. Fees, slippage and average gains and losses still matter. His brief PL-call reference, satellite/optical themes, passive/power components and relative software/SI strength do not provide a reconstructable trade sample or verified performance history. For people without an edge, he suggests learning through index exposure and familiar industries.
