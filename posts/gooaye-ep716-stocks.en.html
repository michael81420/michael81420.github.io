1. Liquidity: assets can fail to become cash

The host recalls April last year: diversified holdings and large caps still failed to provide cash during simultaneous limit-down trading, forcing other investment payments to be delayed. He reports holding cash and no extra leverage now, yet suffering a drawdown above 30% after buying too early. This personal account is not independently audited. Futures exposure must be measured against available cash; the phrase “unlevered” is insufficient.

He watches heavily traded, leveraged favorites for reopened limit-down trading and turnover. The unnamed stock and observation window prevent verification of the reported extra 20 lots. A high-volume rebound cannot prove forced selling has ended. His hope that pressure clears within two or three sessions next week remains a forecast.

Cash planning must cover living costs, committed payments and collateral calls. Selling shares later is not a dependable sole source of cash. A sound long-term thesis can still fail under funding constraints.

3. Trading restrictions and settlement are different

⚠️ TPEx’s reform took effect August 10, 2026, rather than the host’s July reference. Ordinary disposition securities move to roughly two-minute matching, with exceptions for altered trading methods, periodic trading and managed stocks. Pre-funding instead of slower matching is the host’s policy proposal, not an adopted rule.

⚠️ Having cash before buying is broker funding control; T+0, T+1 and T+2 describe settlement. The SEC states that most US stock transactions moved to T+1 on May 28, 2024. US stocks are not evidence of universal T+0. More frequent matching offers opportunities to trade but cannot create willing buyers.

4. Oracle, NVIDIA and BIS: disclosures versus inference

✅ Oracle’s November 20 statement says funding for its OpenAI contract is secured, including vendor financing; additional discussions are not a prerequisite. It still expects positive free cash flow in the second half of FY2028 and more than $30bn in FY2029. Those are forward-looking targets. A post-close clarification does not establish a new financing hole next week. Claims that SoftBank has the greatest leverage and stress were not verified on comparable balance sheets.

✅ NVIDIA announced Huang’s resignation and Kress’s interim appointment on November 12, considering both internal and external permanent candidates. Preferring an insider is an operating judgment. The November 17 results say Rubin has begun shipping; this does not secure 2028 orders. Blackwell and component cancellation rumors lack original order evidence.

✅ BIS announced third-country remote-access restrictions on November 16. Potential gains for compliant suppliers are a hypothesis: restrictions may also suppress or delay demand. They do not establish orders for Taiwanese vendors. A specific SEC investigation timetable and rate probabilities were not verified here; weaker dollars, lower oil or rate cuts are possible catalysts, not dated promises.

5. Shortages can coexist with an earnings and multiple reset

Memory is a deep commodity cycle driven by pricing. GPU and custom-silicon platforms mix structural growth with customer build cycles, export policy and product transitions. A shortage measures current supply and demand; shares price future profits and risk. Neither falling prices prove cancellations nor shortages guarantee a valuation recovery.

The host expects visibility through 2027 and a possible small dip in 2028. He describes Chinese cloud providers adjusting memory orders when compute chips are unavailable, with lower spot prices potentially reviving consumer demand. These are interviews and inferences without named quotes, contracts or quantitative company confirmation. Unnamed Japanese suppliers are not assigned tickers; price elasticity also depends on end-customer budgets.

The TSMC 2022–2023 analogy illustrates risk, not a guaranteed repeat recovery. Claims of seven or eight times earnings or cheap NVIDIA lack a reproducible forward EPS denominator. The diagram assumes EPS falls from 10 to 8 and P/E from 20x to 15x: 200 becomes 120. It is not a company forecast.

6. Three scenarios to verify

Liquidity improves; operations hold

Track restored trading, spreads and market breadth alongside orders, deliveries, revenue and cash flow. A single sharp rebound does not establish a bottom.

A rebound precedes further earnings cuts

If lead times, prices, orders and margins weaken together, rebuild EPS rather than treating a low multiple as automatically cheap.

Compliant orders and new platforms fill the gap

Verify licenses, named customers, shipments and cash collection. A sound direction can still include a long transition gap.

In Q&A, the host favors diversified index ownership and warns that collateral adjustments can trigger calls in a single account. His pre-dividend collateral haircut story does not establish a universal one-week rule or a universal 20% adjustment. An ambiguously named optical company’s ATM financing discussion is not assigned a ticker. Long holding periods do not guarantee recovery.
