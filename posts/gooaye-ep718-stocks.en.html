3. CDS and SoftBank: warnings versus triggers

⚠️ CDS contracts provide credit protection. Wider spreads can reflect changed credit-risk pricing, but inferred default probabilities depend on assumptions including recovery values. Spreads are not observed default probabilities or dates. The host also rejects immediately labeling a borrower the next Lehman. No comparable named-company CDS or bond-yield series was obtained, so their weekly moves were not verified.

Potential escalation signals include sound projects failing to raise funds, major customers delaying or cancelling, redemption restrictions, withdrawn bank lines and forced sales. These are scenarios to monitor, not a list of confirmed incidents. A falling VIX does not replace credit and cash-flow analysis.

✅／⚠️ SoftBank defines LTV as adjusted net interest-bearing debt divided by equity value of holdings. Its policy is below 25% normally, with a 35% extraordinary ceiling and cash for at least two years of bond redemptions. Falling asset values can raise LTV; the policy does not itself establish a loan margin-call trigger or current forced sales. The host also considers US Treasuries if conditions deteriorate. Government bonds and corporate credit carry different risks; duration and currency exposure remain relevant.

Buyback checklist: from authorization to shareholder benefit

Separate authorization from actual shares and cash spent, timing and average price. Then examine funding, debt, equity issuance and net shares outstanding. Teaching example: authorization for one million shares but purchases of 200,000 imply 20% execution by share count. Issuing 300,000 shares in the same period still raises net shares by 100,000. High execution does not establish value or guarantee a price floor.

  • AI: check guidance, procurement commitments and delivery; separate delays, cancellations and duplicate orders.
