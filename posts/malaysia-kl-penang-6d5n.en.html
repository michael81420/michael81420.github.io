[Day 1 · Tue 10/06] Arrival and a first look at Bukit Bintang

Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days of departure (Oct 3–5) — it is free and mandatory since 2024. Taiwanese passport holders are eligible for Autogate, but a first entry requires a manual counter for biometric enrolment. There is no Uber in Malaysia; use Grab for everything.

Arrival at KLIA and hotel check-in (13:25–16:00)

  • Immigration: land at KLIA T1 at 13:25, clear immigration, collect bags, buy a SIM (CelcomDigi / Maxis / U Mobile).
  • Transfer: 50–60 minutes from the airport by Grab, or KLIA Ekspres plus a connection. Leave at 14:30, arrive around 15:30.
  • Opus Residences by Wakely: 82 Jalan Talalla, a 5-minute walk from Maharajalela monorail and about 800 m from Pasar Seni LRT. It is not inside Bukit Bintang: the malls are two monorail stops or a 10-minute Grab away. Petaling Street and Central Market are a 10–15 minute walk.

Bukit Bintang shopping district (16:00–19:00)

  • Getting there: monorail from Maharajalela to Bukit Bintang, two stops (about 6 minutes), or a 10-minute Grab. Once you are there, air-conditioned skybridges connect the malls, so rain is not a problem.
  • What it is: KL's busiest shopping and nightlife district. All six stops below are within a 10-minute walk.
  • Pavilion KL: the district's flagship mall, the widest range of luxury brands and dining, with the crystal fountain out front as a landmark.
  • Lot 10: the green-clad veteran mall; Lot 10 Hutong food court is in the basement.
  • Low Yat Plaza: Malaysia's largest electronics mall — phone accessories, cameras and PC parts in one building.
  • Sungei Wang Plaza: the older local mall next to Low Yat, a maze of small independent shops. More everyday life than Pavilion.
  • Fahrenheit 88: across from Pavilion, concentrated Japanese brands and a Uniqlo flagship.
  • Bukit Bintang crossing: buskers and street-dance crews gather here at dusk — the fastest way to feel the city's street energy.
Where to eat
  • Lot 10 Hutong: a basement food court gathering about 20 heritage hawker stalls from around the Klang Valley (Mo Sun Kau bak kut teh, Kim Lian Kee hokkien mee and more).
  • Pavilion KL food court: satay, nasi kuning and laksa all in one place.

Jalan Alor night food street (19:00–21:00)

  • Getting there: 10–15 minutes on foot from the malls.
  • What it is: KL's most famous open-air food street. From 17:00 the tables come out along the whole road — 20-plus restaurants and hundreds of stalls, open until the small hours.
Where to eat
  • Wong Ah Wah: the street's landmark. Charcoal-grilled chicken wings and satay, 16:00–02:00.
  • Sun Fong Bak Kut Teh: a 50-year-old institution founded in 1971. Not actually on Jalan Alor — it is at 37 Medan Imbi, a 10–12 minute walk; open daily 07:00–23:00.
  • Char koay teow and oyster omelette stalls: cooked to order over a roaring flame.
  • Ikan bakar (grilled stingray) and chicken skewers: the most atmospheric stretch, with the charcoal going full blast after 19:00.
  • Fresh sugarcane juice and coconut water.

Supper (after 21:00)

The two streets behind Jalan Alor have a whole row of places open into the early morning, all walkable. Grab back to the hotel is about 10 minutes (last monorail around midnight).

5–10 minutes on foot
  • Ngau Kee Beef Noodles | Tengkat Tong Shin | 18:00–04:00: a 40-year-old shop with an unmistakable neon sign. Beef ball noodles and dry brisket noodles are the ones to get.
  • Restoran TG's Bistro | Tengkat Tong Shin | 24 hours: the closest round-the-clock mamak. Nasi kandar, roti canai, maggi goreng.
  • Suzi's Corner | Tengkat Tong Shin | 18:00–03:00: late-night Malay fried noodles and nasi goreng. Closed Tuesdays (not on Day 1).
  • Changkat Bukit Bintang | until 02:00–03:00: a row of bars and live-music venues in converted shophouses.
10–15 minutes by Grab
  • Restoran Nasi Kandar Pelita | Jalan Ampang | 24 hours: Malaysia's best-known nasi kandar chain — rice under three or four curries at once.
  • Nasi Lemak Wanjo | Kampung Baru | 06:00–00:30: the sambal is the whole point.
  • Pudu Wai Sek Kai (Glutton Street) | Pudu | approx. 11:00–22:00: an old supper street with the most authentic Cantonese and Peranakan flavours.
  • ABC Bistro Cafe | Masjid India | 24 hours: a round-the-clock mamak in the Indian quarter.
  • Yan Yang A One | Jalan Klang Lama | 24 hours: dim sum in the middle of the night — har gow, siu mai, cheung fun.
  • Kedai Kopi Mee Bon | Taman Million | 06:30–02:00: an old kopitiam still serving dim sum at 2am.

[Day 2 · Wed 10/07] Batu Caves at sunrise, heritage at noon, Twin Towers at dusk

The Taman Connaught night market runs on Wednesdays only — this is the trip's one chance at it. Around town, the LRT, MRT, Monorail and KTM all take a Touch 'n Go card.

Batu Caves (08:30–10:30)

  • Getting there: leave at 07:45. Grab takes 30–40 minutes; or the KTM Komuter Batu Caves line from KL Sentral to the terminus, about 30 minutes plus a 5-minute walk.
  • Hours: daily 07:00–21:00, free entry. 08:00–09:00 is the best window: 26–28°C instead of the 33–36°C at midday, a quiet staircase, and the most dramatic light falling through the Temple Cave's natural opening. Tour groups only start arriving after 09:30.
  • The site: a limestone cave complex dedicated to the Hindu deity Murugan, with a 42.7-metre gold statue at the entrance.
  • The 272 rainbow steps: repainted in 2018 and now the site's signature image. A relaxed climb takes 15–20 minutes. Morning light is front-on, so the gold statue and the coloured stairs photograph well together.
  • Temple Cave: the roof is open to the sky and a shaft of light falls straight into the chamber in the morning.
  • Ramayana Cave: a painted cavern to the left of the main stairs, about RM5, worth adding if time allows.
  • Practicalities: knees must be covered inside (sarongs can be hired on site); wear proper shoes. The macaques will snatch food and phones — carry nothing in your hands, do not feed them, and keep bags zipped.
Where to eat
  • Indian vegetarian restaurants and mamak stalls at the foot of the stairs: open from early morning — thosai or roti canai with teh tarik.
  • Fresh sugarcane juice and coconut water.

Lunch and the ride to KLCC (10:30–12:45)

  • Getting there: 10:30–11:30, Grab straight from Batu Caves to KLCC, about 35–45 minutes.
Where to eat
  • Signatures food court, Suria KLCC: lower ground floor, about 10:00–22:00. Nasi lemak, roti canai, laksa and satay in one place.
  • Madam Kwan's KLCC: level 4 of Suria KLCC, about 11:00–22:00. Reliable nasi lemak and rojak.
  • Little Penang Kafe: level 4 of Suria KLCC, about 11:00–21:30. Penang-style laksa and char kway teow.
  • Restoran Nasi Kandar Pelita: Jalan Ampang, open 24 hours, 10 minutes on foot from KLCC.

KLCC malls and indoor sights (12:45–16:00)

  • Suria KLCC: the mall under the towers, daily 10:00–22:00. Isetan on level 4; the lower-ground supermarket is where the white coffee and durian sweets for souvenirs live.
  • Aquaria KLCC: lower ground of the Convention Centre, 5 minutes from Suria via the skybridge, daily 10:00–20:00 (last entry 19:00). A 90-metre underwater tunnel; allow 1–1.5 hours. Cheaper booked online.
  • Petrosains Discovery Centre: level 4 of Suria KLCC, Tue–Sun 09:30–17:30 (closed Mondays). Hands-on science exhibits, about 2 hours.

PETRONAS Twin Towers & Skybridge (16:00–17:30)

  • Getting there: the ticket desk is inside Suria KLCC. Check in by 15:45; timed entry, and a missed slot is not rebooked.
  • The towers: designed by César Pelli, briefly the tallest building in the world on completion in 1998, with Islamic geometry worked into the facade.
  • Skybridge and the level 86 observation deck: the walkway on levels 41–42 plus the deck above, 45–60 minutes in total. Tue–Sun 09:00–21:00 (last entry 20:30), closed Mondays. Book online in advance and pick a slot around 16:00.
  • Going up at 16:00 puts you back on the ground right at sunset (around 19:10 in October).

KLCC Park, Lake Symphony & the towers after dark (17:30–19:30)

  • Getting there: straight out of the towers — the park is directly behind Suria KLCC.
  • KLCC Park: the best place to photograph the towers in full, especially from the far side of the pool.
  • Lake Symphony fountain: shows every 30 minutes from early evening, with light and music. Best after dark.
  • When to shoot: sunset is around 19:10, and the blue hour from 18:45 to 19:30 — sky still blue, building already lit — is the best 20 minutes the towers get.

Taman Connaught night market & dinner (19:30–22:00)

  • Getting there: Grab from KLCC at 19:30, about 25–30 minutes to Cheras. The MRT Kajang line also stops at Taman Connaught, with a link bridge through Cheras Sentral, but from KLCC that means a change and roughly 40 minutes.
  • Wednesdays only — Oct 7 is the trip's single opportunity. KL's pasar malam are roving markets that set up in a different neighbourhood each night; Taman Connaught is the biggest of them.
  • Scale: roughly 2 kilometres and about 700 stalls along Jalan Cerdas, the longest night market in KL and the second longest in Malaysia. Cooked food, fresh produce, clothing and household goods; walking it one way takes about an hour.
  • Hours: roughly 17:00–23:00. Stalls are all in place after 18:30 and the crowd peaks then, so 20:00 is about right.
  • How to do it: walk about a kilometre one way and turn back, or walk to the far end and get a Grab from there — end to end and back is four kilometres.
  • Practicalities: stalls take cash and Touch 'n Go eWallet almost exclusively, so bring small ringgit notes. It gets crowded; wear bags in front.
What to eat
  • Ramly burger: the griddled Malay burger wrapped in a fried egg — the soul of any pasar malam.
  • Apam balik: a thick peanut pancake, cut fresh off the griddle.
  • Grilled wings and satay: a whole row of charcoal stalls, noticeably cheaper than Jalan Alor.
  • Char koay teow, hokkien mee, oyster omelette: the stretch where the Chinese stalls cluster.
  • Air tebu (sugarcane juice), coconut water, cendol
  • Kuih and tropical fruit: durian, mangosteen and rambutan cut to order.

Grab back to the hotel at 22:00–22:30; no supper tonight.

Fallback: if you skip the market, have dinner at KLCC (19:30–21:00)

  • Madam Kwan's KLCC: level 4 of Suria KLCC, reliable nasi lemak and rojak, roughly 11:00–22:00.
  • Suria KLCC food court: in the basement — nasi lemak, roti canai, laksa and satay in one sitting, roughly 10:00–22:00.
  • Sun Fong Bak Kut Teh: 37 Medan Imbi, daily 07:00–23:00, about 10 minutes by Grab from KLCC.

[Day 3 · Thu 10/08] Putrajaya's pink mosque and the Chinese old town

Petaling Street is in the evening — the stalls are all up by then, and dinner happens there too.

Putra Mosque (Masjid Putra) & Dataran Putra (09:30–11:15)

  • Getting there: 08:15 Grab from the hotel to KL Sentral (about 10 min) → KLIA Transit to Putrajaya & Cyberjaya station, about 20 minutes, roughly RM14 one way, departures about every 30 minutes → Grab 10–15 minutes from the station to the mosque. About an hour door to door.
  • Alternative: Grab the whole way from the hotel, about 40 minutes and RM60–80. Split three ways it is competitive, and it removes the transfer.
  • Visiting hours: non-Muslims Sat–Thu 09:00–12:30, 14:00–16:00 and 17:30–18:00 (Friday afternoons only). This visit is a Thursday, so arriving at 09:30 clears the 12:30 prayer closure comfortably.
  • Putra Mosque: completed in 1999, its rose-tinted granite dome is the signature, sitting on the edge of Putrajaya Lake with room for 15,000 worshippers. Free entry — scan the QR code to register at the visitor entrance, borrow one of the free hooded red robes, remove your shoes, and keep arms and legs covered.
  • Dataran Putra & Perdana Putra: right beside the mosque. The green-domed Prime Minister's office framed against the pink mosque is the classic Putrajaya shot, and costs nothing from outside.
  • Heads up: Putrajaya is wide open with almost no shade. Bring water and a hat.

Back into town and lunch (11:15–13:30)

  • Getting there: Grab back to Putrajaya Sentral → KLIA Transit to KL Sentral → LRT to Pasar Seni, arriving around 12:30 in the Chinatown area.
Lunch (12:30–13:45)
  • Nam Heong Chicken Rice: Hainanese chicken rice since 1938, Michelin-recommended, at 56 Jalan Sultan. Daily 10:00–16:00. Expect a queue.
  • Kim Lian Kee: open since 1927, dark-soy hokkien mee and claypot loh shu fan, at 92 Jalan Hang Lekir. 11:00–23:00, closed Wednesdays (this visit is a Thursday, so it is open).
  • Ho Kow Hainam Kopitiam: a Hainanese coffee shop open since 1956 — kaya toast, half-boiled eggs and kopi, at 1 Jalan Balai Polis. 07:30–15:30, closed Mondays (this visit is a Thursday, so it is open).
  • Hon Kee Porridge: a long-standing Cantonese congee shop. Opens at dawn and closes around 14:30, and often sells out before that — go at breakfast instead.
  • Guilinggao (herbal jelly): sold by several herbalists along the street, available any time in the afternoon.

Kwai Chai Hong & Lorong Panggung shophouse lane (13:45–15:30)

  • Getting there: 2–5 minutes on foot from wherever you ate.
  • Kwai Chai Hong: the Cantonese name for Lorong Panggung, roughly "Little Demon Alley". In 2019 ten pre-war shophouses — six fronting Petaling Street, four on Lorong Panggung — were restored along with the lane between them. The walls carry murals of 1960s Chinese life in KL, hung with red lanterns. Daily 09:00–24:00, free.
  • Lorong Panggung: the pre-war shophouses on both sides are now cafés, small bars, galleries and independent shops. Give it 30–40 minutes.
  • Merchant's Lane: a much-loved café on the upper floor of a Petaling Street shophouse, with the original brickwork and light well intact — the emblem of this quarter's revival.
  • Suggested loop: Kwai Chai Hong for the murals → out through Lorong Panggung → back along the Jalan Sultan shophouses → on to REXKL.
Where to eat
  • Egg tarts and nyonya kuih from the cafés in the lane.

REXKL (15:30–17:00)

  • Getting there: 5–8 minutes along Jalan Sultan from Kwai Chai Hong.
  • REXKL: the 1970s REX cinema, derelict after two fires, reopened in 2019 as a bookshop, exhibition hall and food space with the raw brick and steel left exposed.
  • BookXcess at REXKL: the old auditorium rebuilt as stacked walls of books and stepped seating, with a circular "book maze" at the centre. Mostly English titles and very cheap.
  • Ground floor: food stalls and an exhibition space that often hosts independent markets, art shows and live music.
  • Hours: Mon–Thu 08:30–22:00, Fri–Sun 08:30–24:00. This visit is a Thursday, so it closes at 22:00.

Petaling Street night market & dinner (17:00–19:00) tonight's dinner

  • Getting there: 5 minutes on foot from REXKL.
  • What changes: Petaling Street only becomes itself after dark — awnings go up and the street under the red gateway turns into a dense night market, with the old buildings, antique shops and herbalists trading alongside clothes, souvenirs and hawker stalls. Haggling is normal; start at half the asking price.
  • Central Market (Pasar Seni): 5 minutes away, daily 10:00–22:00. An art-deco market with batik, pewter and handicrafts under one air-conditioned roof.
Dinner, without leaving the street

Most of the famous names here are breakfast-and-lunch places that shut by mid-afternoon. Only the ones marked "open tonight" below are available at this hour.

  • Open tonight　Kim Lian Kee: 92 Jalan Hang Lekir, 11:00–23:00, closed Wednesdays (Thursday this trip). Open since 1927 — dark-soy hokkien mee and claypot loh shu fan. The pick for a proper sit-down dinner tonight.
  • Open tonight　Street stalls: char koay teow, claypot chicken rice, siew mai, grilled pork jerky — all out from 17:00.
  • Open tonight　Air Mata Kucing: the century-old herbal longan drink stall at the head of the street, open until about 22:00.
  • Open tonight　Guilinggao / cendol and ais kacang: the herbalists and dessert stalls mostly run to 21:00–22:00.
  • Closed tonight　Nam Heong Chicken Rice: 56 Jalan Sultan, daily 10:00–16:00. Hainanese chicken rice since 1938, Michelin-recommended.
  • Closed tonight　Ho Kow Hainam Kopitiam: 1 Jalan Balai Polis, 07:30–15:30, closed Mondays. Kaya toast and half-boiled eggs.
  • Closed tonight　Hon Kee Porridge: opens at dawn, closes around 14:30 and often sells out before that. Breakfast or not at all.

KL Tower (Menara KL) at night (19:15–21:00)

  • Getting there: Grab from Chinatown at 19:00, about 15 minutes; or the Monorail to Bukit Nanas and up the hill (steep — take the shuttle).
  • KL Tower: a 421-metre communications tower opened in 1996. Because it sits more centrally and higher than the Twin Towers, this is the only viewpoint that fits the whole of the Twin Towers into a single frame — particularly good at night. The deck is open daily 09:00–22:00, last tickets 21:30.
  • Sky Deck and Sky Box: an open-air deck with glass boxes you can stand in to photograph the city under your feet. Expect a queue. It needs the pricier combo ticket and closes in thunderstorms — the indoor Observation Deck is enough for the night view.
Famous food near the tower (if Chinatown was not enough, or you want one more round)

KL Tower sits on a hill inside the Bukit Nanas forest reserve, and there is nothing famous in the grounds — you have to come back down. The forest trail to Bukit Bintang takes 20 minutes and is unlit at night — take a Grab.

  • Restoran Nasi Kandar Pelita｜Jalan Ampang｜24 hours｜Grab 5–8 min: the country's best-known nasi kandar chain — rice under three or four curries.
  • Lot 10 Hutong｜Bukit Bintang｜about 10:00–22:00｜Grab 8 min: the basement of Lot 10, where long-established hawkers (Hon Kee, claypot chicken rice, pork noodles) share one air-conditioned food hall.
  • Nasi Lemak Wanjo｜Kampung Baru｜06:00–00:30｜Grab 10 min: the sambal is the whole point.
  • Jalan Alor｜Bukit Bintang｜17:00 till late｜Grab 8 min: Wong Ah Wah runs to 02:00.
  • Ngau Kee Beef Noodles｜Tengkat Tong Shin｜18:00–04:00｜Grab 8 min: 40 years old, and the beef ball noodles and dry brisket noodles are why.

Back at the hotel by 21:30 to pack — tomorrow starts at 06:45.

[Day 4 · Fri 10/09] Crossing to Penang by train and ferry

Today runs on the KTM ETS. It terminates at Butterworth on the mainland — it does not reach Penang island, so a sea crossing still follows. Butterworth station, the bus terminal and the ferry jetty are all inside the same Penang Sentral interchange, a few minutes' walk apart.

ETS train: KL Sentral → Butterworth (08:05–12:10)

  • Suggested service: ETS Platinum 9172, departing 08:05 and arriving Butterworth at 12:10 (4h05m). Times change — confirm on the KTMB site before booking.
  • Booking: KTMB's official site or the KITS app. Friday northbound is a peak departure; book well ahead rather than at the counter.
  • Which class: Platinum and Express make the fewest stops and are fastest; Gold stops everywhere and adds 30–45 minutes.
  • Check-in: KTMB requires arrival 30 minutes before departure and does not refund no-shows — be at KL Sentral by 07:35.
  • On board: the air conditioning is fierce — bring a light jacket. Racks are inside the carriage; large cases go by the doors.
  • Fallback: if 08:05 is sold out, take an earlier 07:00–07:30 service. Do not take anything after 09:00 — it eats into Fort Cornwallis in the afternoon.

Ferry: Butterworth → George Town (12:10–13:00)

  • The transfer: Butterworth station is Penang Sentral — train, bus and ferry terminals in one building, connected by covered walkways that are easy with luggage. Allow about 20 minutes.
  • Frequency: every 30 minutes, 06:30–23:30 (last Butterworth sailing 22:30). The crossing is 3.2 km of the Penang Strait, about 15–20 minutes. The fare is around RM2.
  • Important: the ferry is now card-only — contactless credit or debit, no cash. Check your card works before boarding, and carry two from different issuers.
  • The new vessels carry foot passengers, motorbikes and bicycles only — cars use the Penang Bridge. Luggage is no problem.
  • Fallback: if the ferry is suspended or the queue is long, take a Grab from Butterworth station over the Penang Bridge — 30–45 minutes and RM30–45, still in time for a 13:30 check-in.

George Town hotel (218 Jalan Macalister) check-in & lunch (13:30–15:00)

  • Getting there: Pangkalan Raja Tun Uda ferry terminal to 218 Jalan Macalister, about 10–12 minutes by Grab.
  • Where it sits: west of the old town, outside the UNESCO core zone. It is a 15-minute walk to KOMTAR and Penang Times Square, but the murals, Kimberley Street and Fort Cornwallis are 2–3 km away — use Grab (5–10 min, RM8–12) or the free CAT shuttle rather than walking it in 33°C heat.
  • What it buys you: Macalister Road is a working local food belt in its own right (Swatow Lane and Penang Road are walkable), and it sits on the airport side of town — for michael's 15:10 flight on Oct 10, leaving from Macalister saves 5–10 minutes over leaving from the waterfront.
Lunch (walkable from the hotel)
  • Swatow Lane: 12–15 minutes on foot, where Macalister meets Penang Road — old-school ice kacang and char koay teow.
  • Kopitiams along Macalister Road: hokkien prawn mee or chicken rice, several within 5 minutes.

Fort Cornwallis (15:00–17:30)

  • Getting there: about 8–10 minutes by Grab from the hotel (roughly 2.5 km — too hot to walk).
  • The fort: built after Captain Francis Light landed in 1786, and the largest surviving British colonial star fort in Malaysia.
  • Cannon and displays: the Dutch-made "Seri Rambai" cannon, plus the old armoury and magazine.
  • The waterfront: looking out over the Penang Strait to the mainland. The light is best in the late afternoon.
  • City Hall and the Esplanade: the lawn and white colonial buildings just outside the fort, where locals walk at dusk.

The Esplanade & Penang Road food street (17:30–21:00)

  • Getting there: the Esplanade is next to the fort; Penang Road is another 10 minutes on foot or a short Grab.
  • The Esplanade (Padang Kota Lama): a seafront square and promenade with food stalls and a sea breeze — a good place for sunset.
  • Penang Road: one of the densest concentrations of long-running hawker stalls in the city — cendol, char koay teow and prawn mee down one street.
Where to eat
  • The cendol stall shuts at 19:00, so do dessert before dinner here.
  • Penang Road Famous Teochew Chendul (go here first): the century-old cendol stall on Lebuh Keng Kwee with a permanent queue, next door to its own asam laksa stall. Mon–Fri 10:30–19:00, weekends 10:00–19:30.
  • Hokkien prawn mee: an orange-red broth simmered from prawn heads and pork bones, nothing like the Taiwanese dish of the same name.
  • Oh chien (oyster omelette): the Penang version is crisper, egg and sweet potato starch fried until caramelised.
  • Lebuh Chulia night stalls: 5 minutes away, with stalls and small bars along a street of century-old shophouses — a good second stop.

[Day 5 · Sat 10/10] George Town old streets, murals and the museum

The George Town core is compact and its sights are 5–15 minutes apart on foot; but the hotel is on Macalister Road, about 2 km out, so getting in and out of the old town each day means a Grab (5–10 min) or the free CAT shuttle. michael leaves at midday: check out during breakfast at 08:00 and leave the bags at reception, collect them at 11:15, and take a Grab out of George Town at 11:30 — that is the latest safe departure, with the airport 30–40 minutes away. Everything from 12:00 is the friend's itinerary.

George Town old streets (09:00–12:00)

  • Getting around: Grab from the hotel into the old town at 09:00 (about 8–10 minutes).
  • The heritage core: several hundred shophouses from the 18th and 19th centuries, with traditional trade workshops, antique dealers, cafés and studios tucked along the narrow streets.
  • Chew Jetty: a Chinese clan settlement built out over the water, boardwalks lined with century-old houses and provision shops, beside the ferry terminal.
  • Khoo Kongsi: the most ornate Chinese clan house in Penang, with exceptional carving and painted detail.
  • Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion (the Blue Mansion): the indigo-walled residence of a late-Qing merchant, with fixed daily tours (around 11:00, 14:00 and 15:30 — confirm on site). The 11:00 tour would make michael miss the 11:30 departure, so skip it.
  • michael's condensed route (09:00–11:30): Grab to Chew Jetty (09:10–10:00) → Khoo Kongsi (10:10–10:40) → the "Kids on Bicycle" mural on Armenian Street (10:50–11:10) → 11:10 Grab back to the hotel for the bags (about 10 minutes) → leave for the airport at 11:30.
Where to eat
  • A Penang kopitiam: kopi O with charcoal-toasted kaya toast and soft-boiled eggs — open from 07:00.

Street-art walk (12:00–14:00) — friend's itinerary

  • Getting around: all on foot; the main murals cluster along Armenian Street and Lebuh Ah Quee.
  • Kids on Bicycle: by Lithuanian artist Ernest Zacharevic, the best known of them all, usually with a queue to photograph it.
  • Boy on Bike and the rest of the series: scattered through the old town — pick up a mural map and collect them as you go.
  • Marking George Town steel-rod sculptures: 52 wrought-iron caricatures that tell the history of each street with some humour.
  • Along the way: galleries, independent bookshops and old temples (Kuan Yin Teng, Sri Mahamariamman).

Lunch & free time (14:00–15:30) — friend's itinerary

Two daytime-only institutions belong in this block — both are open on Saturday Oct 10

  • Joo Hooi Café | 475 Jalan Penang | 11:00–17:30: asam laksa, char koay teow and cendol in one sitting.
  • Siam Road Charcoal Char Koay Teow | 82 Jalan Siam | Tue–Sat 12:00–18:00 (closed Sun & Mon): Michelin Bib Gourmand charcoal-fried koay teow. About 2 km from the old town, so take a Grab (10 minutes) — and go early, as it closes when it sells out.
Otherwise
  • Cafés and dessert shops: the old town has no shortage of air-conditioned cafés in converted shophouses.
  • Durian and tropical fruit: Penang is Malaysia's durian heartland (Musang King, Red Prawn), but the season runs roughly June–August, and October is off-season — durian ice cream or durian cendol instead.
  • Tambun biscuits and nutmeg products: the definitive Penang souvenirs.

Penang State Museum & Art Gallery (15:30–17:30) — friend's itinerary

  • Getting there: on Farquhar Street, about 10 minutes' walk from the mural quarter.
  • The 200-year-old building spent nine years under restoration from 2017 and only reopened on 23 July 2026.
  • Fifteen galleries and roughly 1,200 objects, from the prehistoric Gua Kepah site through to Penang around independence, with digital and AI-guided elements added.
  • Check the day's hours and ticket price before going — entry was free for a period after reopening and has since returned to a charge.
  • Fallback: if it is closed, Pinang Peranakan Mansion has a superb Baba-Nyonya collection.

Kimberley Street food stalls (17:30–21:00) — friend's itinerary

  • Getting there: 5–10 minutes on foot from the museum, or a short Grab.
  • What it is: after dark the whole street fills with stalls and tables. Most stalls only open in the late afternoon.
Where to eat
  • Duck koay chiap: glossy braised duck with flat rice sheets — the street's signature dish.
  • Char koay teow and hokkien prawn mee stalls: several run at once, so order small portions and share.
  • Cendol and ais kacang
  • Freshly ground soy milk, white coffee, sugarcane juice and herbal teas.

[Day 6 · Sun 10/11] Departure — friend's itinerary

Penang airport is 16–20 km from George Town, 30–40 minutes by road; Sunday traffic is lighter, so 45 minutes is ample.

Last free time in George Town (09:00–11:30)

Pick one (all walkable):

  • Pinang Peranakan Mansion: a rich Baba-Nyonya collection, 1–1.5 hours, and air-conditioned.
  • Chew Jetty and the waterfront streets: quieter and better lit in the morning, good for last photographs.
  • Souvenir shopping: Tambun biscuits and nutmeg products, white coffee, BOH tea. Old-town shops open from about 09:30 and are cheaper than the airport.

Check out, airport and home (11:30–15:10)

  • 11:30–12:00: check out and collect the bags.
  • 12:00–12:45: Grab or hotel shuttle to Penang International Airport.
  • 12:45–15:10: check-in, bag drop, security and boarding. Allow 2–3 hours for an international departure.
  • Duty free: last chance for Beryl's chocolate, white coffee and pewter.
