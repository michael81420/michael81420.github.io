[Day 1 · Tue 10/06] Arrival and a first look at Bukit Bintang

Fill in the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) online within 3 days of departure (Oct 3–5) — it is free and mandatory since 2024. Taiwanese passport holders are eligible for Autogate, but a first entry requires a manual counter for biometric enrolment. There is no Uber in Malaysia; use Grab for everything.

Arrival at KLIA and hotel check-in (13:25–16:00)

  • Immigration: land at KLIA T1 at 13:25, clear immigration, collect bags, buy a SIM (CelcomDigi / Maxis / U Mobile). A meal is served on the flight, so there is no rush to find lunch.
  • Transfer: 50–60 minutes from the airport by Grab, or KLIA Ekspres plus a connection. Leave at 14:30, arrive around 15:30.
  • SKYLINE KL: prime Bukit Bintang location, every major mall within walking distance. Drop the bags, change, and head straight out — budget 30 minutes. You can walk back any time in the evening.

Bukit Bintang shopping district (16:00–19:00)

  • Getting there: 5–10 minutes on foot from the hotel; air-conditioned skybridges connect the malls, so rain is not a problem.
  • What it is: KL's busiest shopping and nightlife district. All six stops below are within a 10-minute walk — three hours is enough to take it slowly.
  • Pavilion KL: the district's flagship mall, the widest range of luxury brands and dining, with the crystal fountain out front as a landmark.
  • Lot 10: the green-clad veteran mall; Lot 10 Hutong food court is in the basement.
  • Low Yat Plaza: Malaysia's largest electronics mall — phone accessories, cameras and PC parts in one building.
  • Sungei Wang Plaza: the older local mall next to Low Yat, a maze of small independent shops. More everyday life than Pavilion.
  • Fahrenheit 88: across from Pavilion, concentrated Japanese brands and a Uniqlo flagship.
  • Bukit Bintang crossing: buskers and street-dance crews gather here at dusk — the fastest way to feel the city's street energy.
Where to eat
  • Lot 10 Hutong: a basement food court gathering about 20 heritage hawker stalls from around the Klang Valley (Mo Sun Kau bak kut teh, Kim Lian Kee hokkien mee and more). Air-conditioned, and the safest possible first meal in KL.
  • Pavilion KL food court: satay, nasi kuning and laksa all in one place.

Jalan Alor night food street (19:00–21:00)

  • Getting there: 10–15 minutes on foot from the malls.
  • What it is: KL's most famous open-air food street. From 17:00 the tables come out along the whole road — 20-plus restaurants and hundreds of stalls, open until the small hours. It runs every night, so it is effectively the city centre's permanent night market.
Where to eat
  • Wong Ah Wah: the street's landmark. Charcoal-grilled chicken wings and satay, 16:00–02:00.
  • Sun Fong Bak Kut Teh: a 50-year-old institution founded in 1971. Not actually on Jalan Alor — it is at 37 Medan Imbi, a 10–12 minute walk; open daily 07:00–23:00.
  • Char koay teow and oyster omelette stalls: cooked to order over a roaring flame.
  • Ikan bakar (grilled stingray) and chicken skewers: the most atmospheric stretch, with the charcoal going full blast after 19:00.
  • Fresh sugarcane juice and coconut water.

Supper (after 21:00)

Bukit Bintang is the easiest place in KL to find a late meal — once Jalan Alor winds down, the two streets behind it have a whole row of places open into the early morning, all within walking distance.

5–10 minutes on foot
  • Ngau Kee Beef Noodles | Tengkat Tong Shin | 18:00–04:00: a 40-year-old shop with an unmistakable neon sign. Beef ball noodles and dry brisket noodles are the ones to get.
  • Restoran TG's Bistro | Tengkat Tong Shin | 24 hours: the closest round-the-clock mamak. Nasi kandar, roti canai, maggi goreng.
  • Suzi's Corner | Tengkat Tong Shin | 18:00–03:00: late-night Malay fried noodles and nasi goreng. Closed Tuesdays — which is exactly Day 1, so this one has to wait.
  • Changkat Bukit Bintang | until 02:00–03:00: a row of bars and live-music venues in converted shophouses.
10–15 minutes by Grab
  • Restoran Nasi Kandar Pelita | Jalan Ampang | 24 hours: Malaysia's best-known nasi kandar chain — rice under three or four curries at once.
  • Nasi Lemak Wanjo | Kampung Baru | 06:00–00:30: the sambal is the whole point. Also a chance to see the old Malay village in the middle of the city.
  • Pudu Wai Sek Kai (Glutton Street) | Pudu | approx. 11:00–22:00: an old supper street with the most authentic Cantonese and Peranakan flavours. Closes earlier than you would expect — arrive before 21:30.
  • ABC Bistro Cafe | Masjid India | 24 hours: a round-the-clock mamak in the Indian quarter.
  • Yan Yang A One | Jalan Klang Lama | 24 hours: dim sum in the middle of the night — har gow, siu mai, cheung fun.
  • Kedai Kopi Mee Bon | Taman Million | 06:30–02:00: an old kopitiam still serving dim sum at 2am.

[Day 2 · Wed 10/07] Batu Caves at sunrise, heritage at noon, Twin Towers at dusk

An early start: be first in line for hotel breakfast at 07:00 and leave for Batu Caves at 07:45. In the evening, the Taman Connaught night market runs on Wednesdays only — this is the trip's one chance at it. Around town, the LRT, MRT, Monorail and KTM all take a Touch 'n Go card.

Batu Caves (08:30–10:30)

  • Getting there: leave at 07:45. Grab takes 30–40 minutes; or the KTM Komuter Batu Caves line from KL Sentral to the terminus, about 30 minutes plus a 5-minute walk.
  • Hours: daily 07:00–21:00, free entry. 08:00–09:00 is the best window: 26–28°C instead of the 33–36°C at midday, a quiet staircase, and the most dramatic light falling through the Temple Cave's natural opening. Tour groups only start arriving after 09:30.
  • The site: a limestone cave complex dedicated to the Hindu deity Murugan, with a 42.7-metre gold statue at the entrance.
  • The 272 rainbow steps: repainted in 2018 and now the site's signature image. A relaxed climb takes 15–20 minutes. Morning light is front-on, so the gold statue and the coloured stairs photograph well together.
  • Temple Cave: the roof is open to the sky and a shaft of light falls straight into the chamber in the morning — the reason not to come at dusk.
  • Ramayana Cave: a painted cavern to the left of the main stairs, about RM5, worth adding if time allows.
  • Practicalities: knees must be covered inside (sarongs can be hired on site); wear proper shoes. The macaques will snatch food and phones — carry nothing in your hands, do not feed them, and keep bags zipped.
Where to eat (breakfast was at the hotel, so no full meal is needed here)
  • Indian vegetarian restaurants and mamak stalls at the foot of the stairs: open from early morning — thosai or roti canai with teh tarik.
  • Fresh sugarcane juice and coconut water: exactly what 272 steps calls for.

Lunch and the ride back into town (10:30–12:45)

  • Getting there: 10:30–11:30 by Grab or KTM back to the Merdeka Square / Masjid Jamek area.
Where to eat
  • Nasi campur: rice with self-selected dishes priced by weight. The spread is widest between 11:30 and 14:00, and it is the quickest way to get to know Malay food.
  • A mamak stall: roti canai stretched and griddled to order, with a glass of teh tarik.
  • Kasturi Walk at Central Market: 8 minutes from Merdeka Square, air-conditioned, plenty of choice — the wet-weather fallback.

KL City Gallery & Merdeka Square (12:45–15:15)

  • Getting around: everything in this quarter is 5–10 minutes apart on foot.
  • KL City Gallery: known for its enormous scale model of the city and the "I ♥ KL" sign. A fast way to grasp how the city is laid out. Daily 09:00–18:30.
  • Merdeka Square: where the Malaysian flag was first raised in 1957, with a nearly 100-metre flagpole at its centre.
  • Sultan Abdul Samad Building: the copper-domed clock tower opposite the square, and KL's most classic photo subject.
  • Masjid Jamek: a red-and-white brick mosque completed in 1909 at the confluence of two rivers. Non-Muslim visiting hours are generally 08:30–12:30 and 14:30–16:00, so do the gallery and the square first and visit the mosque after 14:30. Robes are lent free at the entrance.
Where to eat
  • An old-town kopitiam: duck inside during the hottest part of the afternoon for an iced kopi and kaya toast.

PETRONAS Twin Towers & Skybridge (16:00–17:30)

  • Getting there: leave at 15:15. LRT from Merdeka Square to KLCC is about 15 minutes, Grab 15–20. Allow the full 45 minutes — timed entry, and a missed slot is not rebooked.
  • The towers: designed by César Pelli, briefly the tallest building in the world on completion in 1998, with Islamic geometry worked into the facade.
  • Skybridge and the level 86 observation deck: the walkway on levels 41–42 plus the deck above, 45–60 minutes in total. Tue–Sun 09:00–21:00 (last entry 20:30), closed Mondays. Book online in advance and pick a slot around 16:00.
  • Going up at 16:00 puts you back on the ground right at sunset (around 19:10 in October), so one visit covers both the daytime view and dusk — no need for a second trip after dark.

KLCC Park, Lake Symphony & the towers after dark (17:30–19:30)

  • Getting there: straight out of the towers — the park is directly behind Suria KLCC.
  • KLCC Park: the best place to photograph the towers in full, especially from the far side of the pool.
  • Lake Symphony fountain: shows every 30 minutes from early evening, with light and music. Best after dark.
  • When to shoot: sunset is around 19:10, and the blue hour from 18:45 to 19:30 — sky still blue, building already lit — is the best 20 minutes the towers get.
  • Suria KLCC: somewhere air-conditioned to wait for the light.

Taman Connaught night market & dinner (19:30–22:00)

  • Getting there: Grab from KLCC at 19:30, about 25–30 minutes to Cheras. The MRT Kajang line also stops at Taman Connaught, with a link bridge through Cheras Sentral, but from KLCC that means a change and roughly 40 minutes.
  • Wednesdays only — Oct 7 is the trip's single opportunity. KL's pasar malam are roving markets that set up in a different neighbourhood each night; Taman Connaught is the biggest of them.
  • Scale: roughly 2 kilometres and about 700 stalls along Jalan Cerdas, the longest night market in KL and the second longest in Malaysia. Cooked food, fresh produce, clothing and household goods; walking it one way takes about an hour.
  • Hours: roughly 17:00–23:00. Stalls are all in place after 18:30 and the crowd peaks then, so 20:00 is about right.
  • How to do it: walk about a kilometre one way and turn back, or walk to the far end and get a Grab from there — end to end and back is four kilometres. Dinner is whatever you eat along the way.
  • Practicalities: stalls take cash and Touch 'n Go eWallet almost exclusively, so bring small ringgit notes. It gets crowded; wear bags in front.
What to eat
  • Ramly burger: the griddled Malay burger wrapped in a fried egg — the soul of any pasar malam.
  • Apam balik: a thick peanut pancake, cut fresh off the griddle.
  • Grilled wings and satay: a whole row of charcoal stalls, noticeably cheaper than Jalan Alor.
  • Char koay teow, hokkien mee, oyster omelette: the stretch where the Chinese stalls cluster.
  • Air tebu (sugarcane juice), coconut water, cendol: essential halfway through.
  • Kuih and tropical fruit: durian, mangosteen and rambutan cut to order.

Grab back to the hotel at 22:00–22:30. After a 07:00 start, 272 steps and two kilometres of night market, this is a night to skip supper.

Fallback: if you skip the market, have dinner at KLCC (19:30–21:00)

  • Madam Kwan's KLCC: level 4 of Suria KLCC, reliable nasi lemak and rojak, roughly 11:00–22:00. Air-conditioned and the most comfortable option after a long day.
  • Suria KLCC food court: in the basement — nasi lemak, roti canai, laksa and satay in one sitting, roughly 10:00–22:00.
  • Sun Fong Bak Kut Teh: 37 Medan Imbi, daily 07:00–23:00, about 10 minutes by Grab from KLCC.

[Day 3 · Thu 10/08] Heritage, old streets and REXKL

Today covers KL's colonial heritage and its Chinese old town. There is no afternoon return to the hotel — REXKL's air conditioning covers the hottest hours instead. Tomorrow's ETS leaves at 08:05 and means a 06:45 wake-up, so pack tonight and head back early; nothing after KL Tower.

National Mosque (Masjid Negara) & the old railway station (09:00–11:00)

  • Getting there: leave at 08:45 by Grab or KTM to "Kuala Lumpur" station, about 15 minutes. The two sites are 5–10 minutes apart on foot.
  • National Mosque: completed in 1965, known for its folded-umbrella blue roof and 73-metre minaret. Open to non-Muslims outside prayer times (closed for Friday midday prayers; this visit is a Thursday, so not affected). Robes and headscarves are lent free; shoes come off at the door.
  • Old railway station: opened in 1910, a white heritage building blending Moorish, Mughal and British colonial styles, with the old KTM headquarters opposite. Tomorrow's ETS does not leave from here — it leaves from KL Sentral, one stop away. Do not mix them up.

Petaling Street (Chinatown) (11:30–13:30)

  • Getting there: leave at 11:00, KTM or LRT to Pasar Seni, then 3–5 minutes on foot.
  • Petaling Street: KL's defining Chinese old street — under the red gateway, rows of old buildings, antique shops, herbalists and hawker stalls.
  • Central Market (Pasar Seni): an art-deco market 5 minutes away with batik, pewter and handicrafts under one air-conditioned roof. Buy the KL-only souvenirs here — after this the trip moves to Penang.
Lunch (13:30–14:30)
  • Nam Heong Chicken Rice: Hainanese chicken rice since 1938, Michelin-recommended, at 56 Jalan Sultan. Daily 10:00–16:00. First choice here, but the queue is long — go earlier if you can.
  • Kim Lian Kee: open since 1927, dark-soy hokkien mee and claypot loh shu fan, at 92 Jalan Hang Lekir. 11:00–23:00, closed Wednesdays (this visit is a Thursday, so it is open).
  • Hon Kee Porridge: a long-standing Cantonese congee shop. Opens at dawn and closes around 14:30, so arriving at 13:30 usually means it has sold out — go at breakfast instead.
  • Guilinggao (herbal jelly): sold by several herbalists along the street, available any time in the afternoon.

Kwai Chai Hong & Lorong Panggung shophouse lane (14:30–15:30)

  • Getting there: it runs off Petaling Street — 2–3 minutes on foot.
  • Kwai Chai Hong: the Cantonese name for Lorong Panggung, roughly "Little Demon Alley". In 2019 ten pre-war shophouses — six fronting Petaling Street, four on Lorong Panggung — were restored along with the lane between them. The walls carry murals of 1960s Chinese life in KL, hung with red lanterns. Daily 09:00–24:00, free.
  • Lorong Panggung: the pre-war shophouses on both sides are now cafés, small bars, galleries and independent shops. Narrow lane, low buildings, small businesses, no chains — the closest thing here to a Chifeng Street or Ninenzaka. Give it 30–40 minutes.
  • Merchant's Lane: a much-loved café on the upper floor of a Petaling Street shophouse, with the original brickwork and light well intact — the emblem of this quarter's revival.
  • Suggested loop: Petaling Street → Kwai Chai Hong for the murals → out through Lorong Panggung → back along the Jalan Sultan shophouses → on to REXKL.
Where to eat
  • Egg tarts and nyonya kuih from the cafés in the lane, with a kopi — enough to hold you until the night market.

REXKL (15:30–17:00)

  • Getting there: 5–8 minutes along Jalan Sultan from Kwai Chai Hong.
  • REXKL: the 1970s REX cinema, derelict after two fires, reopened in 2019 as a bookshop, exhibition hall and food space with the raw brick and steel left exposed — KL's most successful adaptive reuse project.
  • BookXcess at REXKL: the old auditorium rebuilt as stacked walls of books and stepped seating, with a circular "book maze" at the centre. The most photogenic bookshop in Malaysia, mostly English titles and very cheap.
  • Ground floor: food stalls and an exhibition space that often hosts independent markets, art shows and live music.
  • Hours: Mon–Thu 08:30–22:00, Fri–Sun 08:30–24:00. This visit is a Thursday, so it closes at 22:00.
  • Entirely indoors and air-conditioned, which covers the hottest and most thunderstorm-prone part of the afternoon; the Chinatown night market is a 5-minute walk away at 17:00.

Chinatown night market (17:00–18:45)

  • What changes: Petaling Street only comes fully alive after dark — awnings go up and the whole street turns into a dense night market selling clothes, souvenirs and food at once. Haggling is normal; start at half the asking price.
Where to eat
  • Air Mata Kucing: the century-old herbal longan drink stall at the head of the street — the one thing everyone drinks here.
  • Char koay teow, siew mai and cendol: made to order at several stalls, good for something small before dinner.

KL Tower (Menara KL) at night & dinner in the revolving restaurant (19:00–21:00)

  • Getting there: Grab from Chinatown at 18:45, about 15 minutes, in time for the 19:00 booking; or the Monorail to Bukit Nanas and up the hill (steep — take the shuttle).
  • KL Tower: a 421-metre communications tower opened in 1996. Because it sits more centrally and higher than the Twin Towers, this is the only viewpoint that fits the whole of the Twin Towers into a single frame — particularly good at night.
  • Sky Deck and Sky Box: an open-air deck with glass boxes you can stand in to photograph the city under your feet. Expect a queue.
  • Atmosphere 360: one full rotation takes about an hour; a Malay and international buffet, and the meal includes deck admission. Daily 10:30–23:00 — book the 19:00 seating online in advance.

Wrap up at 21:00, pack at the hotel, then sleep — tomorrow starts at 06:45.

[Day 4 · Fri 10/09] Crossing to Penang by train and ferry

Today runs on the KTM ETS, not a plane. The key fact: the ETS terminates at Butterworth on the mainland — it does not reach Penang island, so a sea crossing still follows. The good news is that Butterworth station, the bus terminal and the ferry jetty are all inside the same Penang Sentral interchange, a few minutes' walk apart.

ETS train: KL Sentral → Butterworth (08:05–12:10)

  • Suggested service: ETS Platinum 9172, departing 08:05 and arriving Butterworth at 12:10 (4h05m). Times change — confirm on the KTMB site before booking.
  • Booking: KTMB's official site or the KITS app. Friday northbound is a peak departure; book well ahead rather than at the counter.
  • Which class: Platinum and Express make the fewest stops and are fastest; Gold stops everywhere and adds 30–45 minutes.
  • Check-in: KTMB requires arrival 30 minutes before departure and does not refund no-shows — be at KL Sentral by 07:35.
  • On board: four hours, so take a window seat for the rubber plantations and kampung along the way. The air conditioning is fierce — bring a light jacket. Racks are inside the carriage; large cases go by the doors.
  • Fallback: if 08:05 is sold out, take an earlier 07:00–07:30 service. Do not take anything after 09:00 — it eats into Fort Cornwallis in the afternoon.
  • The route passes through Ipoh, the birthplace of Malaysian white coffee.

Ferry: Butterworth → George Town (12:10–13:00)

  • The transfer: Butterworth station is Penang Sentral — train, bus and ferry terminals in one building, connected by covered walkways that are easy with luggage. Allow about 20 minutes.
  • Frequency: every 30 minutes, 06:30–23:30 (last Butterworth sailing 22:30). The crossing is 3.2 km of the Penang Strait, about 15–20 minutes. The fare is around RM2.
  • Important: the ferry is now card-only — contactless credit or debit, no cash. Check your card works before boarding, and carry two from different issuers.
  • The new vessels carry foot passengers, motorbikes and bicycles only — cars use the Penang Bridge. Luggage is no problem.
  • Why it is worth it: the crossing is a classic Penang experience in its own right — the George Town waterfront skyline and the Penang Bridge in the distance, none of which you get by flying.
  • Fallback: if the ferry is suspended or the queue is long, take a Grab from Butterworth station over the Penang Bridge — 30–45 minutes and RM30–45, still in time for a 13:30 check-in.

George Town check-in & lunch (13:30–15:00)

  • Where to stay: inside the George Town heritage zone. Three reasons: the Pangkalan Raja Tun Uda ferry terminal sits on the edge of the old town, 5–10 minutes by Grab; Fort Cornwallis, the murals, Kimberley Street and Penang Road are all walkable; and the airport run on Oct 10 is predictable from here.
  • Shortlist: Cheong Fatt Tze — The Blue Mansion (a heritage boutique hotel that is itself a sight), Eastern & Oriental Hotel (colonial grande dame from 1885, closest to the fort), Bayview Hotel Georgetown (mid-range, good value).
  • The city: George Town was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008 and preserves a very large stock of 18th- and 19th-century shophouses.
Lunch
  • A traditional kopitiam: the train only has a snack counter, so most people land hungry. There are several within a 10-minute walk of the jetty — a bowl of hokkien prawn mee or a plate of chicken rice.

Fort Cornwallis (15:00–17:30)

  • Getting there: 10–15 minutes on foot from the old-town hotels, or 5–10 by Grab.
  • The fort: built after Captain Francis Light landed in 1786, and the largest surviving British colonial star fort in Malaysia.
  • Cannon and displays: the Dutch-made "Seri Rambai" cannon, plus the old armoury and magazine.
  • The waterfront: looking out over the Penang Strait to the mainland — the side you arrived from by train this morning, with the ferries still shuttling across. The light is best in the late afternoon.
  • City Hall and the Esplanade: the lawn and white colonial buildings just outside the fort, where locals walk at dusk.

The Esplanade & Penang Road food street (17:30–21:00)

  • Getting there: the Esplanade is next to the fort; Penang Road is another 10 minutes on foot or a short Grab.
  • The Esplanade (Padang Kota Lama): a seafront square and promenade with food stalls and a sea breeze — a good place for sunset.
  • Penang Road: one of the densest concentrations of long-running hawker stalls in the city — cendol, char koay teow and prawn mee down one street.
Where to eat
  • The cendol stall shuts at 19:00, so do dessert before dinner here.
  • Penang Road Famous Teochew Chendul (go straight there at 17:30): the century-old cendol stall on Lebuh Keng Kwee with a permanent queue, next door to its own asam laksa stall. Mon–Fri 10:30–19:00, weekends 10:00–19:30 — be in the queue by 18:30.
  • Hokkien prawn mee: an orange-red broth simmered from prawn heads and pork bones, nothing like the Taiwanese dish of the same name.
  • Oh chien (oyster omelette): the Penang version is crisper, egg and sweet potato starch fried until caramelised.
  • Lebuh Chulia night stalls: 5 minutes away, with stalls and small bars along a street of century-old shophouses — a good second stop.

Six hours of travel today, so keep the evening light and head back after dinner.

[Day 5 · Sat 10/10] George Town old streets, murals and the museum

The George Town core is compact and the hotel is inside it, so most of the day is walkable; the free CAT shuttle or a short Grab covers the hottest stretches. michael leaves at midday: check out during breakfast at 08:00 and leave the bags at reception, collect them at 11:15, and take a Grab out of George Town at 11:30 — that is the latest safe departure, with the airport 30–40 minutes away. Everything from 12:00 is the friend's itinerary.

George Town old streets (09:00–12:00)

  • Getting around: the hotel is in the old town; everything is 5–15 minutes on foot.
  • The heritage core: several hundred shophouses from the 18th and 19th centuries, with traditional trade workshops, antique dealers, cafés and studios tucked along the narrow streets.
  • Chew Jetty: a Chinese clan settlement built out over the water, boardwalks lined with century-old houses and provision shops — right beside the ferry terminal from yesterday.
  • Khoo Kongsi: the most ornate Chinese clan house in Penang, with exceptional carving and painted detail.
  • Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion (the Blue Mansion): the indigo-walled residence of a late-Qing merchant, with fixed daily tours (around 11:00, 14:00 and 15:30 — confirm on site). The 11:00 tour would make michael miss the 11:30 departure, so skip it.
  • michael's condensed route (09:00–11:15): hotel → Chew Jetty (09:00–09:50) → Khoo Kongsi (10:00–10:35) → the "Kids on Bicycle" mural on Armenian Street (10:45–11:15) → back to the hotel for bags.
Where to eat
  • Joo Hooi Café: an old coffee shop at 475 Jalan Penang where one table gets you asam laksa, char koay teow, oyster omelette and cendol at once — the most efficient meal in Penang. Daily 11:00–17:30, so it is still shut when this block starts at 09:00. michael cannot make it; the friend should slot it into the block after 12:00.
  • A Penang kopitiam: kopi O with charcoal-toasted kaya toast and soft-boiled eggs — the standard local breakfast, open from 07:00. This, or a roadside nasi lemak stall, is michael's window for Penang hawker food.

Street-art walk (12:00–14:00) — friend's itinerary

  • Getting around: all on foot; the main murals cluster along Armenian Street and Lebuh Ah Quee.
  • Kids on Bicycle: by Lithuanian artist Ernest Zacharevic, the best known of them all, usually with a queue to photograph it.
  • Boy on Bike and the rest of the series: scattered through the old town — pick up a mural map and collect them as you go.
  • Marking George Town steel-rod sculptures: 52 wrought-iron caricatures that tell the history of each street with some humour.
  • The whole quarter is best treated as a walking treasure hunt, past galleries, independent bookshops and old temples (Kuan Yin Teng, Sri Mahamariamman).

Lunch & free time (14:00–15:30) — friend's itinerary

Two daytime-only institutions belong in this block — both are open on Saturday Oct 10

  • Joo Hooi Café | 475 Jalan Penang | 11:00–17:30: asam laksa, char koay teow and cendol in one sitting, on the way back from the murals.
  • Siam Road Charcoal Char Koay Teow | 82 Jalan Siam | Tue–Sat 12:00–18:00 (closed Sun & Mon): Michelin Bib Gourmand charcoal-fried koay teow, now run by the founder's son. About 2 km from the old town, so take a Grab (10 minutes) — and go early, as it closes when it sells out.
Otherwise
  • Cafés and dessert shops: the old town has no shortage of air-conditioned cafés in converted shophouses — the right move during the hottest hours.
  • Durian and tropical fruit: Penang is Malaysia's durian heartland (Musang King, Red Prawn), but the season runs roughly June–August, and October is off-season — durian ice cream or durian cendol instead.
  • Tambun biscuits and nutmeg products: the definitive Penang souvenirs, worth picking up here.

Penang State Museum & Art Gallery (15:30–17:30) — friend's itinerary

  • Getting there: on Farquhar Street, about 10 minutes' walk from the mural quarter.
  • The 200-year-old building spent nine years under restoration from 2017 and only reopened on 23 July 2026 — the newest thing on this itinerary, visited in its third month back.
  • Fifteen galleries and roughly 1,200 objects, from the prehistoric Gua Kepah site through to Penang around independence, with digital and AI-guided elements added.
  • Check the day's hours and ticket price before going — entry was free for a period after reopening and has since returned to a charge.
  • Fallback: if it is closed, Pinang Peranakan Mansion has a superb Baba-Nyonya collection.

Kimberley Street food stalls (17:30–21:00) — friend's itinerary

  • Getting there: 5–10 minutes on foot from the museum, or a short Grab.
  • What it is: after dark the whole street fills with stalls and tables — George Town's classic evening eating street. Most stalls only open in the late afternoon.
Where to eat
  • Duck koay chiap: glossy braised duck with flat rice sheets — the street's signature dish.
  • Char koay teow and hokkien prawn mee stalls: several run at once, so order small portions and share.
  • Cendol and ais kacang: the way to finish a tropical evening.
  • Freshly ground soy milk, white coffee, sugarcane juice and herbal teas.

[Day 6 · Sun 10/11] Departure — friend's itinerary

Penang airport is 16–20 km from George Town, 30–40 minutes by road; Sunday traffic is lighter, so 45 minutes is ample. The return is by air, not by ferry — do not confuse it with the Day 4 route into the city.

Last free time in George Town (09:00–11:30)

A spare half-morning. Three options, all within walking distance:

  • Pinang Peranakan Mansion: a rich Baba-Nyonya collection, 1–1.5 hours, and air-conditioned.
  • Chew Jetty and the waterfront streets: quieter and better lit in the morning, good for last photographs.
  • Souvenir shopping: Tambun biscuits and nutmeg products, white coffee, BOH tea. Old-town shops open from about 09:30 and are cheaper than the airport.

Check out, airport and home (11:30–15:10)

  • 11:30–12:00: check out and collect the bags — most hotels check out at 12:00, so the timing works.
  • 12:00–12:45: Grab or hotel shuttle to Penang International Airport.
  • 12:45–15:10: check-in, bag drop, security and boarding. Allow 2–3 hours for an international departure.
  • Duty free: last chance for Beryl's chocolate, white coffee and pewter.
  • Airport cafés: one final Penang white coffee.
