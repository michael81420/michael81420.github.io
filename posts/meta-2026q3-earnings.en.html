Meta．Q3 2026 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of $64.60B, +26% YoY, above the top of its own guidance ($61–64B). The ad business is fine.
The new facts are elsewhere: free cash flow of −$2.12B, the first negative since the IPO, and a $9.89B teen-litigation settlement — last quarter's warning of a "possible material loss" became a booked charge within two months. Built from the IR release and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: Meta IR release (via multiple outlets) + Futu Period: 2026-07-01 – 2026-09-30 Reported: 2026-10-21 after close Compiled: 2026-10-25

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

💧 FCF turned negative, and guidance implies a deeper hole next quarter. Operating cash flow $32.79B; quarterly capex $34.91B (incl. finance lease principal; +12% QoQ, +80% YoY); FCF −$2.12B (IR basis) vs +$10.63B a year ago. Full-year capex guide floor rose $130B → $135B (top unchanged at $145B). With $85.82B spent in nine months, Q4 implies $49.2–59.2B, 41–70% above this quarter. Last quarter we asked whether FCF would return to $8–10B. It did the opposite.

📉 Strip out the settlement and margins are still sliding. GAAP operating margin was 20% (40% a year ago); the $9.89B legal charge accounts for 15.3pp. Ex-charge it is 35.4%, 1.4pp below last quarter's ex-one-off 36.8% and about 4.6pp below a year ago. Two quarters in a row of "decline even after removing one-offs" confirms last quarter's structural-erosion read.

⚖️ Litigation went from "possible" to "booked", at 4x the prior accrual. On the Q2 call management warned of "material loss" outside the $2.4B accrual. This quarter: a settlement with 52 state attorneys general, up to ~$18.59B, with $9.89B recognized now. Whether the remaining ~$8.7B hits the P&L later is not clarified.

🪙 A new financing signal: non-voting Class C shares. From 2027-01-19, one Class C share per existing share (effectively a 2-for-1), which media read as a way to raise capital without diluting Zuckerberg's control. With buybacks at $0 for nine months and cash and securities of $82.45B against $80.92B of long-term debt (net cash near zero), "return cash to holders" has turned into "prepare to raise it".

★ The market's verdict: $697.52 → $648.85 (−7.0%) the next day; an after-hours gain of ~4% fully reversed. Over the past 22 trading days META is −17.4%, while PINS is +19.2% and SNAP +16.8%. The beat was a business beat; the selling was about cash flow and capex.

1. Cash flow: operating cash flow still grows, capex grows faster

Operating cash flow of $32.79B is +2.9% QoQ versus $31.86B. Backing out last year (FCF $10.625B + capex $19.374B) gives about $30.0B, so YoY growth is only about +9% (it was +24.7% last quarter). Operating cash flow growth decelerated faster than revenue growth (+26%). Part of that is timing (legal charge accrued but not yet paid in cash), so it should not all be read as the core business weakening, but it should not be ignored either.

Capex of $34.906B = purchases of property and equipment $33.942B + finance lease principal $0.964B. Nine-month capex is $85.824B (vs $50.078B). Net PP&E rose from $176.4B at end-2025 to $253.2B, up $76.8B in nine months. D&A is already $7.154B, +44% YoY, with capex still rising.

Balance sheet: cash and marketable securities $82.45B (June: $90.26B, −$7.8B); long-term debt $80.92B with no new issuance this quarter. Net cash is about +$1.5B, essentially gone. No buybacks in nine months (vs $26.25B a year ago) but $1.36B of dividends were paid. Restricted cash is $12.5B (from $1.6B at year-end) and other liabilities $13.3B (from $4.3B); both look related to the settlement but the release does not say so.

📌 ⚠️ FCF definition: our Q2 post used Futu's cash flow statement (capex $30.116B) and got Q2 FCF of $1.746B. On the IR basis (capex $31.08B) Q2 FCF was $0.784B. This post uses the IR basis throughout. A Futu-style calculation using only purchases of property and equipment ($33.94B) would give roughly −$1.15B this quarter (own calculation); same direction.

2. Margins: GAAP 20%, still 35.4% ex-settlement

Operating income was $12.99B (−37% YoY), margin 20% (40% a year ago). Adding back the $9.89B settlement gives $22.88B and 35.4%. Q2 on the same basis was 36.8%, so the adjusted series is 40.6% (Q1) → 36.8% (Q2) → 35.4% (Q3), three declines in a row.

Cost lines (IR): cost of revenue $13.37B (+45% YoY; gross margin 79.3% by our calculation vs 81.4% in Q2 and 82.0% a year ago); R&D $20.05B (+32%); D&A $7.15B (+44%); stock comp $7.36B (+33%). Every cost line grows faster than revenue (+26%). What eats margin is depreciation and inference cost (cost of revenue), not marketing.

Segments (IR): Family of Apps operating income $17.12B. If the full $9.89B sits in this segment (our assumption; the summary does not give the allocation), adjusted FoA income is about $27.0B, a margin of ~42.1% vs 49.2% a year ago ($24.97B ÷ $50.77B), about 7pp lower. Reality Labs lost $4.13B, narrower than Q2's $4.62B and the $5.13B expected.

Headcount is 66,775, −15% YoY and −11.5% QoQ (Q2: 75,472). Cutting staff did not stop margin erosion, which says the pressure comes from AI infrastructure, not payroll.

3. Advertising: the volume/price balance shifted again

Ad revenue was $63.06B (+26% YoY; ~$61.85B expected), 97.6% of revenue. Volume vs price: impressions +10% (Q1 +19%, Q2 +14%; third straight deceleration and the lowest of five quarters), price per ad +14% (+12% in both Q1 and Q2; the first uptick). Last quarter's post described the weight shifting from volume to price; this quarter extends and deepens that.

By region (IR): US & Canada ad revenue $28.44B, +33% YoY, price +23% (Q1 +14%, Q2 +20%, Q3 +23%, three quarters of acceleration), impressions +8%; Europe price +10%, impressions +8%; Asia-Pacific impressions +14% but price only +1%; Rest of World price +24%. US & Canada is ~45.1% of ad revenue (42.6% a year ago). Growth is concentrated in US and Canada pricing, the main source of "price carrying the load"; whether that price momentum continues matters more than global impressions.

Family daily active people were 3.64B (+3% YoY; Q2 3.60B, also +3%), so growth held at +3%. Average revenue per person was $17.72 (vs $14.46, about +22.5% by our calculation).

📌 Last quarter we set "impressions below +10% with price not rising" as the trigger for ad revenue trouble. Impressions came in at exactly +10% (not broken) and price rose to +14%, so the trigger did not fire and +26% revenue held. But the Q4 revenue guide of $73–76B implies +21.9–26.9% YoY (midpoint ~+24.4%), the first time growth is pre-announced to slow (including ~1pp of FX headwind).

4. Guidance: revenue ahead, capex and expenses still rising

Capex guidance path: $120–135B (Q4'25 call) → $125–145B (Q1) → $130–145B (Q2) → $135–145B (now). The floor has been raised four times in a row; the top has not moved since Q1. The range is narrowing, but toward "we will definitely spend a lot". Nine months total $85.82B, so Q4 implies $49.2–59.2B (own calculation); ZeroHedge's "up to $59B in Q4" is consistent, against a prior single-quarter record of this quarter's $34.9B.

Expense guidance: $165–169B → $175–179B, a +$10B step that matches the $9.89B settlement. Excluding it the range is $165.1–169.1B, essentially unchanged. Implied Q4 expenses are $47.9–51.9B (own calculation) vs about $41.7B in Q3 ex-settlement (own calculation), +15–24% QoQ. Against Q4 revenue of $73–76B, implied Q4 operating margin is ~29–37%, midpoint ~33% (own calculation), below this quarter's adjusted 35.4%.

Other: Reality Labs 2026 loss $18–19B (nine months $12.78B, so Q4 implies $5.2–6.2B, larger than Q3's $4.13B), about $15B in 2027; Q4 tax rate 15–17%; management expects 2026 operating income ex-legal-charge to exceed 2025. No 2027 capex figure appears in what we could access; JPM cites a buy-side 2027 opex-plus-capex range of $435–475B.

5. Earnings call takeaways

★ No full call transcript was obtained this time. We have the release, reprints of CFO commentary, and media summaries; anything oral below is second-hand and Q&A content is unavailable. Items marked "release" are written text, not oral remarks.

1) Changes in wording (compared with the Q2 wiki [call] entries)

· Teen litigation: on the Q2 call (7/29) Li said multiple U.S. trials may ultimately result in a material loss, outside the $2.4B accrual. This quarter's written result is a settlement with 52 states and a $9.89B charge, up to ~$18.59B. "May" became "booked" two months later, at 4.1x the earlier accrual (own calculation). That is a fact, not an opinion.

· Reality Labs: Q2 said ~70% of spend had shifted to AI glasses and wearables; this quarter adds numbers: 2026 loss $18–19B and ~$15B in 2027, the first direction for next year (via IR reprints). Yet Q4 implies a loss of $5.2–6.2B, larger than Q3, with no explanation, and no AI-glasses shipments or revenue split.

· CEO language: the release quotes Zuckerberg: Our investments are paying off, the core business is strong, and we're seeing velocity across the board (written, not oral). We have no Q2 original line in the same slot to compare word for word, so no upgrade or downgrade call.

2) Where analysts pressed: Q&A not obtained. Pre-call focus from sell-side and buy-side was 2027 capex (DB had estimated ~$210–215B) and ROI, but those are pre-call expectations, not questions actually asked.

3) Dodges and vagueness: on the Q2 call Li said Meta was not giving a 2027 capex outlook (We aren't providing a specific outlook for 2027 capex at this time, Yahoo, 2026-07-30). The Q3 material we saw still has no 2027 capex number; ZeroHedge (pre-call) says the company no longer guides beyond this year, but we cannot confirm whether Q3's call declined again. The gap between the $18.59B ceiling and the $9.89B booked is also not explained in the summaries.

4) Numbers disclosed on the call or in CFO commentary: Muse has more than 10 million accounts in the U.S. and Canada (Zuckerberg, AP reprint); CFO Li: Muse's compute intensity means that expanding its user base will require significant investment, and we expect total serving costs to rise, with compute cost efficiency expected to improve more than 10-fold over the next year (quoted by ZeroHedge in a pre-call article, so it may come from written CFO commentary rather than oral remarks). The first is a growth claim [opinion]; the second is a new cost signal: Muse serving costs will rise, consistent with cost of revenue +45%.

6. Closing the loop: Q2 watch items

Two of six failed, FCF and margins, in the same direction: last quarter left too much room for weak FCF being a one-quarter event. With the capex guide floor already raised twice, "back to $8–10B" was still listed as a reasonable benchmark, when the guidance already implied a worse next quarter. Two cross-company lessons were appended to the stock wiki's lessons.md.

7. Valuation: forward PE back to 19.7x, but cash flow has not answered yet

📉 Futu forecast valuation, forward PE 19.7x (2026-10-24; current valuation 21.9x, one-year mean 25.5x, one-year percentile 16.8%). Last quarter on the same basis: 17.9x / 5.2%. The stock went from $556.71 (three days after Q2 results) to $642.62, +15.4%, so PE recovered from 17.9x to 19.7x. Last time "cheap because it was sold off"; this time it recovered halfway and was sold again by 7%.

Analysts (Futu, updated 2026-10-22): 44 analysts, 86.4% buy / 13.6% hold / 0% sell, average target $849.74 (high $1,000 / low $580) vs $763.61 last quarter, a +11% increase while the stock fell, implying ~+32%. A tension: targets up, stock down, meaning the sell side is valuing revenue and AI prospects while buyers price FCF and capex. Target revisions tend to lag, as noted last quarter.

Earnings-day reactions (Futu): Q2 next day −7.9% ($585.15 → $538.61); Q3 next day −7.0% ($697.52 → $648.85). Two straight prints of roughly −7 to −8% next day; implied volatility fell from 47% before to 40% after.

Cycle severity (invest-playbook): digital advertising is a mid-amplitude cycle, but Meta's variable now is the capex cycle, and it is in expansion. Forward PE covers only 2026–27 earnings, and depreciation has not fully reached the P&L (D&A +44% YoY with a larger Q4 capex). Whether it is cheap or expensive waits on FCF; PE is a weak anchor right now.

8. Peer scan: digital ad platforms

Group definition: digital advertising platforms (Meta gets 97.6% of revenue from ads), alongside Google, Amazon advertising, Reddit/Pinterest/Snap social advertising and The Trade Desk programmatic advertising, all driven by advertiser budgets. The ones that are also big AI capex spenders are META, GOOG and AMZN; the smaller social platforms are not.

The clearest split over the past month (22 trading days, Futu closes): the three big capex platforms META −17.4%, GOOG −6.4%, AMZN −0.6%, versus low-capex PINS +19.2% and SNAP +16.8%. Reading (interpretation, not fact): the market is punishing capex burden, not ad demand. PINS is at 37.4x forecast PE and the 90th percentile, no longer cheap; SNAP's forecast PE is 57.3x but its current valuation is negative, so the reading is meaningless. TTD's forecast valuation of 100.95x vs 15.5x current is too far apart; ⚠️ likely a Futu forecast artifact, not relied upon.

10. Bull and bear

Three reasons to be constructive:

① The core beat is real: revenue +26% and above the guide top, US and Canada price +23% accelerating for three quarters, daily actives holding at +3%. Core EPS of $7.40 (ex-settlement) versus a single-source "beat by $0.26" headline implies a small beat.

② Management is clearing uncertainty: settlement landed, headcount down 15%, Reality Labs given a ~$15B direction for 2027. Less uncertainty than last quarter.

③ Sell-side support: 86.4% buy ratings and a target raised to $849.74. If capex converts into ad efficiency as management argues, this is the investment-phase low.

Three reasons to be cautious:

① Good numbers, but not in cash flow: in the same quarter revenue beat, FCF turned negative, net cash vanished and buybacks stayed off, with the Q4 guide implying worse. Operating cash flow grew ~9% YoY versus revenue +26%.

② Adjusted margins down three quarters in a row: 40.6% → 36.8% → 35.4%, with a Q4 guide midpoint near 33%. Costs outgrow revenue across the board and depreciation has not finished rolling in.

③ Legal tail not closed: the 52-state deal is one line of exposure. Media also report a $25B penalty ordered by a New Mexico court on 10/21 (seen only in PPC Land and a Ground News page, not in Meta documents, not independently verified). If true it is the same order of magnitude as $9.89B, and risk has not converged.

★ The disagreement is unchanged: how many years until AI capex pays back. One new data point: revenue is clearly being pushed (US and Canada pricing is accelerating), but payback is visibly slower than spend.
