TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 A second straight blowout beat — guidance simply couldn't keep up with real demand. The FY26 Q3 guide given in March called for revenue of $33.5B ± $0.75B and non-GAAP EPS of $19.15 ± $0.40. Actual results: $41.46B and $25.11 — both beating by roughly 25%. HBM and server DRAM shortages are severe enough that even Micron underestimated its own quarter.

💰 Capex guidance has been raised four times this year, but this is nothing like Meta's cash-flow-eating capex race. FY26 capex started the year guided at $18B, then climbed to $20B, then roughly $25B, and now lands around $27B — yet quarterly adjusted free cash flow still jumped to $18.3B (more than doubling QoQ from $6.9B), with operating cash flow of $25.4B. Capacity is being added faster while cash flow keeps accelerating — that's a sign supply can't keep up with demand, not a sign of cash burn.

📊 Despite the blowout, forward PE is just 7.5x — the market doesn't believe this margin level is permanent. That's the lowest in this peer table except SK hynix. The stock has already rallied hard, yet the multiple is compressing — the market is pricing this HBM/DRAM shortage as a cyclical peak, not a new normal.

1. Five quarters of revenue: from $11.3B to past $50B

Three of the last five quarters were "record" prints, and growth is accelerating, not fading — QoQ growth ran +21%, +75%, +74%, with guidance pointing to another +21% next quarter. This isn't a one-off inventory restock; it's a structural supply shortage in HBM/DRAM layered on top of pricing power.

2. Core numbers vs. the last two quarters

EPS is non-GAAP (core, excluding stock-based comp, restructuring, and other one-off items). FCF is the company's own reported adjusted free cash flow (excludes government incentive proceeds).

3. Guidance: last quarter's ceiling is this quarter's floor

FY26 full-year capex has moved through $18B → $20B → ~$25B → ~$27B — four upward revisions in one year, all in service of HBM supply and 1-gamma DRAM capacity. The Q4 guide itself ($50.0B revenue / $31.00 non-GAAP EPS) is already enormous, but as of September 21 Street consensus sits at $50.6B / $31.27 — the market expects even more than the company's own guide midpoint, which is unusual for a memory stock.

4. Valuation: is 7.5x forward PE "cheap," or a signal of a cycle top?

Forward PE of 7.5x (Futu, as of 2026-09-26), well below its own roughly 29x one-year average. Analyst consensus target is $1,501.94 (19 analysts, 94.7% buy / 5.3% hold / 0% sell) versus a current price of $1,082.28 — implying roughly +38.7% upside.

★ Read this cheapness carefully: a low forward PE means the market is treating the current 84%+ gross margin as a cyclical peak, not the new normal. Memory stocks have repeatedly shown their multiple compress hardest in the very quarter margins hit a new high, because the market is already pricing in mean reversion. What actually decides whether this stock is cheap is not the 7.5x number — it's how many more quarters the HBM supply gap lasts.

Group definition: names tied to this cycle's AI-server-driven DRAM / HBM / NAND memory shortage. Covers the three big DRAM+HBM makers (Micron / SK hynix / Samsung) and the NAND side (WDC / SanDisk / Kioxia); the shared thesis is that AI data centers have opened a structural memory supply gap, with prices and margins spiking together.

6. What to watch next quarter (falsifiable)

  • FY26 Q4 actual results (reporting 2026-09-30, four days after this note was compiled) — will they clear the $50.0B ± $1B revenue guide and $31.00 ± $1 non-GAAP EPS guide, let alone the more bullish Street consensus of $50.6B / $31.27? A miss here would be the first time in this supercycle that guidance wasn't easily cleared.
  • Whether FY27 capex guidance steps up again from the already four-times-revised ~$27B FY26 base, and whether adjusted FCF can still hold above roughly $15B/quarter — capex rising alongside accelerating FCF is the signal that supply still trails demand.
  • Whether sequential DRAM/HBM spot and contract price increases start to decelerate — the earliest leading indicator that this shortage cycle is entering its later stage.

7. The bull and bear case

The bull case: this AI memory shortage is structurally different from past PC/mobile inventory cycles — HBM capacity is gated by advanced packaging and yield, not something capex alone can fix quickly, and the industry consensus is that capacity is "booked through 2027." Forward PE of 7.5x sits well below its own average, and with 94.7% of analysts on buy and a target price implying nearly 40% upside, fundamentals and sentiment are pointing the same way right now.

The bear case: every past instance of memory gross margin hitting a new high has been followed by the market pre-pricing mean reversion, and four capex upgrades in one year means the entire industry — not just Micron — is racing to add supply. Supply-side responses in memory have historically moved faster than the market expects; once 2027 capacity starts ramping, the price turn could come faster than this rally did. The real disagreement is whether HBM's technical barriers genuinely slow the supply correction more than past DRAM cycles did.
