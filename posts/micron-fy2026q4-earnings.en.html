Micron．FY2026 Q4 Earnings, Unpacked

Revenue of $54.23B, up 30.8% QoQ and 379% YoY. Non-GAAP EPS of $33.42 vs a $31.27 consensus is a 6.9% beat, far smaller than last quarter's 23.8%.
Gross margin hit a record 87.0%, yet the Q1 guide of ~86.25% is the first dip of this cycle. Micron also disclosed 26 Strategic Customer Agreements and $32B of customer commitments for the first time. Built from the IR release, call transcript and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: Micron IR release + call transcript Period: 2026-05-29 – 2026-09-03 (FY26 Q4) Reported: 2026-09-30 after close Compiled: 2026-10-01

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

The beat is still there, but the blowout is gone. Revenue of $54.23B topped the pre-quarter guide midpoint ($50.0B) by 8.5% and the Futu consensus ($50.6B) by 7.2%; core EPS of $33.42 beat $31.27 by 6.9%. Last quarter's beat was 23.8%. FY26 was also a 53-week year, making this a 14-week quarter (my own calendar derivation; neither the release nor the call says so), so stripping the extra week, weekly revenue grew about 21.5% QoQ, not the headline 30.8%.

The first hint of a margin peak. Non-GAAP gross margin of 87.0% (84.9% last quarter) and operating margin of 82.3% (81.2%) were records, but the Q1 FY27 gross margin guide of about 86.25% is the first guide below the prior quarter's actual. The CFO called Q1 the "floor," attributing roughly $1B of higher cost to inventory absorbing FY26 incentive compensation, and management softened pricing language to "more moderate rate of price increases."

Visibility has moved from "shortage" to "contracts". 26 Strategic Customer Agreements covering "over 35% of our revenue through 2030," $32B of customer financial commitments (mostly cash deposits), and "more than 75%" of FY27 output already committed. Quarterly adjusted FCF was $33.2B (61.2% FCF margin), while FY27 first-half capex runs about $25B with the second half higher.

1. Quarterly revenue: growth persists, the slope changes

QoQ growth ran +75% (Q2), +74% (Q3), +30.8% (Q4), and a guided +13.4% at the midpoint for Q1 FY27. The slope is clearly flattening, but read it in two layers: (1) Q4 has an extra week and Q1 one fewer, so headline QoQ is inflated and then depressed by the calendar; (2) my own per-week estimate is about $3.19B/week in Q3, $3.87B/week in Q4 (+21.5%) and $4.73B/week implied by the Q1 guide (+22.1%) — on a per-week basis growth has barely decelerated. The week counts are my [derivation] from the fiscal year-end of 2026-09-03 and last quarter's 05-28; Micron did not mention them in the release or call, so confirm with the 10-K.

2. Core numbers: this quarter vs. last quarter vs. last year vs. expectations

EPS is non-GAAP (excludes SBC, patent license charges and loss on debt prepayment, about $0.55/share in total; GAAP EPS of $32.87 also beat, so the beat is not driven by one-offs). Consensus is Futu as of 2026-09-21 (from our prior note); guide is the company's 2026-06-24 guidance. FCF is the company's adjusted FCF; FCF margin = FCF / revenue (my calculation).

3. Segments: Core Data Center takes the growth, Cloud Memory margin moves the other way

Two things stand out: (1) Core Data Center added $6.5B in one quarter, about half of the $12.77B total increase; (2) Cloud Memory is the only unit whose operating margin fell (78% to 76%), and it also grew slowest (+18.3%), losing 3.2 points of share. The other three units' operating margins rose 2 to 4 points. The release gives no reason and the call did not ask — this is the one item this quarter with numbers but no explanation.

4. Guidance: revenue to new highs, but gross margin and opex deserve a closer look

FY26 full year: revenue $133.19B (FY25 $37.38B, +256%), operating cash flow $89.68B, net capex $27.37B (in line with the roughly $27B guided last quarter), adjusted FCF $62.31B. For FY27 capex the company only gave the first half (about $25B) and said the second half is higher, with no full-year total. Q1 non-GAAP opex guided at about $2.06B is roughly $0.5B below this quarter's $2.57B; I did not find an explanation for the gap (the extra week and incentive compensation played a role but were not quantified). Q1 FY27 consensus: not available from Futu.

5. Call takeaways

Transcript taken from the public versions on stockanalysis.com and Investing.com, cross-checked against the IR release. Our wiki had no prior [call] entries and the previous note did not use a transcript, so no like-for-like before/after quote comparison is possible this time; the lines below set the baseline for next quarter. Everything management says is [opinion].

New phrasing (baseline for next quarter)

  • Supply/demand: "memory and storage supply demand conditions to be much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than they were in 2026"; "we do not have line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance."
  • Pricing: "higher gross margins beyond fiscal Q1 for the remainder of fiscal 2027, with a more moderate rate of price increases" — the first time slowing price increases appear in the company's own wording, alongside "margins meaningfully above any prior cycle peak margins."
  • HBM: "calendar 2027 HBM prices are much higher than 2026 levels and that most of next year's HBM volume is already sold."

What analysts pressed on (5 questions, 3 themes)

  • Capital return vs. capex (UBS Arcuri, BofA Arya): how to allocate FCF. Management said it will "increase our capital return" from 2026-12-09 but gave no amount.
  • 2027-2028 pricing and HBM de-spec risk (Cantor Muse, BofA Arya, TD Cowen Sankar): management answered "greater tightness in the industry in 2027 and in 2028 versus 2026" and that HBM "continues to grow faster than conventional DRAM through 2028"; on de-spec, customers optimize to "ship more units," not to cut memory needs.
  • SCA coverage (JPMorgan Sur): more than 75% of FY27 output is already committed.

Where management dodged or stayed vague

  • Buyback size and target cash level: only "target cash level by end of fiscal Q1," no figure. Actual FY26 repurchases were just $650M (IR) against $62.31B of FCF.
  • Full-year FY27 capex: only Q1 of about $11.5B and H1 of about $25B; H2 "higher."
  • 2027 HBM price: only "much higher," no magnitude. HBM revenue only "grew faster than total company revenue," with no dollar amount or share.
  • Ultimate SCA penetration: kept the "around 50%" target; 26 signed agreements now cover over 35%, and management acknowledged it "could be less."

Numbers given only on the call

  • Data center SSD revenue of "nearly $10 billion," "more than 10 times the year ago quarter," over two-thirds of NAND revenue. (This is SSD, not HBM; the business unit was not stated and I do not assign it.)
  • Inventory of 129 days, up 9 days sequentially, attributed to "node end-of-life related build ahead."
  • Customer cash deposits of $12.7B at period end, $12.3B received in Q4, to be "returned to customers over time towards the latter half of each agreement's term" (the balance sheet's contract liabilities are $12.90B; different scope, not a contradiction).
  • SCAs: 26 agreements, over 35% of revenue through 2030, three quarters with a defined pricing framework and the rest negotiated periodically; customer financial commitments of $32B, mostly cash deposits; some customers asking to extend into 2031.
  • FY27: Q1 capex about $11.5B, H1 about $25B, tax rate about 15.5%; about $1B of higher Q1 cost from inventory absorbing incentive compensation and other factors; construction capex growing "meaningfully higher" than equipment capex.

6. Checking last quarter's "what to watch"

7. Valuation: about 6.1x forward PE, 0.4th percentile, provided consensus EPS holds

Forward PE of about 6.1x (Futu forecast valuation, queried 2026-10-01; 7.5x in the prior note), trailing 14.3x, one-year average 29.3x, one-year historical percentile 0.40%. My own cross-check: the $1,065.11 price (9/30 close; the post-earnings reaction was not yet available) divided by FY26 non-GAAP EPS of $75.52 gives 14.1x, consistent with Futu's trailing figure; 6.1x implies forward EPS of roughly $174 (my back-calculation), while the Q1 FY27 EPS guide of $38.15 annualizes to about $153, so consensus already assumes continued sequential growth. 23 analysts: 95.65% buy, 4.35% hold, 0% sell; average target $1,546.90 (high $2,200 / low $1,200), about +45% vs $1,065.11.

Per the invest-playbook: memory is a high-amplitude cycle, and a low forward PE often means the market is discounting margin mean reversion rather than genuine cheapness. This quarter adds evidence for "cycle amplitude is being dampened by contracts" (SCAs covering 35% of revenue through 2030, $32B of commitments) and one data point for "peaking" (Q1 gross margin guide dips, price increases turn "moderate"). This quarter's numbers cannot separate the two; the next two quarters' gross margins will, as to whether they rise as the CFO said.

9. Risks and the bear case

  • Pretty numbers, but the beat is shrinking: the EPS beat fell from +23.8% to +6.9%, and the headline +30.8% QoQ includes an extra week (derived). Guidance is less conservative now, so the era of easy beats may be ending.
  • First guided margin dip: Q1 gross margin of about 86.25% is below this quarter's 87.0%. If the CFO's "Q1 is the floor" fails, margins turning down would mark this cycle's inflection.
  • Inventory and capex: inventory days of 129, up 9, which management attributes to a build ahead of node end-of-life; FY27 H1 capex of $25B with H2 higher and construction growing fastest. That capacity eventually becomes supply, the bears' standard objection to 2028 and beyond (my inference; not how the company frames it).
  • Deposits are liabilities: $12.9B of contract liabilities are to be returned to customers in the later part of agreements; $32B of commitments is not revenue; 25% of SCA revenue is still "negotiated periodically."
  • Bull case: 75% of FY27 output committed, most 2027 HBM sold at higher prices, management saying 2027-2028 are tighter than 2026, plus a 61% FCF margin and about 6.1x forward PE — fundamentals and valuation point the same way. The disagreement is whether contracts really dampen the downside.

10. What to watch next quarter (falsifiable)

  • Whether FY27 Q1 actual non-GAAP gross margin (reported around December 2026) is at least 86.25%; anything below falsifies the CFO's "Q1 is the floor." The Q2 gross margin guide must also exceed 86.25%, otherwise "higher gross margins for the remainder of FY27" fails.
  • Whether Q1 revenue lands at or above the low end of $61.5B ± $1.5B (at least $60.0B) and EPS at least $38.15, and whether the beat margin returns to 10% or more (it was 6.9% this quarter).
  • Whether Q1 net capex is close to the roughly $11.5B guide, adjusted FCF holds above $30B per quarter, and the capital return announced from 12/9 exceeds the scale of FY26's $650M buybacks.
