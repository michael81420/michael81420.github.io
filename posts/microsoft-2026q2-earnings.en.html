TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

☁️ Azure's 43% is the fiscal year's best, and the acceleration is genuine. The four-quarter path reads 40% → 39% → 40% → 43%, and more importantly constant currency also came in at 43% — last quarter the headline was 40% against 39% CC, so FX was contributing; this quarter the two match, meaning no FX component. FY27 Q1 guidance goes further, to roughly 45% CC.

🧮 Unpack the beat: only $0.20 of the $0.60 surprise is operating. GAAP EPS of $4.81 against consensus of roughly $4.21 looks like a +14.3% beat. But the company's disclosed non-GAAP figure of $4.74 strips only OpenAI's $0.07 — it does not strip the newly disclosed Anthropic investment gain of $3.2B (about $0.33/share). Add both back and core EPS is roughly $4.41, narrowing the real beat to about +4.8%.

📉 Gross margin has fallen four quarters running — the number to remember from this print. 69.0% → 68.0% → 67.6% → 67.2%, down 1.4pp YoY. Operating margin held roughly flat (45.1% vs 44.9%) on expense leverage, but the depreciation pressure at the gross line is a deteriorating reported fact, not an opinion. Management waved it off on the call as "efficiency gains" without addressing it directly.

💧 Free cash flow is shrinking. FCF of $19.6B was down 23% YoY (from $25.6B), while cash capex rose 109% over the same period ($17.1B → $35.8B). GAAP net income grew strongly while the cash actually available to return or reinvest contracted — that gap is exactly why the numbers look good and the quarter feels worse.

★ One easily misread positive: the calendar-2026 capex outlook was "cut" to about $175B from $190B. That is not a spending reduction — it reflects extending the useful life of data centres and office buildings from 15 to 25 years, which reclassifies some future finance leases as operating leases (excluded from capex). The company stated explicitly that "outside of this useful-life impact, capex investment expectations remain unchanged." It looks like a positive and is functionally neutral. FY27 Q1 capex guidance is "over $50B," a step up from this quarter's $41B.

1. Unpacking EPS: only 20 cents of the 60-cent beat is operating

This deserves its own section because the company's own non-GAAP definition isn't clean. Microsoft's reported non-GAAP EPS of $4.74 strips only the $480M ($0.07/share) OpenAI equity-method item. But this quarter carried a newly disclosed Anthropic investment gain of $3.2B (about $0.33/share) — a figure given verbally on the call, absent from the release, and not stripped from non-GAAP.

Restore both and core EPS is roughly $4.41, a beat of about 4.8% against the $4.21 consensus. 4.8% is still a beat, and the core business is not the problem — but it is a different order of magnitude from the headline 14.3%.

📌 These equity-method gains are symmetric. When the underlying valuations give back, the same accounting produces a symmetric one-time loss. The OpenAI line has posted loss quarters before. In other words, some future quarter's GAAP EPS may be dragged down by the same mechanism — and it shouldn't be read as the core business deteriorating then either. Alphabet had the identical phenomenon this quarter ($6.26/share from Anthropic and SpaceX), and both should be measured with the same ruler.

2. The central tension: demand up, margin down

The two lines together are the whole story: the top one (demand) is rising, the bottom one (earnings quality) is falling — and both are continuous and directional, not single-quarter noise.

The good side: Azure's 43% is the fiscal year's single best quarter and above the ~40% CC implied by management's Q3 guidance. Commercial RPO reached $678B (+84% YoY); commercial bookings excluding OpenAI accelerated from +7% last quarter to +18%, meaning non-OpenAI baseline demand is strengthening too. Call language — "demand continues to exceed available capacity" — matches prior quarters, with no softening toward "capacity has caught up."

The bad side: gross margin at 69.0% → 68.0% → 67.6% → 67.2%, four straight declines, −1.4pp YoY. Operating margin holds at 45.1% (44.9% a year ago) because SG&A and R&D are growing more slowly than revenue — that is, expense leverage is patching the hole at the gross line. That patch has a limit: costs can't be compressed indefinitely, and the depreciation pressure on gross margin is still rising.

★ Analysts pushed on the drivers and CFO Amy Hood replied that efficiency gains are "quickly monetized in the quarter"; but the fact of four consecutive gross-margin declines got no direct quantified response and no trough timing. This is one of the clearest dodges on the call.

3. Cash flow: capex +109%, FCF −23%

Operating cash flow itself is strong: $55.4B, +30% YoY. The problem is that capex ran faster — cash capex went from $19.4B to $35.8B, +109% YoY; including finance leases the figure is $41B (+69% YoY), consistent with the company's earlier "over $40B" guide.

Net them and FCF of $19.6B is 23% below the $25.6B of a year ago. Net income rising while cash generation shrinks is the gap that makes a good-looking print feel bad. And next quarter's capex guide is "over $50B" — another step up from this quarter's $41B.

★ On that "calendar-2026 capex cut from $190B to $175B": it is purely an accounting change. Extending the useful life of data centres and office buildings from 15 to 25 years reclassifies some future finance leases as operating leases, which are excluded from capex. The company stated explicitly that outside of the useful-life impact, capex expectations are unchanged. Reading it as a spending cut is a misjudgment.

4. The three segments: only cloud is accelerating

① Intelligent Cloud is the only accelerating segment: +28% → +29% → not disclosed → +32%, driven by Azure at 43%. Microsoft Cloud revenue was $59.3B, +27% YoY.

② P&BP (productivity software) is decelerating gently: 17% → 16% → 17% → 14%, the slowest of the fiscal year. The Copilot seat surge (below) did not fully offset it — meaning Copilot is still additive rather than the primary engine.

③ MPC (personal computing) has weakened four quarters running: +4% → −3% → −1% → −4%, on soft Windows OEM and device demand. FY27 Q1 guidance still implies a "low 20s decline" for OEM, so there is no near-term improvement in sight.

The call also revealed a new per-seat-plus-consumption pricing model — a shift from pure seat licensing toward usage-based billing. That's a familiar monetisation path for a cloud vendor, and it means Copilot's revenue model is moving from "selling licences" to "selling usage."

Nadella's answer on model strategy is worth recording: he emphasised a multi-model architecture and that "every model is substitutable," directly addressing concerns about over-dependence on OpenAI. That framing helps Microsoft, but it simultaneously erodes the narrative value of OpenAI exclusivity — it cuts both ways.

6. Guidance: FY27 full-year capex refused a number

Every FY27 Q1 segment got a specific range. Only full-year capex got the qualitative "will grow year-over-year," with no dollar amount or growth rate. The market is left with analyst estimates (around $220B, not a company figure).

This is the clearest dodge on the call. In an environment where the market is actively repricing debt-funded AI capex, refusing to quantify capital intensity is itself information — either the number isn't set, or it's large enough that they'd rather not say it first.

7. Valuation: a 44 percentile, the highest in the peer group

📊 Forward PE 23.5x, at the 44.0 percentile of its one-year range (one-year mean 30.16x); forward PS 8.8x at the 48.4 percentile.

Price action: from a October 2025 high of about $538 to a July 2026 low of about $390 (−28%, reflecting persistent worry about margin and capex), then +19% across the two sessions after earnings (7/30–7/31, $390.54 → $464.72).

⚠️ The problem only shows up in cross-section: GOOGL sits at the 3.2 percentile, AMZN 0.8%, META 5.2%, NVDA 6.4%, ORCL 5.2% — peers are almost uniformly in single digits, and MSFT's 44% stands out.

Two readings: ① the market's confidence in this print has repaired ahead of peers, a quality premium; or ② MSFT simply hasn't yet been through the deep de-rate the rest of the group has, meaning its relative position is "not yet" rather than "not going to." Much of this rally is MSFT outperforming its peers, which is worth watching for over-extension.

Cycle intensity (invest-playbook): the AI cloud infrastructure cycle is mid-phase — demand clearly expanding, supply still catching up — not mature. MSFT's forward PE of 23.5x sits below its own one-year mean of 30.2x, so the post-earnings move is a repair re-rating rather than a new-high premium, with no overheating signal. But that is versus itself; versus peers it is the most expensive name on the board.

8. Peer group scan

Group definition: hyperscale cloud plus enterprise-software AI monetisation. Covers the three major public clouds (Azure / AWS / GCP), cloud challengers, and the enterprise-software names monetising via AI agents. The shared feature is rapidly rising capital intensity alongside AI monetisation through cloud subscriptions or software seats.

Two observations:

① The growth ranking and the valuation ranking of the three clouds are inverted. Growth: GCP (+82%) > Azure (43%) > AWS (+37%). Valuation percentile: AMZN (0.8%) < GOOGL (3.2%) < MSFT (44%). The fastest grower has the lowest percentile; the middle grower has the highest — the dislocation is worth noting on its own.

② ORCL is the best control case. Same RPO-explosion story (+363%, $638B), but because capex overshot its own guide, FCF turned negative and equity issuance was announced, the stock fell 35% after reporting. It demonstrates that a large RPO cannot rescue a multiple by itself — the balance sheet has to hold — which is precisely where MSFT is comparatively solid.

10. Both sides of the argument

Three reasons to be constructive:

① Azure's 43% is a real acceleration, with CC matching the headline (last quarter CC was below it) — no FX component. RPO of $678B (+84% YoY); bookings ex-OpenAI accelerating from +7% to +18%, so baseline demand is strengthening too. The supply gap shows no sign of easing.

② Copilot monetisation has hard numbers. Seats 15M → 20M → 30M, GitHub Copilot revenue +60% QoQ, Foundry at 100,000 customers. Amid widespread "when does AI make money" scepticism, this is one of the few companies producing verifiable figures.

③ The balance sheet is the strongest in the group. Against ORCL's capex overrun plus negative FCF plus equity issuance, Microsoft's FCF — though down YoY — remains positive at $19.6B, with no need to dilute shareholders.

Three reasons to be cautious:

① Four straight gross-margin declines with no trough given. 69.0% → 67.2%, −1.4pp YoY; operating margin holds only on expense leverage, and expenses can't be compressed forever. FCF is down 23% YoY, so cash generation is deteriorating.

② The quality of the EPS beat is questionable. Restoring Anthropic and OpenAI leaves a core beat of roughly +4.8%. These equity-method gains are volatile and reverse; they may become a drag.

③ The valuation percentile is the highest among peers. 44% against single digits elsewhere. MPC has weakened four quarters with no improvement; FY27 full-year capex was refused a number, so doubts about capital intensity and ROI remain unanswered. In a group where multiples are being compressed broadly, the most expensive name carries the most de-rating risk.
