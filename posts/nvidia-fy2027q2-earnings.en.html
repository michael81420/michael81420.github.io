NVIDIA．FY2027 Q2 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of $96.2B, +106% YoY, crushing the $92.17B consensus — on the surface, another flawless print.
The real story is cash flow: net income of $59.7B against operating cash flow of just $24.1B, a gap that jumped from $8.0B last quarter to $35.6B. Built from the IR release and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: NVIDIA IR release + CFO Commentary Period: 2026-04-27 – 2026-07-26 Reported: 2026-08-26 after close Compiled: 2026-09-26

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

💧 The gap between net income and operating cash flow widened 4.5x in a single quarter — that matters more than the revenue number. GAAP net income was $59.7B (+126% YoY), but operating cash flow was only $24.1B (−52.1% QoQ). Last quarter (Q1 FY27), net income was $58.3B and OCF was $50.3B — a gap of just $8.0B. This quarter the gap widened to $35.6B. The main driver: days sales outstanding (DSO) stretched from 45 to 60 days — more of the revenue is being generated by extending customers' payment terms.

📈 Revenue and earnings were, once again, a textbook beat. Revenue of $96.2B beat the $92.17B consensus (+4.4%); non-GAAP EPS of $2.22 beat the ~$2.09 estimate (+6.3%). Data Center revenue was $89.0B (+117% YoY), 92.5% of total revenue. Q3 guidance of $108B also came in above the analyst estimate of $104.2B.

📦 Inventory is building too, ahead of the next product cycle. Inventory rose from $25.8B to $31.6B (+$5.78B), which management attributed to stocking up for the full-production ramp of Vera Rubin in Q3. That's not necessarily bad, but it means capital will get locked into inventory and receivables even faster from Q3 — whether cash conversion recovers is the first thing to check next quarter.

★ On valuation: forward PE is only 18.3x, the 6.0 percentile of its one-year range — the stock and earnings both hit records, yet the PE sits near a historical low, suggesting the market isn't pricing "how long can this growth last" aggressively.

1. The cash truth: record profit, but cash-conversion quality is slipping

Breaking the gap down: accounts receivable rose $22.35B (DSO went from 45 to 60 days — management said this reflects more generous payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers); inventory rose $5.78B (from $25.8B to $31.6B, stocking up for Vera Rubin's Q3 production ramp); prepaid and other assets rose $5.50B. Together, roughly $35.9B, which accounts for almost the entire gap between net income and OCF.

Quarterly capex was only $2.677B (+52.4% QoQ), so free cash flow was still a healthy $21.34B — this isn't a company burning cash, it's one where a growing share of reported profit is getting locked up in receivables and inventory. That's a different mechanism from Meta's capex eating cash flow directly, but it's worth watching just as closely: a growing slice of revenue growth is being financed by looser credit terms to customers.

2. Guidance: $108B, well above estimates, as Vera Rubin ramps

Q3 FY27 guidance calls for revenue of $108B ±2%, up 12% QoQ from this quarter, and above the analyst estimate of $104.2B. Gross margin guidance (GAAP and non-GAAP) is 74% ±50bps, slightly below this quarter's 75.0% — management itself acknowledged that ramping a new product (Vera Rubin) dilutes margin early on.

CEO Jensen Huang said on the call that the AI infrastructure build-out is "at full steam," Vera Rubin is now in full production, and referenced ecosystem partners collectively mobilizing over $500B of third-party capital for AI infrastructure. Media reports also cited a verbal outlook of roughly 70% revenue growth for FY2028 — that's call commentary, not formal guidance, and should be read as such.

3. Valuation: the stock hit a record, yet forward PE sits near a historical low

📊 Trailing PE 28.5x, forward PE 18.3x, 1-year percentile just 6.0% (current price $225.07). Earnings are growing faster than the share price, which is why forward PE has actually fallen — the opposite of what most people assume about "AI bubble" names.

Analyst consensus (Futu, updated 2026-09-25): 31 analysts, 100% buy, average price target $324.32 (high $465 / low $275), implying roughly +44% from the current price. That unanimous buy rating is itself worth noting — it means there's very little dispersion of opinion among institutions, which leaves more room for downgrades if any negative signal appears.

Cycle-intensity read (invest-playbook): AI data-center compute is in a high-intensity expansion phase — capex, capacity and demand are all rising together, with no sign yet of any of the three peaking. A low forward PE is normal at this stage, but it also means the valuation is highly sensitive to whether demand keeps holding up.

4. Peer-group scan

Group definition: AI data-center compute silicon. NVDA is the outright leader in GPU/AI accelerators. The group includes its only comparable GPU/CPU rival AMD, custom-ASIC and networking-silicon players AVGO/MRVL, the foundry that makes everyone's chips (TSM), and the tightest supply-chain bottleneck of all — HBM memory supplier MU. All of these names ride the same AI capex cycle.

Side by side, NVDA (6.0%) and AVGO (2.4%) sit at the lowest valuation percentiles on the table, yet both are accelerating — suggesting the market hasn't fully priced in how long this growth phase can run. By contrast, MRVL (91.2%) and AMD (84.8%) are already at historically high percentiles, leaving far less room for error if their outlooks disappoint.

6. Bull and bear cases

The bull case:

① Demand looks broad-based, not single-customer. Q3 guidance of $108B beat the analyst estimate of $104.2B, and ecosystem partners have collectively pledged over $500B of third-party capital for AI infrastructure. ② Valuation sits at a 6.0 percentile — a historical low. Earnings are growing faster than the stock, so forward PE has actually come down. ③ Vera Rubin is already in full production, meaning the next product cycle has already started — there's no need to wait until next year for a new catalyst.

The bear case:

① Cash-conversion quality has clearly deteriorated. GAAP net income was $59.7B, but OCF was only $24.1B; the gap comes mainly from looser customer payment terms (DSO 45→60 days) and inventory build. That's revenue growth being financed, in part, by looser credit. ② The $2.7B of quarterly capex is only NVIDIA's own spending — the real risk is concentrated in the hundreds of billions of dollars of capex commitments from its cloud and AI customers, which sit entirely outside NVIDIA's own financials but directly determine whether this growth rate can hold. ③ The Q3 guide of $108B is already a high bar; if sequential growth slows meaningfully from this quarter's +18% QoQ, the stock is likely to be very sensitive to that deceleration.

★ The real disagreement is about how you read the simultaneous build-up in receivables and inventory. Bulls see it as normal during an AI infrastructure expansion; bears see it as an early sign that revenue quality is starting to weaken. Next quarter should make this much clearer.
