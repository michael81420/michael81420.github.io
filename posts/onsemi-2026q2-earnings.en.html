TL;DR · What Actually Mattered

🎭 The "beat" is a Non-GAAP beat; on a full GAAP basis this was a miss. Non-GAAP EPS $0.74 vs. $0.71 consensus (+4.2%) sent the stock up after hours. But GAAP diluted EPS was $0.56 — below the $0.60–0.72 floor the company guided to in its Q1 release. The gap is restructuring and acquisition costs of $24.9M, asset impairments of $16.3M, and other net expense of −$32.6M, all excluded from Non-GAAP. FabRight is still running, so this gap won't close soon.

🔥 The first hard numbers on capacity tightness showed up. Utilisation jumped 77% → 83% (+6pp) in a single quarter, lead times stretched from 27 to 32 weeks, and management announced a second round of price increases to cover input and foundry costs. They also volunteered a conversion factor on the call: each 1pp of utilisation is worth roughly 25–30bp of gross margin. These are the concrete signals of power semis flipping from destocking to tight capacity — not adjectives.

✂️ But none of that pricing has reached this quarter's margin yet. Q2 GAAP gross margin was 38.4%, essentially flat against 38.5% last quarter. The actual proof point is the Q3 guide of 40–42% Non-GAAP gross margin — a full step above this quarter's 39.3%. The entire bull case rests on that range, and it gets tested in three months.

⚡ The recovery is happening in power only — it isn't broad. PSG (Power Solutions, including SiC/GaN) grew +19% YoY and +13% QoQ, carrying the whole company. AMG (Analog and Mixed-Signal) was −2% YoY and just +1% QoQ — the only shrinking segment. Automotive was still −2% QoQ. Narrow breadth means that if the AI data centre leg cools, no second segment catches the fall.

★ Ignore the trailing P/E of 53x at the 85th percentile — that's a recovery-phase denominator distortion. Use forward P/E of 22.5x against a five-year mean of 23.1x: it's sitting right on its own average. Not expensive, but not cheap enough to be paid for taking cyclical risk either.

1. Take the beat apart first

The $0.18 per share difference breaks into three pieces: restructuring and acquisition costs of $24.9M (the FabRight fab-lite transition is still under way), asset impairments of $16.3M, and other net expense of −$32.6M. All excluded from Non-GAAP.

Two cases to distinguish here. If these are genuinely the tail of a one-off restructuring, then Non-GAAP is the better read on operations. But FabRight's savings don't start showing up until 2027 (~$35M a year, full run-rate in 2028), which means the expense side has several more quarters to run. "One-time" charges that recur for several quarters aren't one-time.

📌 The sharper point: the company missed a range it set for itself three months ago. Guidance is the company's forecast of its own cost structure. Missing analysts means outsiders got it wrong; missing your own guide means you got your own P&L wrong. Those are not the same event.

2. Core numbers and the eight-quarter shape

Revenue was $1,603.5M (+9.2% YoY, +6.0% QoQ), modestly ahead of the ~$1.59B consensus. Inventory improved across the board: distributor inventory 10.8 → 10.1 weeks, total inventory days 201 → 192. Free cash flow of $425.4M was nearly 4x the year-ago figure.

4. The tightness signal fired — the margin hasn't

All three of these came only from the call, not the press release: utilisation 77% → 83%, lead times 27 → 32 weeks, and roughly 25–30bp of gross margin per 1pp of utilisation. That last one is the most actionable sentence of the quarter — on its own arithmetic, +6pp of utilisation should be worth 150–180bp of gross margin.

Yet Q2 GAAP gross margin was 38.4% against 38.5% last quarter — essentially unchanged. Neither the utilisation benefit nor the pricing benefit has hit the P&L. The Q3 Non-GAAP gross margin guide of 40.0–42.0% (vs. 39.3% actual this quarter) is the first proof point.

📌 This is the sentence in this post most worth trying to falsify. Management says pricing shows up in Q3–Q4. That claim has been written down as an explicit numeric range (40–42%), and three months from now we'll know. If Q3 lands below 40%, the "tight capacity" narrative itself needs re-rating — because all the signals were there and none of them reached margin.

5. Guidance: raised across the board

Two wording changes worth logging:

① The full-year AI data centre outlook was upgraded from "double" to "more than double" (off a 2025 base of roughly $250M). Management's phrasing: it "remains our fastest-growing business."

② SiC was broken into two separately reported curves for the first time: China automotive SiC +60–70% YoY for the year, AI data centre SiC close to +60% YoY. Previous quarters just said "SiC growth." Splitting out growth sources usually has one of two motives — genuine diversification, or a need to prove you aren't dependent on one market. This quarter reads as the former, but watch whether the split changes again.

7. Valuation: place the cycle first, then talk P/E

Cycle amplitude: high. Power semis are a deep cycle. Gross margin went from 45.4% in FY24Q3 to 20.3% in FY25Q1 — 25 percentage points in eighteen months. That amplitude is exactly why trailing P/E is close to useless in this part of the chain.

Where it sits now: revenue of $1,603.5M is still about 9% below the $1,761.9M prior peak; gross margin of 38.4% is about 7pp below the 45%+ it ran then; and 83% utilisation isn't historically full either. That reads as mid-recovery — the trough is confirmed behind us, but the late-cycle overheating zone is still some distance away.

Forward P/E 22.5x (Futu, five-year window) against its own five-year mean of 23.1x — essentially on the average. The trailing 53.05x at the 85th percentile is a distorted denominator; don't use it. Futu's own valuation model concludes "slightly overvalued: market cap has grown a little faster than earnings."

The price reaction is worth logging: the stock had already fallen from ~$89 to ~$80 in the week before the print (tech sold off broadly over the same stretch), spiked to $84.51 after hours on the day, then closed the next session at $81.23 — the good news didn't hold. Coverage is 21 analysts, 52.4% Buy / 47.6% Hold / 0% Sell, average target $112.29 (high $150 / low $90), implying about +38%. But most of those targets were set before the print; watch for revisions.

8. Peer group scan

Group definition: automotive and industrial power semiconductors plus SiC/GaN wide-bandgap. It covers discrete power devices (MOSFET / IGBT / SiC MOSFET), power modules, and high-voltage analog and driver ICs. End markets concentrate in EV inverters and on-board chargers, industrial automation, and the newer 800V AI server power architecture.

Three observations:

① The valuation tiers inside this group are unusually clean. The IDM leaders (ON 22.5x / STM 28.7x / Infineon 30–34x) sit well below the niche and thematic names (ALGM 71.6x / POWI 69.1x / VICR 48.2x). ON has the lowest forward P/E in the table — and also the narrowest recovery breadth (AMG still shrinking).

② The pure thematic names aren't comparable on fundamentals. WOLF and NVTS both carry negative forward P/Es, meaning the market expects continued losses. They have the highest SiC/GaN purity but function as options, not positions — putting them in the same valuation frame as ON is a category error.

③ The Taiwan names are a different world. Mospec, Uni-Calsonic, APEC and Episil sit at the tail of the chain as pure shortage-and-pricing plays, on trailing P/Es of 56–144x, with valuation long since decoupled from monthly revenue growth. Listed here for contrast only; don't compare them directly with an IDM that owns its fabs.

★ Infineon: Futu doesn't quote the US OTC line (IFNNY), so it needs ETR:IFX. Web sources disagree badly on its forward P/E (24.36x / 33.52x / 34.70x) and this page could not cross-verify a single credible figure — check a European data source before using it. The Taiwan P/Es are prior figures, not re-verified for this post.

10. Risks and the other side

1. The GAAP-to-Non-GAAP gap may widen further. FabRight is still running and the charges have several quarters left. Trust in the "beat" erodes — especially when what's being missed is the company's own guide.

2. Recovery breadth is insufficient. AMG weak two quarters running, automotive still negative QoQ, and the entire growth line resting on PSG (SiC plus AI data centre power). If the AI leg cools, no second segment catches it.

3. Whether pricing converts is still an assumption. The Q3 margin guide is built on "pricing shows up," which is the company's own forecast. Automotive customers have long contracts and real leverage, and management gave no magnitude on pass-through.

4. The stock barely responded to good news. It failed to hold the day's intraday high the following session. That could be macro flows overwhelming a single stock's print — or the market seeing through to point 1. Verify those separately rather than assuming the former.

5. Chinese SiC competition is a blank on the call. The company reported its own China growth without discussing what domestic capacity does to pricing. Discount asymmetric information.

The bull case: "+6pp utilisation, +5 weeks of lead time, and a second price increase is a textbook upward inflection in a power semi cycle, and margin lagging a quarter or two is normal, not a problem"; plus "forward P/E on the mean, for a deep cyclical just off the trough, with mid-recovery earnings — that's positive odds." Both hold. The disagreement is only whether you believe that Q3 40–42%. It's the shared pivot of every bull and bear argument here.
