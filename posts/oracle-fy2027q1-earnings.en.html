Oracle．FY2027 Q1 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue grew 30%, above the top of its own 27–29% guide; OCI grew 121%; non-GAAP EPS of $1.92 beat by a wide margin. The stock was up as much as 8.5% intraday — and closed down 1.7%.
Of last quarter's three watch items, the demand test passed and the margin test failed. The money problem is still there, just in a different shape. Built from the IR release, call highlights and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: Oracle IR release + earnings call highlights Period: 2026-06-01 – 2026-08-31 (FY27 Q1) Reported: 2026-09-10 Compiled: 2026-09-17

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 RPO is converting faster than last quarter's worry implied. Revenue was $19.35B, +29.6% YoY, clearing the company's own 27–29% range. OCI hit $7.4B, +121% YoY (last quarter +93%), on 300,000+ GPUs delivered in the quarter (about 3× Q4) and 850MW of new capacity. Last quarter's "the backlog takes too long to convert" concern got an answer in actual revenue.

💸 The cash gap narrowed, but it did not close. Quarterly capex was $28.5B (Q4: $16.5B). Operating cash flow hit a record $23.1B (helped by customer prepayments), yet FCF was still −$5.4B, with no timeline for turning positive. Full-year capex guidance of $90–95B (net cash ≤ $70B) sat far above the roughly $68B the market had in mind — the main reason the stock closed red.

📉 Gross margin is down nine quarters in a row, and the decline is accelerating. TTM gross margin went 65.8% → 64.0% (−1.9pp, versus roughly −1.3pp per quarter before). Single-quarter gross margin was just 60.0%, against 67.3% a year ago. The falsification test set last quarter — "breaks below 65% with no sign of recovery" — has been triggered.

🎯 Roughly half of the $664B RPO is tied to OpenAI. RPO rose $26B in the quarter, +46% YoY (last quarter +363% was a base effect), and management now says "about half converts within 36 months." The good news: non-OpenAI backlog doubled in a year. The bad news: single-customer concentration is now the largest non-financial risk in the story.

★ Compared with last quarter, the shape of the story is the same but the centre of gravity moved. Last quarter the market doubted whether orders would become revenue; this quarter revenue answered that. The new doubt is whether margins and cash flow can keep up once they do. The $20B ATM issuance is done and interest-bearing borrowings fell from $129.5B to $125.3B — the balance sheet actually improved this quarter.

One pass, one fail, one neutral. That mix explains the price action well: revenue good enough to gap up 7.5% at the open, margins and capex bad enough to close red.

★ The dilution figure in ③ is backed out from GAAP diluted EPS (net income to common ÷ diluted EPS: $4.68B ÷ $1.56 ≈ 3.00B shares, versus $4.22B ÷ $1.45 ≈ 2.91B last quarter). It is not a company disclosure — treat it as an order of magnitude only.

2. $664B of RPO: growth normalises, concentration surfaces

Quarterly RPO growth dropped from $85B last quarter to $26B, and YoY fell from +363% to +46%. The YoY slide is mostly a base effect (FY26 Q1 was the quarter the big OpenAI deal landed), not a demand slowdown — Oracle still booked $30B+ of new AI contracts, and management stressed that most were structured with customer prepayments or bring-your-own-hardware, requiring no additional Oracle capital.

The conversion language moved from "12% within 12 months, 34% in months 13–36" to "about half within 36 months" — roughly 46% to roughly 50%. The 12-month figure was not disclosed this time; that number is worth chasing next quarter.

📌 Concentration is the new storyline. Outside analysis puts roughly half of the $664B as OpenAI-related. Non-OpenAI backlog doubling in a year is real diversification progress, but any change in OpenAI's funding or demand would take out a large piece of Oracle's RPO in one go — a risk the headline number does not show.

3. Cash flow: the gap narrowed, but the capex guide spooked the market

Operating cash flow was $23.1B, +184% YoY — Oracle's best quarter ever — but it includes large customer prepayments, a product of contract structure rather than purely earned cash. Capex was $28.5B, leaving FCF at −$5.4B, the sixth negative quarter in a row.

Better than last quarter: ① net cash capex after prepayments was only about $18B; ② the $20B ATM is done, and interest-bearing borrowings (short-term $7.6B + long-term $117.7B) fell from $129.5B to $125.3B — last quarter's "no new debt" pledge is holding so far; ③ cash plus short-term investments ended at $37.1B.

Worse than last quarter: full-year capex guidance of $90–95B (net cash no more than $70B) versus a market expectation of roughly $68B. Interest expense was $1.43B, +55% YoY. Management gave no timeline for positive FCF, saying only that each project delivers "roughly 100% free cash flow conversion" once ramped.

4. Gross margin: nine straight quarters down, and falling faster

TTM gross margin went 65.8% → 64.0%, with the quarterly step widening from about 1.3pp to 1.9pp. The single quarter is starker: $11.6B gross profit ÷ $19.35B revenue = 60.0%, versus 65.2% last quarter and 67.3% a year ago. Revenue grew 30% but gross profit only 16% — every extra dollar of OCI earns far less gross profit than the legacy business.

Management's explanation is unchanged: pressure comes from the data-centre ramp (costs land before full contracted revenue), and margins stabilise once the ramp is done. They added that with supply tight, "prices go up rather than down," and renewed GPU capacity priced 20% above prior contracts — the only concrete evidence this quarter for the "dip now, recover later" story.

Operating margin held, though: non-GAAP operating margin was 42%, flat YoY, thanks to lower R&D (15.3% → 14.2% of revenue) and sales expense (12.4% → 11.3%). Covering a gross-margin gap with expense discipline only goes so far.

5. Core numbers and segment detail

Revenue of $19.35B beat consensus of about $19.14B by 1.1%. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.92 beat the $1.74 consensus by 10% and cleared the company's own $1.72–$1.76 guide. The release lists no one-off investment gains like last quarter's Ampere / Bloom items, so the home-page table uses $1.92 on a non-GAAP basis.

Five-quarter segment series:

① OCI at $7.4B is now more than 70% larger than Cloud Apps at $4.2B — the biggest cloud segment. Multicloud Database revenue grew 353% YoY; GPU utilisation was 97.9%.

② SaaS and licences still aren't moving. Cloud Apps +10% (Fusion +14%, industry apps +20%+), Software −3%; the call flagged slower NetSuite decision cycles. All the growth still rides on OCI alone.

③ Software dropping from $6.82B to $5.5B is seasonal (Q4 is the big licence quarter), not a new problem — the −3% YoY is the number to watch.

6. Guidance: revenue and EPS raised again, capex raised with them

FY27 revenue of "at least $90B" (+34%) was reiterated; non-GAAP EPS nudged up from $8.05 to $8.10 (+19%). Q2 revenue +30–34% and cloud +65–71% mean OCI keeps accelerating for at least one more quarter.

Two new, concrete execution-risk signals: ① last quarter pointed to "close to 1GW" of Q1 deliveries; the actual was 850MW, a bit short; ② the call acknowledged delays at data centres in New Mexico and Wisconsin, which management says won't affect FY27. Neither is large, but they point the same way: building a little slower than promised.

7. Valuation: 19.6x forward, 6.8 percentile — and NVDA is now cheaper

📉 Forward PE 19.6x, 6.8th percentile of its five-year history (five-year mean 34.7x, mean −1σ 24.7x — still below the band). Forward PS 4.39x, 39.4th percentile. The stock closed at $143.16 on 2026-09-16, about 10% above the $129.87 at the last note, but about 6% below the pre-earnings close (~$153).

The gap between the PS percentile (39.4%) and the PE percentile (6.8%) is wider than last quarter (29.4% vs 9.1%). Same message, louder: the market will pay for revenue, not for earnings — and this quarter's accelerating margin decline is exactly what that pricing expects.

Cycle intensity (invest-playbook): AI compute demand is still in expansion (97.9% GPU utilisation, renewals +20%, Q2 cloud guide +65–71%). Oracle's own funding cycle eased a little (issuance done, borrowings down, more prepayment structures), but $90–95B of capex means next year's cash gap will get scrutinised all over again.

⚠️ Last quarter's "same multiple as NVDA" question has flipped: NVDA's forward PE is now 17.4x, below ORCL's 19.6x. One is a cash machine with no dilution pressure; the other has negative FCF and just issued $20B of stock — and the latter is the pricier one. Unless you believe Oracle's EPS growth slope will clearly beat NVDA's, that relative pricing deserves scepticism.

Consensus: 29 analysts, 86.2% Buy (last quarter: 31 analysts, 90.3%), average target $251.77 (high $400 / low $145), down from $262.25. Both coverage and the Buy share slipped slightly.

8. Peer group scan

Group definition: AI cloud infrastructure / hyperscale data-centre capex. Companies that spend heavily to build GPU data centres and rent compute to enterprises and AI labs, sharing the triple pressure of capex intensity, free cash flow and debt/dilution. Extends upstream to GPU suppliers and sideways to data-centre power and cooling.

Two observations:

① Valuation percentiles are low across the board, for different reasons. GOOGL (2.0%), NVDA (0.3%), CRWV (PS 3.2%) and ORCL (6.8%) all sit near five-year lows. But GOOGL and NVDA forward PEs were pushed down by earnings upgrades, while ORCL and CRWV were knocked down by capital-structure doubts. The same low percentile means different things.

② AMD is still the only name with a high percentile (68.2%, 58x forward), and VRT stays mid-range (31.3%). The market trusts the shovel sellers more than the miners — unchanged from last quarter.

10. Both sides of the argument

Three reasons to be bullish:

① Orders are turning into revenue, faster than expected. Revenue +30% beat its own top end, OCI +121%, 300k GPUs delivered in one quarter, and Q2 cloud is guided +65–71%. Last quarter's biggest question — "RPO converts too slowly" — now has a real answer.

② The capital structure improved this quarter. The $20B issuance is done, borrowings fell $4.2B, prepayments cut net cash capex to about $18B, and $30B+ of new contracts need no extra capital. Renewals at +20% show pricing power is on Oracle's side.

③ Valuation is still at the 6.8th five-year percentile; 19.6x forward against guidance of +19% EPS and +34% revenue is a PEG of barely 1.0.

Three reasons to be bearish:

① Gross margin has no floor and is falling faster. 60% in the quarter, TTM down nine quarters with a widening step; +30% revenue bought only +16% gross profit. "Stabilises after the ramp" comes with no date and no number.

② Capex stepped up another notch. FY27 at $90–95B versus the roughly $68B the market expected; FCF negative for six quarters with no timeline; interest expense +55% YoY. Prepayments flatter cash flow, but that is customer money to be repaid in service.

③ Single-customer concentration. About half of the $664B RPO is OpenAI-related; add data-centre delays and slightly light deliveries, and the "nobody disputes demand" consensus is starting to crack.

★ Where the debate sits: last quarter it was "can the balance sheet hold?" — and this quarter it did. The new debate is unit economics. Bulls say margin pressure is a ramp-phase effect and the +20% renewals prove it; bears say a 60% quarterly gross margin plus $90B+ of capex means ROIC may never return to old-Oracle levels. Next quarter's gross margin is the most direct test.
