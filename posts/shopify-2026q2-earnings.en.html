Shopify．Q2 2026 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

GMV, revenue, gross profit and free cash flow all grew more than 30% together. Adjusted EPS of $0.42 beat estimates, and the stock jumped over 25% premarket and closed up 17%.
But underneath, gross margin slipped 0.9pp YoY, and GAAP EPS swung wildly on equity-investment fair-value gains — not a number to read as core earnings. Built from the IR release and Futu data. Not investment advice.

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 Four key metrics grew 30%+ together — a rare, broad-based print. GMV of $115.567B (+32% YoY), revenue of $3.583B (+34% YoY), gross profit of $1.708B (+31% YoY), and free cash flow of $654M (+55% YoY, 18% margin). Management itself called it a "monster quarter," and the stock jumped over 25% in a day.

📉 Gross margin actually slipped 0.9pp YoY, because the revenue mix is shifting. Gross margin was 47.7% vs 48.6% a year ago and 48.9% last quarter. The reason: Merchant Solutions revenue (which includes Shopify Payments and carries a lower margin) grew +37.4% YoY, notably faster than Subscription Solutions (the higher-margin line), which grew +22.3% YoY. Merchant's share of total revenue rose from 75.5% to 77.6%. Revenue growth looks great, but "revenue quality" is drifting toward the lower-margin side.

🎢 GAAP EPS swung wildly across the last two quarters — don't read it as core earnings. Last quarter (Q1 2026) GAAP EPS was a loss of $0.45; this quarter it's a gain of $1.16 (net income of $1.5B vs $906M a year ago). That swing from a loss to a large gain is mostly driven by fair-value changes in Shopify's equity investment holdings, not by the core business. The number to actually anchor on is adjusted EPS: $0.42 this quarter, against a $0.40 estimate and $0.35 a year ago.

★ GAAP operating margin, by contrast, has climbed steadily: 10.9% (a year ago) → 12.1% (last quarter) → 13.6% (this quarter), expanding for a second straight quarter. That line reflects the actual earning power of the core business far better than GAAP EPS does.

1. The core numbers: broad growth, but the margin mix is shifting

By segment, Subscription Solutions revenue was $802M (+22.3% YoY) and Merchant Solutions revenue was $2,781M (+37.4% YoY). Merchant's share of total revenue rose from 75.5% a year ago to 77.6% — the direct cause of the gross margin pressure. That isn't necessarily bad news: it means actual transaction volume flowing through the Shopify platform (including Shopify Payments) is growing faster than subscription fees, it just happens to convert at a lower blended margin.

★ Capex was just $4M. Cash and equivalents of $1.656B plus marketable securities of $3.291B add up to roughly $4.95B in total liquidity — the capital structure of a typical asset-light SaaS e-commerce platform.

2. Guidance: same pace next quarter, no sign of slowing

Q3 2026 guidance: revenue growth in the "low-thirties %" range, gross profit growth "mid-to-high twenties %," operating expenses at 33–34% of revenue, FCF margin "high-teens to low-twenties," and stock-based compensation of $150M. This is essentially the same language used for the Q2 guide — the same growth-rate range has now been guided two quarters running, and the actual Q2 print of +34% landed at or slightly above the top of that range, suggesting management's confidence in holding this pace is genuine rather than a number that gets walked back later.

3. Valuation: 68x forward P/E is the richest in its peer group

📊 Forward P/E of 68.1x (based on the $142.25 close on 2026-09-25; market cap $183.0B; forward P/S 13.8x). Analyst consensus is Buy, with 54 analysts averaging a price target of $172.77, implying roughly +21.5% from current levels.

Against its e-commerce SaaS peer group, 68x is clearly the most expensive multiple (most peers sit between 10x and 30x). That premium corresponds to this quarter's four core metrics all growing 30%+ together, plus a steadily improving FCF margin — the market is paying a much higher multiple for "durable growth" here than for comparable names, which also means the stock is unusually sensitive to any core metric (especially GMV/revenue growth) coming in below expectations.

Cycle intensity (invest-playbook framework): e-commerce SaaS sits in a moderate cycle, linked to consumer spending and the macro backdrop, though Shopify's subscription-plus-take-rate model gives revenue real stickiness. The genuine variable is the continuing rise in Merchant Solutions' share of revenue and its long-term pressure on gross margin — a cost that the 68x multiple doesn't yet appear to fully price in.

4. Peer group scan

Group definition: E-commerce SaaS / storefront and checkout infrastructure for merchants. Shopify leads the space of helping merchants build storefronts and process payments; Commerce (formerly BigCommerce, now trading as CMRC) is its most direct enterprise-tier competitor; Wix skews toward small-business/DIY site building; Etsy is a handmade-goods vertical marketplace — not SaaS, but competing for the same consumer traffic and merchant mind-share; Global-e is cross-border checkout infrastructure that both partners with and competes against Shopify; PayPal goes head-to-head with Shopify Payments at the checkout layer.

One cross-sectional observation: SHOP's 68.1x sits well above every other name in the group (most are 8–30x), reflecting that it's the only company in this group with all four core metrics growing 30%+ at once. CMRC (formerly BigCommerce) at 8.4x is the cheapest in the group, but its growth has also clearly stalled — the two aren't really comparable on the same tier.

5. What to watch next quarter (falsifiable)

① Does Q3 revenue hold the low-thirties% guide?
This quarter was +34%, a third straight quarter above 30%
In range or above = pace continues; clearly under 30% = this quarter may have been the peak
② Does the Merchant-mix drag on gross margin continue?
47.7% this quarter, −0.9pp YoY, Merchant Solutions now 77.6% of revenue
Mix rising further with margin below 47% = structural pressure persists; stabilizing = this quarter was a one-off step
③ Does GAAP EPS swing again on investment fair value?
Q1 loss of $0.45, Q2 gain of $1.16, largely driven by investment marks
Another large swing next quarter means headline EPS should keep being read separately from core profitability (adjusted EPS / operating margin)

6. Bull and bear cases

Three reasons to be bullish:

① Four core metrics grew 30%+ together — a rare, broad-based print. GMV, revenue, gross profit and FCF didn't come at the expense of one another.

② Guidance has now used the same growth-rate range for two quarters running, and the actual result landed at or above the top of that range. Management's confidence in the pace looks genuine, not a number set up to be walked back.

③ FCF margin has improved for two straight quarters (16%→15%→18%), on an extremely light capital structure (just $4M of capex) — cash generation wasn't sacrificed for growth this quarter.

Three reasons to be bearish:

① Valuation is the richest in its peer group. A 68x forward P/E will react sharply to any growth result that falls short of expectations.

② Gross margin is structurally shifting toward the lower-margin Merchant Solutions line, down 0.9pp YoY. If that mix shift continues, the downward pressure on gross margin is a trend, not noise.

③ GAAP EPS is extremely volatile. Another large loss-driving fair-value markdown could easily be misread against the headline number, adding noise to the stock.

★ The real disagreement is how to read the structural gross-margin decline. Bulls see it as a reasonable trade-off — lower revenue quality but much larger absolute scale; bears see it as an early sign that growth is being diluted into lower-quality revenue.
