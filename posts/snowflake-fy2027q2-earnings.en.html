Snowflake．FY2027 Q2 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Product revenue of $1.49B, +37% YoY — a third straight quarter of acceleration (32%→34%→37%). Non-GAAP operating margin expanded in step to 15.3%, and core EPS of $0.62 crushed estimates. The stock jumped over 20% after hours.

Source: Snowflake IR release + call highlights Period: 2026-05-01 – 2026-07-31 (FY27 Q2) Reported: 2026-09-02 after close Compiled: 2026-09-26

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

📈 Growth isn't just holding up — it's accelerating. Product revenue YoY growth has risen for three straight quarters: 32% (Q2 FY26) → 34% (Q1 FY27) → 37% (this quarter). Non-GAAP operating margin expanded right alongside it: 11% → 12% → 15.3%. Growth and margin moving up together — not a trade-off in either direction — is the single line worth remembering from this print.

💰 Core EPS of $0.62 vs. a $0.45 estimate — a beat of nearly 38%. Revenue of $1.55B also topped the $1.48B consensus by roughly 4.9%. Management raised full-year FY27 product revenue guidance for the second time, from $5.84B (given in May) to $6.07B, and lifted the non-GAAP operating margin guide from 13.5% to 14.5%. The stock jumped over 20% after hours.

💧 FCF fell to just $83.8M (5.4% margin) this quarter — but that's seasonal, not a sign of weakening. Last quarter (Q1 FY27) FCF was $232.8M (roughly 17% margin); a year ago (Q2 FY26) adjusted FCF margin was also 6%. Annual-contract cash from SaaS customers tends to land in Q4/Q1, so Q2 is structurally the low point every year — this year lands in the same spot as last year, not a new problem.

★ Snowflake is still GAAP-unprofitable (operating loss of $263.0M, margin −17%), and stock-based comp keeps the GAAP/non-GAAP gap wide. That's also why the "133x" forward P/E only makes sense against a non-GAAP EPS estimate — running it off GAAP EPS gives a negative, meaningless number.

1. The core numbers: acceleration plus margin expansion

Remaining performance obligations (RPO) stood at $9.00B, +30% YoY, giving forward revenue visibility. Net revenue retention (NRR) was 126%, up slightly from 125% a year ago. Customers with $1M+ trailing-12-month product revenue reached 828, +27% YoY. Within the AI product line, CoCo (the AI coding agent) reached 9,100 accounts, up more than 2,000 in the quarter — management said on the call that AI-related products drove roughly half of this quarter's growth acceleration.

★ Both FCF and capex are small numbers here (capex was just $7.6M) — this is an asset-light subscription software model, structurally different from the hyperscalers currently pouring tens of billions into AI infrastructure capex. The same "FCF anxiety" framework doesn't apply the same way.

2. Guidance: two straight raises, no reversal

Q3 FY27 product revenue guidance is $1,588–1,593M (+37–38% YoY) — asking for growth to accelerate slightly further. Non-GAAP operating margin guidance is 15.5%, above this quarter's actual 15.3%. The full-year FY27 product revenue guide moved from $5.84B (May) to $6.07B (this quarter), and the full-year non-GAAP operating margin guide moved from 13.5% to 14.5% — both lines raised in the same direction, with no reversal along the way.

3. Valuation: 133x forward P/E is expensive, but the method matters

📊 Forward P/E of 133.4x (based on the $335.94 close on 2026-09-25; market cap $118.4B; forward P/S 21.8x). Analyst consensus is Buy, with 50 analysts averaging a price target of $425.19, implying roughly +26.6% from current levels.

One thing worth flagging: Futu's GAAP-EPS-based trailing/forward P/E both come out negative (because Snowflake is still GAAP-unprofitable), which carries no valuation meaning at all. The commonly cited "133x" forward P/E is computed against the analyst consensus for non-GAAP/adjusted EPS instead. The two methods diverge enormously — always be explicit about which one is being used.

Cycle intensity (invest-playbook framework): cloud data platforms sit in a moderate-to-low cycle category — revenue is mostly subscription-based with good visibility ($9.0B in RPO) — but the valuation itself swings hard, as this quarter's 20%+ single-day pop shows. At 133x, a large amount of "growth accelerating + margin expanding" good news is already priced in; any miss against guidance next quarter on either growth or margin carries real multiple-compression risk.

4. Peer group scan

Group definition: Cloud data platforms / data infrastructure. Snowflake is the leading data warehouse and data cloud, sitting in the same infrastructure layer companies use once their data moves to the cloud as MongoDB (document databases), Datadog (observability/monitoring), and Elastic (search and log analytics). Palantir sits one layer up — an AI/decision-analytics platform built on top of data — and is frequently compared to Snowflake on valuation. (Confluent used to belong in this group too; it was acquired by IBM and delisted in March 2026 — a footnote on consolidation in this space.)

One cross-sectional observation: SNOW at 133.4x, PLTR at 100.0x, and DDOG at 101.1x are the three most expensive names in the group — all three share the trait of growth that is still accelerating or holding at a high rate. MDB at 59.2x and ESTC at 25.1x are notably cheaper, corresponding to growth that has slowed to a moderate pace. Valuation within the group currently tracks growth speed fairly directly — it isn't moving up or down together as a block.

6. Bull and bear cases

Three reasons to be bullish:

① Growth and margin are rising together with no reversal across three quarters. Product revenue YoY went 32%→34%→37%, and non-GAAP operating margin went 11%→12%→15.3% — a combination rarely seen in a maturing SaaS company.

② Guidance has been raised twice in a row, and management's confidence is growing. The FY27 product revenue guide moved from $5.84B to $6.07B, and the margin guide from 13.5% to 14.5%, with the direction consistent both times.

③ The AI product line is starting to contribute real growth. CoCo accounts rose by 2,000 in one quarter to 9,100, and management said AI-related products drove roughly half of this quarter's acceleration — a genuine new growth engine, not just narrative.

Three reasons to be bearish:

① Valuation already prices in a lot of good news. Forward P/E of 133x and P/S of 22x make this one of the most expensive names in its peer group — the stock will be highly sensitive to any result that falls short of guidance.

② Still GAAP-unprofitable, and the stock-comp gap hasn't narrowed. Operating loss of $263.0M (margin −17%) shows the underlying financial structure beneath the non-GAAP halo hasn't yet turned positive.

③ FCF fell to just $83.8M this quarter. There's a seasonal explanation, but only a real recovery in Q4/Q1 will confirm this was seasonal rather than a new source of pressure.

★ The real disagreement is how many more quarters this "acceleration plus margin expansion" combination can run. Bulls see a structural inflection; bears think 133x has already bought the next 2–3 quarters of good news in advance.
