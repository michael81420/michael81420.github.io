SpaceX．2026 Q3 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of $14.58B, up +161% YoY; first operating profit of $2.79B (19.1% margin vs −1.8% last quarter); diluted EPS of $0.35 (consensus roughly $0.10–0.15).
But net income of $4.40B sits $1.6B above operating income, quarterly free cash flow is about −$10.0B (derived), and Q4 capex is guided to $35–40B. The stock rose 8.6% the next day (vs −13.6% last quarter). Built from the IR release, a call summary, and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: SpaceX 8-K Ex-99.1 (SEC) + Futu + secondary call summaries Period: 2026-07-01 – 2026-09-30 Reported: 2026-10-20 after close Compiled: 2026-10-21

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🔄 The core business changed shape: SI overtook Starlink as the largest revenue source, and operating income went from −$0.14B to +$2.79B. Revenue rose 86.5% QoQ, with SI alone adding $5.0B in a quarter (52% of revenue vs 33% last quarter). Consolidated gross margin was 62.4% (55.3% last quarter, derived). It is not the new normal: management said verbally that Q4 gross margin will return close to Q2 levels (Q3 included one-time customer-mix benefits).

🧾 The $0.35 EPS is thinner than the headline. Net income of $4.40B is $1.61B above operating income: non-operating other income of $1.03B (of which $561M is related-party), a tax benefit of $0.39B, and net interest of +$0.20B. Strip other income and the tax benefit and EPS is about $0.24 (pre-tax upper bound, derived) — still above consensus, but roughly a third of reported EPS is not operations. Conversely, operating income was depressed by $1.2B of Cursor acquisition-related stock compensation.

💸 Capex looks flat, but the heavy quarter is next. Q3 capex was $19.6B (+6.7% QoQ), operating cash flow about $9.6B (derived), so quarterly FCF is about −$10.0B; cash and securities fell from $100B to $89.9B. Q4 capex is guided to $35–40B (verbal on the call), about 1.8–2.0x Q3. Last quarter's test — did capex peak? — answer: no.

★ Only a secondary call summary (MarketBeat) was available — no full transcript or Q&A; every "verbal on the call" figure below is flagged as secondary. Forward PE is 47.4x and forward PS 15.1x (Futu forecast valuation).

1. Core numbers: this quarter vs last quarter vs last year vs expectations

⚠️ Consensus varies by source: EPS consensus is $0.10 per MarketBeat and $0.15 per another summary; revenue consensus is $12.7B (Bloomberg, secondary) or $12.89B (pre-earnings preview). Futu has no single-quarter consensus, so the beat can only be given as a range: revenue about +13–15%, EPS about 2.3–3.5x. IR vs Futu: operating income is $2,791M in the IR and $2,794M at Futu ($3M gap; IR takes precedence); other line items match.

2. Segments: the revenue mix flipped

SI added $5.0B in one quarter, and only $0.61B of that is acquired Cursor (IR: counted from the 2026-08-14 closing). The remaining ~$4.4B comes from what the IR calls "newly activated cloud services agreements" plus Grok enterprise and consumer adoption. The company disclosed no customer concentration, contract term, or pricing — the biggest information gap this quarter: SI is now over half of revenue, yet who is paying and for how long is invisible.

Space (the launch business) grew only +6% YoY. Total launches were 35 (Q2: 38, year-ago: 44) and mass to orbit was 476 tonnes (year-ago 525, −9%); the +84% QoQ revenue jump came mainly from customer launches rising from 10 to 12 and customer payload from 87 to 119 tonnes. Starship Flight 14 reached orbit for the first time carrying 26 Starlink V3 satellites — a technical milestone, but it did not translate into Space revenue growth this quarter.

3. Earnings quality: where the $4.40B came from

The good side: operating income itself is real — Connectivity operating income was $2.37B (YoY +99%), SI operating income was $431M (about $1.65B excluding the $1.2B Cursor stock compensation, derived), and Adjusted EBITDA was $8.81B (YoY +306%). The discount side: (1) other income of $1.03B includes $561M from related parties, and the IR does not explain its nature; (2) taxes were a $390M benefit (a $562M expense a year ago); (3) Connectivity's 55% gross margin includes a roughly 3-point one-time tariff refund (verbal on the call), so excluding it the margin is about 52%, flat with Q2 (52.0%).

📌 Conclusion: the operating beat and the swing to profit are real, but headline EPS of $0.35 should not be used as a run-rate. We use $0.19–$0.24 as core EPS (derived: operating income plus net interest of $2.99B ÷ 1,247.5M shares = $0.24 pre-tax upper bound; about $0.19 at an assumed 21% tax rate). Both ends are above consensus.

4. Capex and cash: the pressure is only starting

The IR release gives only nine-month cumulative cash flow: operating cash flow of $13.10B and capex of $48.07B. Subtracting the first-half figures used in last quarter's note (operating cash flow $3.47B, capex $28.48B) gives Q3 operating cash flow ≈ $9.63B and FCF ≈ −$9.96B (derived; the capex piece ties exactly to the $19.59B segment total in the IR, while the operating cash flow piece is a subtraction). The company still does not publish single-quarter cash flow directly.

Three cross-checks: (1) cash plus marketable securities of $89.86B is $10.1B below Q2's $100B, consistent with the derived FCF; (2) debt including finance leases is $38.8B vs $22.9B at the start of the year; (3) deferred revenue rose from $14.3B at Q2 to $17.5B (+$3.2B, balance-sheet difference), meaning about a third of operating cash flow came from customer prepayments — the same lesson as ASML: prepayment-boosted cash flow is not earning power.

If Q4 operating cash flow resembles Q3 (assumption), Q4 FCF is about −$25 to −$30B and ending cash falls to roughly $60–65B before any new financing (derived). Management also said (secondary summary) that the company is investment grade and in talks with debt providers.

5. Guidance and the call

Cross-checking the company's own numbers (derived): Q1 2027 revenue guidance of $33–37B equals $11–12.3B per month, or $132–148B annualized, while the "$100B ARR by year-end" target only requires December revenue of $8.3B. The two targets do not line up: either $100B is a conservative anchor, or the Q1 guide includes lumpy or timing-driven revenue. This is the contradiction to press on next quarter.

Call takeaways (MarketBeat summary only — no full transcript)

Wording changes (before/after quotes, from the IR releases)

① Segment name: Q2 8-K "AI Segment" and "AI solutions & infrastructure" → Q3 8-K "SI Segment" (Super Intelligence) and "SI solutions & infrastructure". Fact: the name was broadened and the Q3 release no longer calls the segment "AI".

② CFO commentary: Q2 "significant margin expansion led by our new AI compute agreements … We ended the second quarter with $100 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities" → Q3 "We are building the infrastructure of the future with discipline — focused on capital efficiency, strong returns on invested capital, and maintaining a robust, investment-grade balance sheet". Fact: the Q3 commentary no longer cites the cash balance and instead stresses capital efficiency, return on invested capital, and an investment-grade balance sheet. [View] This reads as a response to the −13.6% reaction to last quarter's capex, and as a preview of financing.

③ Launch timing: IR release says of Starbase, Louisiana "first launch targeted for 2029"; on the call Musk called 2028 an "internal target" and 2029 "highly likely" (secondary summary). The release and the verbal remark differ by a year.

Analyst focus: the summary records only one exchange (the Tesla merger), where Musk said each side formed an independent special committee, terms are not agreed, and there is no guarantee of a deal. The remaining analyst focus areas were not obtained.

Dodges and vagueness: Starship Flight 15 success is "more likely than not"; Louisiana 2028 is only an "internal target"; compute is split roughly half internal and half external cloud customers, which "could shift over time"; SI customer concentration, contract terms and pricing, and quarterly cash flow were not disclosed.

Numbers given only on the call (verbal, secondary): Q4 capex $35–40B; Q1 2027 revenue $33–37B; Q4 gross margin near Q2 levels; compute nearly 2 GW in October, 2.5–3 GW by year-end, and no fewer than 13 GW by end-2027; 800,000 paid weekly Grok Bot users; Starlink Mobile above 22 million monthly users (+37% QoQ); backlog of $63B (including $17.5B of deferred revenue, about $42B recognized within a year; Q2: $47.5B); Musk said 2027 year-end ARR could be $500B to $1T ([view], not guidance).

7. Valuation

📉 Forward PE 47.4x, forward PS 15.1x (Futu forecast valuation; price about $161.7, next-day close $160.52). Trailing PE is negative and not meaningful. Futu's TTM PS of 95.1x (77.9th percentile, with only ~4 months of listing history) uses pre-Q3 trailing revenue; ⚠️ after updating for Q3, market cap of about $2.18T (13.58B IR shares × $160.52, derived) ÷ TTM revenue of about $32.0B is roughly 68x. The two must not be mixed.

Cycle first: hybrid. Starlink is structural growth (13.6M subscribers, +89% YoY), but this quarter's valuation story is led by SI, whose revenue comes from compute leasing — a capital-intensive, contract-driven, commodity-like cycle where compute prices, renewals and funding costs decide the trough. Forward PE of 47x rests on the Q3 high-margin mix and the non-operating items persisting, while management has already said Q4 gross margin will fall back.

For reference, not adoption: Futu consensus has 33 analysts, 81.8% Buy, and an average target of $247.86; Morningstar's quantitative model gives 2 stars, fair value $75.69 (⚠️ updated 10/19, before earnings), a narrow moat, very high uncertainty, and a price/fair value ratio of about 2.1x at the current price (derived). The gap is more than 3x, showing how wide the assumption spread is; per this site's principles, sell-side targets are not relied on.

10. Bull and bear cases

Three bull points:

① Operating leverage is real. Operating income went from −$0.14B to +$2.79B, Adjusted EBITDA margin is 60.4% (45.3% last quarter), and Connectivity's operating margin is 45.1% with subscribers and ARPU rising together ($66 to $69).

② Demand visibility improved. Backlog is $63B (Q2: $47.5B, +33%), deferred revenue is $17.5B, and Q1 2027 revenue guidance of $33–37B is the first formal forward range.

③ Relative strength with calmer volatility. Pre-earnings implied volatility was 61.8% (115% before Q2), and the stock rose 8.6% afterward versus −13.6% last time; the market has shifted from fearing capex to waiting for earnings.

Three bear points:

① Great numbers, negative cash. FCF is about −$10.0B and Q4 capex guidance nearly doubles; if operating cash flow does not keep up, cash would approach financing territory in under three quarters.

② Earnings quality is discounted and not repeatable. Other income (including $561M from related parties), the tax benefit, the tariff refund, and the Q3 customer-mix benefit all flatter Q3; management has already warned Q4 gross margin will fall back.

③ Concentration and governance. SI is over half of revenue with no customer-concentration disclosure; Tesla merger talks, related-party dealings (related-party interest expense of $351M, other income of $561M), and staged lockup releases (our wiki records roughly 28% of shares unlocking on the Q3 print; not re-verified this time) are all non-fundamental supply variables.

★ Compare Oracle FY26Q4: a clean beat with capex above plan, negative FCF and an equity raise sent the stock sharply lower. SPCX has the negative FCF and the funding need, but this quarter added an operating-profit turn and a large revenue beat. Market tolerance depends on whether profit shows up in the same quarter, not on capex itself.
