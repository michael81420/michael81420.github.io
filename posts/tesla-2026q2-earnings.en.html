Tesla．Q2 2026 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

The third full quarter after the US $7,500 EV tax credit expired on 2025/9/30. Deliveries of 480,126, +25% YoY, a Q2 record, and revenue of $28.24B beat consensus by 2.4% —
but non-GAAP EPS of $0.33 against a $0.55 consensus is a 40% miss, total gross margin missed by 270bp, and over 90% of GAAP net income came from a SpaceX stake gain and a one-time tax item. Built from the IR shareholder letter and Futu cross-checks. Not investment advice.
Source: Tesla SEC 8-K Exhibit 99.1 shareholder letter Period: 2026-04-01 – 2026-06-30 Reported: 2026-07-22 after close Compiled: 2026-08-03

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚗 On demand, one thing is now proven: it sells without the subsidy. With the tax credit gone and no policy tailwind at all, deliveries still grew 25% YoY to 480,126, a Q2 record. Vehicle inventory fell from 27 days to 15 days (production of 451,758 ran below deliveries of 480,126, drawing down stock), and the company describes itself as supply-constrained. This is the strongest bull argument in the print.

🧾 But earnings quality is poor, and it broke in three places at once. ① Regulatory credit revenue of $146M, −67% YoY, with its contribution to auto gross margin shrinking from 2.2pp to 0.6pp; ② energy storage gross margin collapsed from 39.5% to 20.4% (the company cites a one-time cell warranty charge); ③ total gross margin of 16.8% against a 19.5% consensus is a 270bp miss, with operating margin down to 1.4%. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.33 versus $0.55 is a 40% miss.

🎭 Over 90% of the $1.114B GAAP net income is not operating. $763M (after tax) came from an unrealised gain on the SpaceX stake and $274M from a one-time California deferred tax asset reversal — $1.037B combined, or 93%. Strip them and core earnings are roughly $77M. This is the same mechanism as Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon this quarter; the difference is that Tesla's operating base is far smaller, so the masking effect is far stronger.

🤖 Robotaxi, Cybercab and Optimus all disclosed $0 of revenue this quarter. Robotaxi now operates in seven metros and added three Florida cities in July on an unsupervised basis; paid FSD subscriptions reached 1.48M (+56% YoY) — but the company discloses no revenue figure for any of them. Meanwhile the tone on Optimus turned distinctly cautious: management called it the hardest product they've ever tried to manufacture, with an early S-curve that will be "flat and long."

★ The one business where volume, price and quality all improved is Services & Other: $4.58B, +50.4% YoY, with a record 14.1% gross margin (5.4% → 9.2% → 14.1% across three quarters) and a share of revenue rising from 13.5% to 16.2%. This is the closest thing to evidence that software monetisation is producing real cash — though the company likewise doesn't break out FSD subscription revenue.

1. Unpack net income first: only $77M of $1.114B is operating

This chart is the starting point for the quarter. GAAP net income of $1.114B (−4.9% YoY, +133.5% QoQ) looks passable — until you unpack it:

$763M (after tax) is an unrealised gain on the SpaceX stake — non-cash, non-operating, and it swings back when the underlying valuation does. $274M is a one-time reversal of a California deferred tax asset. Together $1.037B, or 93% of net income. The remaining $77M is the operating business.

The mechanism itself isn't new this quarter: Alphabet booked $99B from Anthropic and SpaceX, Microsoft $3.2B from Anthropic, Amazon $53.4B from Anthropic. The difference is relative scale — those three have operating earnings large enough that one-time gains merely make good numbers look better; Tesla's operating earnings are small enough that the one-time gains carry the entire result.

And non-GAAP EPS — which should already handle some adjustments — still missed consensus by 40%, down 18% YoY and 19.5% QoQ. Both definitions point to the same conclusion: operating earnings power is receding, and this isn't accounting noise.

2. Three businesses, three different margin directions

① Energy storage is the biggest surprise in the print. Gross margin fell from 39.5% in Q1 to 20.4%, down 1914bp in a quarter. The company attributes it to cell-supplier warranty costs and calls it one-time. Revenue of $3.14B also missed the $3.77B consensus by 16.7% — even though deployments of 13.5 GWh (+40.6% YoY) were genuinely strong.

★ This is the largest single piece of noise in the "storage is the new profit pillar" narrative. If margin returns above 30% next quarter, the one-time explanation holds; if it stays near 20% or lower, the narrative needs reassessing. This is the most falsifiable test of the quarter.

② Auto margin excluding credits is actually improving. Non-GAAP auto gross margin of 16.3% is up 130bp YoY (from 15.0%), driven by lower import tariffs and falling inventory costs. The −290bp QoQ move mostly reflects Q1 one-time benefits (warranty and tariff) not repeating, not a deteriorating trend.

③ Services is the only business good on all three counts. Gross margin at 5.4% → 9.2% → 14.1%, three straight expansions and a record; revenue +50.4% YoY; share of revenue up from 13.5% to 16.2%. Low capital intensity, high margin, scalable. But the company does not break out FSD subscription revenue — only that paid FSD subscriptions reached 1.48M (+56% YoY) — so how much of this is software versus parts and maintenance cannot be verified externally.

3. Regulatory credits are disappearing, with nothing to replace them

Automotive regulatory credit revenue was $146M, down 66.7% YoY and 61.6% QoQ (against $439M a year ago and $380M last quarter).

The impact is clearest in gross margin contribution: credits went from 2.2pp of auto gross margin a year ago to 0.6pp. That is also why GAAP auto gross margin (including credits) of 16.9% is down 30bp YoY while the non-GAAP figure excluding credits is up 130bp — manufacturing efficiency is genuinely improving; credits are the drag.

The problem is that nothing replaces this line. Credit revenue comes from other automakers buying emissions allowances from Tesla; as US policy shifts and other manufacturers raise their own EV mix, that demand declines structurally. There is no policy path to a replacement in the near term. At this rate, quarterly credit revenue falling below $100M is a matter of time.

📌 A framing worth adopting: the market has been used to treating "Tesla gross margin" as a proxy for manufacturing efficiency. But credits are mixed into it, and credits have nothing to do with manufacturing efficiency. From here, the number to watch is auto gross margin excluding credits — 16.3% this quarter. That is the true level of the core business and the figure to track next quarter.

4. Capex +132% QoQ, FCF negative for the first time in two years

Operating cash flow is actually good: $4.70B, +85% YoY. But capex jumped from $2.49B to $5.79B (+132% QoQ, +142% YoY), turning free cash flow to −$1.09B — the first negative print in nearly two years. Cash and short-term investments fell from $44.74B to $43.52B.

⚠️ And the capex run-rate is actually behind schedule. FY2026 guidance is "over $25B," but the first half spent only $8.28B — the second half needs roughly $8.35B per quarter (another 44% above this quarter's $5.79B) to reach the floor. CFO Taneja's framing was that the company is "willing to accept lower capital efficiency in exchange for accelerated execution."

The guidance hasn't broken, but the annualised pace is behind. This is a hard, directly verifiable number for next quarter: does Q3 capex reach roughly $8.35B? If it does, execution is catching up. If it doesn't, either guidance gets cut or Q4 has to spike.

5. Robotaxi and Optimus: progress yes, revenue zero

Two shifts in framing are worth recording:

① The stated Robotaxi bottleneck changed. Last quarter it was regulatory and supply; this quarter management describes it as "safety — the march of nines reliability — rather than demand." The city count did deliver (seven metros plus Miami, Orlando and Tampa added unsupervised in July), but changing the constraint from regulation to safety reliability may simply be a rephrasing that sidesteps the demand question, and warrants watching.

② The Optimus tone turned distinctly cautious. The timeline is unchanged (still "later this year" for production), the Model S/X line has been removed and the first-generation Optimus line is being installed. But management described it as "the hardest product to manufacture" with an early "S-curve that will be flat and long" — against last quarter's "Fremont ready for production by year-end" and "Gen 3 hands deployed around the clock in the factory in Q2," this is a retreat from execution detail into expectation management.

★ The single most important fact: Robotaxi, Cybercab and Optimus all disclosed $0 of revenue this quarter. Not "small" — there is no separately disclosed revenue figure for any of them in the filing. The valuation premium the market pays for these three stories currently has no financial number attached to it.

6. Valuation: over 90% of the market cap is narrative option value

📐 Trailing PE 288.2x, forward PE 284.7x, at the 28th percentile of its one-year range (the percentile was as high as 71.2% on 2026-07-08 before the stock fell nearly 20%). But 284x in absolute terms is an astronomical figure for any automotive stock — the low percentile is only relative to its own history.

Cycle intensity (invest-playbook): automotive is a high-intensity cyclical industry, and Tesla itself demonstrated how sensitive EV demand is to subsidies — after the credit expired on 2025/9/30, deliveries fell for three straight quarters (Q3'25, Q4'25, Q1'26) before recovering in Q2'26. But Tesla's share price no longer prices as a cyclical automaker; it prices as an AI and robotics narrative stock.

The method behind the chart: treat Tesla as a pure auto plus energy plus services company, apply peer automaker forward PEs (GM 7.3x, Toyota 9.5x, BYD 17.6x — using even the global EV leader's multiple as the ceiling) to TTM non-GAAP EPS ($0.50 + $0.50 + $0.41 + $0.33 = $1.74), and the implied "core business fair price" is roughly $25–31. The actual price is $311.

★ This is illustrative arithmetic, not a rigorous SOTP — it ignores Tesla's growth premium, the higher multiple energy arguably deserves, and the fact that TTM EPS sits at a trough depressed by one-time factors. But the directional conclusion is clear: the weaker the core earnings, the more the price depends on narrative, and the more fragile it becomes. This is precisely that kind of quarter.

Analyst consensus: 24 analysts, average target $405.8 (high $505 / low $130), with buy/hold/sell at 41.7% / 50% / 8.3%. Note that hold is half the book and there is an 8.3% sell allocation — rare among mega-cap tech (compare AMZN at 97.4% buy and META at 88.1%). The $130-to-$505 dispersion says analysts have no consensus on this company at all.

Price action: $374.01 before earnings → $298.32 at the 7/31 low (−20.2%) → $311.21 intraday on 2026-08-03.

9. Both sides of the argument

Three reasons to be constructive:

① Demand is proven without subsidies. With the credit gone and no policy tailwind, deliveries still grew 25% YoY to a Q2 record, and inventory compressed to 15 days. This is the hardest bull argument in the print.

② Services is a genuine high-margin engine. Gross profit of $648M at a record 14.1% margin, expanding structurally for three straight quarters (5.4% → 9.2% → 14.1%). Low capital intensity and scalable — the closest thing to evidence of software monetisation producing cash.

③ Auto gross margin excluding credits is actually up 130bp YoY. Core manufacturing efficiency is still improving; the QoQ decline is largely a base effect from Q1 one-time benefits, not a deteriorating trend.

Three reasons to be cautious:

① Over 90% of earnings are not operating. $763M of SpaceX gain plus $274M of tax reversal is 93% of net income, leaving roughly $77M of core; and non-GAAP EPS still missed consensus by 40%. Both definitions point to receding operating earnings power.

② Cash is starting to flow out while capex is behind schedule. FCF turned negative at −$1.09B (first in nearly two years) and cash fell $1.22B QoQ; hitting FY26's >$25B requires another 44% per quarter in the second half, with no corresponding revenue or margin return visible yet.

③ All three narratives disclosed $0 of revenue. Robotaxi, Cybercab and Optimus have no separately disclosed revenue, and the commercialisation framing (safety bottleneck, a long flat S-curve) is more cautious than last quarter — while the premium the market pays for them is roughly 90% of the market cap.

★ The disagreement here differs in kind from the others. For Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon the question is "how many years until capex pays back" — a timing question. For Tesla it is "will Robotaxi and Optimus happen at all" — an existence question. The first can be argued with a discount rate; the second only with a probability. That is also why half the analyst book sits on hold.
