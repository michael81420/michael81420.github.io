TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 Revenue, EPS, gross margin and operating margin all hit records at once. Revenue of US$40.20B (+33.7% YoY, +12.0% QoQ) landed at the top of the company's own $39.0–40.2B guide from last quarter; diluted EPS of US$4.31 per ADR beat the roughly $3.80 consensus by over 13%. Advanced technologies (7nm and below) made up 77% of wafer revenue, with AI/HPC demand as the main engine.

📈 Capex jumped 42% QoQ to a record high, but free cash flow wasn't gutted the way it has been elsewhere. Quarterly capex of about $15.7B (+42% QoQ, +67% YoY) was an all-time high, but operating cash flow rose in step to about $24.8B, leaving free cash flow of roughly $9.1B — down 17% QoQ, yet up 44% YoY. Compared with other names in this AI-capex cycle where FCF has been cut to almost nothing, TSMC's margin structure and pricing power are giving it far more cash-flow resilience.

🔺 FY26 capex guidance was raised this year: from $52–56B early in the year, held near the high end in April, and now raised outright to $60–64B. In the same call, the CFO flagged that N2 (2nm) ramp will dilute gross margin by 3–4pp, with Q3 gross margin guided to 65–67% — already a step down from this quarter's record 67.7%.

★ On valuation: forward PE is around 23.1x, but that sits at the 66.8th percentile of its one-year range — the post-earnings rally has already eaten into some of the apparent cheapness.

Guidance: capex raised, margin guide cut in advance

Two layers here. First: Q3 revenue guidance of $44.6–45.8B implies roughly +12.4% QoQ at the midpoint versus this quarter's actual $40.20B — momentum management itself is confident about. Second, and the real story: FY26 capex guidance went from $52–56B early in the year, held near the top of that range in April, and is now raised outright to $60–64B — the whole range shifted up, not just the floor. Management stressed on the call that this reflects long-term customer commitments, not a single-quarter pull-forward.

On the same call, the CFO proactively flagged that 2nm (N2) ramp will dilute gross margin by 3–4pp; Q3 gross margin is guided to 65–67%, a step down from this quarter's record 67.7%. A guidance cut alongside a capex raise is a combination worth watching closely.

Valuation: forward PE isn't expensive, but the percentile has climbed

📊 Forward PE is about 23.1x (1-year average around 32.8x), sitting at the 66.8th percentile of its one-year range — the post-earnings rally has already taken back some of the cheapness; this isn't a low point.

Analyst consensus (as of 2026-09-25): 8 analysts, 87.5% buy / 12.5% hold / 0% sell, average price target of $547.38 (high $650 / low $440) versus the $450.61 price at query time, implying roughly +21.5% upside. The sample size is small, so read this with some caution.

Cycle-severity read (invest-playbook): foundry manufacturing is inherently capital-intensive and cyclical, but this cycle is being driven by structural AI/HPC demand plus TSMC's near-irreplaceable pricing power in advanced nodes — closer to a "structural expansion" than a classic inventory cycle. The variable to actually watch is whether capex keeps rising without a matching gain in pricing power. So far capex and gross margin are rising together, with no divergence yet.

Scanning the Peer Group

Group definition: Foundry (logic semiconductor contract manufacturing). Beyond TSMC, this covers companies with the same "manufacturing chips for others" business model, split between advanced and mature process nodes. (Fabless design customers like Nvidia/Apple and upstream equipment makers like ASML belong to different groups and aren't included here.)

One cross-sectional observation: TSMC is the only name in this group with 77% of revenue coming from advanced nodes. UMC and GlobalFoundries have no presence below 7nm at all; SMIC's valuation is pinned near the bottom of its own one-year range by export controls; and Intel Foundry is still trying to prove it can win external advanced-node orders. The valuation spread within this group is really about who can — and can't — do advanced-node manufacturing.

Bull & Bear

Three reasons to be bullish:

① Four records at once isn't a coincidence. Revenue, EPS, gross margin and operating margin all hit records in the same quarter, which means advanced-node pricing power and yields are compounding together, not a one-off item.

② Cash-flow resilience beats peers. Even with capex up 42%, FCF is still up 44% YoY — this expansion cycle is still in the phase of "earning faster than spending," unlike peers whose FCF has been cut to nearly nothing.

③ Advanced-node pricing power is still expanding. 77% of revenue now comes from 7nm and below, and TSMC is the only name in its peer group actually capable of shipping at the most advanced nodes at volume — the marginal return on capex still looks positive.

Three reasons to be cautious:

① Capex guidance keeps rising with no sign of a top. $52–56B to $60–64B is already a 15%+ increase; if this trajectory continues, depreciation will keep pressuring the income statement for years.

② Gross margin guidance was cut proactively. Q3's 65–67% is a step down from this quarter's record 67.7%, the first such guided-down signal in recent quarters, and N2 yield ramp uncertainty is still live.

③ Valuation percentile isn't low anymore. It's at the 66.8th percentile, and the analyst sample is only 8 names — if Q3 momentum disappoints, there's real room for a valuation reset.

★ The real fork in the road: do you read the capex raises as demand being confirmed, or as the cycle running hot. The cash-flow numbers currently favor the former, but the proactive margin-guidance cut is the first concrete piece of evidence for the latter.
