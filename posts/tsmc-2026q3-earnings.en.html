TSMC．2026 Q3 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of US$46.72B (+41.2% YoY, +16.2% QoQ) beat the top of the company's own guide by about 2%; operating margin of 58.7% also beat guidance.
But gross margin slipped 1.3pp QoQ, and a 14.3% rise in operating income became only +8.0% in EPS. Capex fell QoQ, FY26 growth was raised to about 44%, and capex stayed at $60–64B. Built from the IR release, the call transcript and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: TSMC 3Q26 release (SEC 6-K) + 2026-10-15 call transcript Period: 2026-07-01 – 2026-09-30 Reported: 2026-10-15 Compiled: 2026-10-16

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🚀 Revenue cleared the top of the company's own guide, and 2nm debuted at 8% of wafer revenue. US$46.72B against a $44.6–45.8B guide is about 2.0% above the top (last quarter it merely touched the top); NT$ revenue of 1.494 trillion also beat the LSEG 19-analyst consensus of NT$1.46 trillion by roughly 2.3%. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) were 79% of wafer revenue (77% last quarter); HPC was 68% of revenue, +22% QoQ.

⚖️ Margins diverged, and EPS lagged operations. Gross margin of 66.4% sat inside the 65–67% guide but fell 1.3pp from 67.7% (management blames the 2nm ramp); operating margin of 58.7% beat the top of the 56–58% guide because opex was only 7.8% of revenue. Operating income grew 14.3% QoQ, yet pre-tax income grew only 5.6% and EPS (NT$29.43) 8.0%. Backing out from IR growth rates, non-operating income fell from roughly NT$96B to roughly NT$35B, which is why headline EPS trailed operations; the 6-K excerpt does not break down the line.

🔺 Capex was not raised again, but the hint of "more to come" got heavier. Q3 capex was US$13.8B (NT$443B), 12% below Q2's $15.7B; operating cash flow of about NT$920B left FCF of about US$14.9B, the highest of the last three quarters. But nine-month capex is about $40.5B, so hitting the $60–64B FY26 guide implies roughly $19.5–23.5B in Q4 alone, and the CFO said 2026–2028 capex will be "significantly higher than the past three years" while calling a 2027 number "a bit too early."

★ On valuation: forward PE is about 19.4x (23.1x in our Q2 piece); the ADR closed at $422.25 on 10/15, down 1.8% on the day. It looks cheaper because the price fell and earnings estimates rose, not because growth slowed.

Guidance: Full-Year Growth Raised, Capex Unchanged

Layer one: FY26 USD revenue growth moved from "slightly above 40%" to "around 44%", consistent with the Q4 guide midpoint of $53.3B (nine-month $122.8B + Q4 $53.3B ≈ $176.1B, about +44% on FY25; our own arithmetic check). The Q4 midpoint implies +14.1% QoQ, and IR cites about +58% YoY.

Layer two: the capex range did not move, but the implied Q4 of roughly $19.5–23.5B is more than 40% above Q3's $13.8B; management also said 2026–2028 capex will be "significantly higher than the past three years" and conceded that "tool price inflation is real." The gross margin guide of 65–67% has a 66% midpoint, 0.4pp below Q3's actual, and was not raised.

Earnings Call Takeaways (transcript obtained)

Changes in wording (against last quarter's wiki and our Q2 piece)

① Full-year growth language upgraded: "slightly above 40%" last quarter, "around 44%" now (IR and call). That before/after gap is a fact.

② Capex framing went from "long-term customer commitments" to "2026–2028 significantly higher": last quarter said only that this year's expansion was driven by long-term customer demand; this quarter adds "CapEx from 2026 to 2028 expected to be even more significantly higher than the past three years," a first public admission that "The tool price inflation is real," and a promise that "revenue growth will outpace the increase in CapEx."

③ The 3–4pp dilution was re-attributed: ⚠️ our Q2 piece wrote "3–4pp" as N2 ramp dragging the next quarter's gross margin. In this call, Wendell Huang put overseas-fab dilution at "closer to 2%" today, widening to 3–4%, and clarified that this applies to the latter part of the five-year period, not the next few quarters. Actual Q3 gross margin fell only 1.3pp and stayed inside guidance. The Q2 attribution does not match this quarter's wording; I cannot re-verify the original Q2 remarks and only flag the gap.

④ Profitability ceiling: "there is no ceiling for TSMC's profitability, but a dramatic increase will not happen overnight either." Against the host's narrative (Gooaye EP679) of gross margin edging toward 70% eventually, the company itself gave no 70% target, and the transcript has no long-term gross margin target.

Where analysts pressed

① 2027 capex, tool inflation, capacity growth (UBS's Sunny Lin, JPMorgan's Gokul Hariharan): how big next year's expansion is and what it costs in margin. ② How big AI demand is and whether CPUs count (Citi's Laura Chen, Bank of America's Haas Liu, Macquarie's Arthur Lai): whether the mid-to-high-50s% AI accelerator CAGR for 2024–2029 is being raised. ③ Customers and competition (Morgan Stanley's Charlie Chan on IDM customers insourcing, TD Cowen on the secondary wafer market and High-NA, Stifel on the GlobalFoundries interposer deal and pricing).

Evasions and vagueness

• Whether the AI CAGR is raised: only "It probably will be higher", no new number.
• Wafer capacity growth rate: "I cannot answer this question right now because of my customer continue to increase their demand."
• 2027 capex: "a bit too early."
• IDM customer: "I don't comment on a specific customer."
• Pricing on existing versus new contracts: "very strategic," scope unanswered.
All are asks for specific numbers that were declined, which makes them risk points for the next call or January's.

Bull vs Bear

Three reasons to be constructive:

① No sign of demand loosening. Revenue beat the top of guidance by 2%, full-year growth was raised about 4pp, inventory days fell to 82, and management called demand "extremely robust."

② Cash flow improved at the same time. Capex fell 12% while operating cash flow was about $28.8B and FCF about $14.9B, a three-quarter high.

③ Valuation came down. Forward PE of 19.4x is a little over four-fifths of the 23.1x in our Q2 piece, operating margin of 58.7% beat guidance, and the price is still sliding.

Three reasons for caution:

① EPS did not keep up with operations. Operating income +14.3% QoQ but EPS only +8.0%, with non-operating income backed out from roughly NT$96B to roughly NT$35B; if last quarter's non-operating gain included FX or investment gains, the underlying EPS step-up is actually sturdier, but the 6-K excerpt cannot confirm that.

② Capex trajectory rising, details refused. Q4 implies at least $19.5B, 2026–28 is "significantly higher," and the 2027 number and capacity growth rate were both declined; tool inflation is now acknowledged.

③ A dilution path for gross margin. Q3 fell 1.3pp, the Q4 midpoint drops another 0.4pp, and overseas-fab dilution is about 2% now, 3–4% later. The stock still closed down 1.8% on the print.

★ The fork: whether "more capex + slightly lower gross margin" is a fair price for capturing structural AI demand, or capital intensity stacking toward the next cycle's peak. The numbers side with the former for now, but the questions management declined (2027 capex, capacity, AI CAGR) are exactly the evidence the latter camp needs.
