UnitedHealth．Q2 2026 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Revenue of $112.0B, up just +0.4% YoY — almost no growth. Yet adjusted EPS of $6.38 against a $4.91 estimate is a +30% beat, and the stock rose 7% premarket.
The real story: the improvement comes mostly from fewer claims paid, not more revenue — the medical care ratio fell from 89.4% a year ago to 86.7%. Built from the IR release, call summaries, and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: UnitedHealth Group IR release + earnings call summaries Period: 2026-04-01 – 2026-06-30 Reported: 2026-07-16 before market open Compiled: 2026-09-26

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

📉 A huge EPS beat, driven by a structural improvement in the medical care ratio (MCR). Adjusted EPS of $6.38 vs. $4.91 expected — a 30% beat. MCR fell from 89.4% a year ago to 86.7%, also well below the ~88.5% analysts had modeled. This is the clearest sign yet that cost discipline is working after 2025's medical-cost blowup.

📊 But revenue growth is nearly zero — this is margin recovery, not a growth story. Revenue was $112.0B, up only +0.4% YoY, one of the weakest prints in recent quarters. UnitedHealthcare revenue of $86.0B was even slightly down YoY. The engine here is the cost side, not demand.

🔎 Days claims payable (DCP) keeps falling — worth watching closely. DCP dropped from 48.6 days in Q1 to 47.0 days. The 2025 crisis started with under-reserved claims — a continued decline in DCP is the first leading indicator for whether this recovery is durable.

★ Full-year guidance has been raised for a second straight quarter: adjusted EPS from >$18.25 to $19.50–20.00. Forward PE is 16.4x, slightly above the peer median — the valuation now reflects "recovery," not "undervaluation."

1. Medical care ratio (MCR): three quarters, against the guidance band

MCR fell sharply from 89.4% a year ago to 86.7% — the main driver of the EPS beat. But it rose QoQ (83.9% in Q1 to 86.7% in Q2, up 2.8pp), still inside the FY26 guidance band of 88.1%±0.25pp. This is likely seasonal (utilization typically varies by quarter) and not yet a sign of deterioration — but the QoQ direction is worth tracking into Q3.

2. Revenue is nearly flat — the recovery engine is on the cost side

Revenue was $112.0B, up +0.4% YoY, a modest beat against the $110.81B estimate, but one of the weakest growth rates in recent quarters. Breaking down the two main segments: UnitedHealthcare $86.0B (slightly down YoY) and Optum $65.7B (slightly down YoY) — neither major segment grew meaningfully; the +0.4% at the consolidated level comes mostly from smaller lines and pricing.

Optum's operating income is the one piece of "volume" good news this quarter: up 29% QoQ to nearly $4B, rebounding from Q1's 15% decline. But this is a single quarter's rebound — not yet enough data to call it a structural trend.

📌 Falsifiable test: if Q3 revenue growth stays below +1% YoY while MCR stops improving, this earnings recovery is entirely "one-time cost normalization," not "the business re-accelerating" — and those two stories have very different implications for the valuation.

3. Guidance: raised for a second straight quarter, same direction

The FY26 adjusted EPS guidance trajectory: >$17.35 (Q4'25 call) → >$18.25 (Q1'26) → $19.50–20.00 (this quarter) — raised for a second straight quarter, and this time management gave a specific range instead of a floor, a clear sign of rising confidence in second-half visibility. Operating cash flow guidance was quantified for the first time at ~$24.0B, alongside a commitment to at least $5.0B in buybacks — the first clear sign since the 2025 crisis that financial flexibility is being reactivated.

4. Valuation: fairly priced for "recovery," not "undervalued"

📈 Forward PE of 16.4x (GuruFocus, as of 2026-09-24), slightly above the Healthcare Plans industry median of 16.1x. Already well recovered from the single-digit PE during the 2025 crisis.

Cycle-intensity read (invest-playbook): managed care is a mid-intensity cycle industry driven by two variables — claims cost trend and federal rate policy. The 2025 crisis was a claims-cost (MCR) blowup, not a demand collapse; the 2026 recovery is cost-trend normalization, not the business re-expanding. The current forward PE fairly prices this "recovery from the bottom" phase, but it is not cheap — the genuinely undervalued window (single-digit PE) passed at the depth of the 2025 crisis.

5. Peer group scan

Group definition: large U.S. managed care / health insurance. Covers commercial insurance, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, and pharmacy benefit management (PBM) — the shared variables are claims-cost ratio and federal rate policy.

One cross-sectional note: the whole group took an unexpected claims-cost hit in 2025, and 2026 is broadly a "cost normalization" recovery story — the difference is the pace of recovery and Medicare Advantage exposure. HUM has the most concentrated MA exposure, CNC/MOH are exposed to Medicaid redetermination, while CI/ELV are relatively more diversified.

7. Bull and bear cases

Three reasons to be bullish:

① The MCR improvement is structural, not noise. It improved 2.7pp YoY and came in well below what analysts had modeled — cost-discipline actions (portfolio and pricing changes) are clearly working.

② Guidance has been raised for two straight quarters. Moving from a floor value to a specific range is itself a signal of rising management confidence.

③ Financial flexibility is coming back. Cash flow is healthy, capex is under 1% of revenue, and buybacks of at least $5.0B are back — the financial retrenchment of the 2025 crisis is thawing.

Three reasons to be bearish:

① Revenue growth is nearly zero. All of the improvement comes from "fewer claims paid" — if the MCR uptick continues into Q3, this earnings-growth engine disappears quickly. It is not a story that extrapolates linearly.

② DCP keeps declining. The 2025 crisis began with under-reserved claims — the direction here deserves close attention even if the absolute level isn't yet abnormal.

③ The comp base for this beat is 2025's worst year on record (MCR blew up, the CEO changed, guidance was withdrawn at one point) — a large part of the 30% beat is an easy comp, and shouldn't be linearly extrapolated into future quarters.

★ There is really one point of disagreement: is this MCR improvement genuine cost discipline, or just a bounce off an unusually bad 2025 base? Bulls and bears agree revenue isn't growing — the difference is whether you believe the cost-side improvement holds into Q3 and Q4.
