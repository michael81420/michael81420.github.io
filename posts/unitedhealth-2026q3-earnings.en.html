UnitedHealth．2026 Q3 Earnings, Unpacked ｜🧾

Adjusted EPS of $4.69 against about $4.14 expected is a beat of roughly $0.55 — but the quarter booked a $610M favorable reserve release (about $0.53 per share, derived), nearly the entire beat.
Meanwhile MCR rose from 86.7% in Q2 to 88.4%, revenue was $112.7B (-0.4% YoY), and the raised full-year guide implies Q4 EPS of only about $2.20. Built from the IR release and Futu data. Not investment advice.
Source: UnitedHealth Group IR / SEC 8-K + Futu Period: 2026-07-01 – 2026-09-30 Reported: 2026-10-13 Compiled: 2026-10-14

TL;DR · What actually mattered this quarter

🔎 The quality of the EPS beat is thin. Adjusted EPS of $4.69 vs about $4.14 expected (LSEG), +13%. But the IR release discloses $610M of net favorable reserve development, and it is not excluded from adjusted EPS. Roughly $0.53 per share after tax (derived); strip it out and EPS is about $4.16, essentially in line.

📈 MCR is back up and revenue turned negative — Q2's "fewer claims" story did not continue. MCR 88.4% (Q2 86.7%, +1.7pp QoQ; 89.9% a year ago), slightly above the 88.35% band ceiling used in the Q2 note. Revenue $112.7B, -0.4% YoY; Optum Health revenue -10% YoY, Medicare Advantage members -9.8% YoY.

🧮 Guidance went up, but the arithmetic says Q4 will be weak. FY26 adjusted EPS moved from $19.50–20.00 to "at least $20.50"; the first three quarters sum to $18.30, so Q4 only needs about $2.20 (derived), less than half of Q3's $4.69. Whether that is a conservative floor, seasonality, or no more releases cannot be settled without a transcript.

★ Forward PE is about 17.8x on Futu's forecast valuation. The stock gapped up about 5% at the 10/13 open (intraday high $399.88) and gave it all back, closing at $377.76 (-0.2%) — the market did not pay for this beat.

1. Medical care ratio (MCR): half of Q2's improvement given back

MCR still improved 1.5pp YoY (89.9% to 88.4%), but rose 1.7pp QoQ, and that 88.4% already includes the $610M favorable release (the IR ties it to 2026 dates of service). The release gives neither an ex-release MCR nor a UnitedHealthcare-only MCR, so how many points the release flatters is not available, and not estimated. The operating cost ratio of 12.8% (13.5% a year ago) is a separate, genuine YoY improvement.

📌 The Q2 note's test: "Q3 revenue growth stays below +1% YoY while MCR stops improving." Result: revenue -0.4%, MCR up QoQ — the condition is met. But MCR is still better YoY, so it is not "all one-time"; it is a smaller improvement of lower quality.

2. EPS beat decomposition: a $0.55 beat, about $0.53 of it from the reserve release

Fact (IR): net favorable reserve development was $610M this quarter; adjusted EPS excludes only intangible amortization ($373M), divestiture / South America held-for-sale items ($148M after tax) and restructuring items ($74M after tax). The reserve release is not excluded. Derived (not a reported figure): roughly $0.53 per share after tax, leaving adjusted EPS of about $4.16, essentially level with the $4.14 estimate.

This is my arithmetic, not company language: it uses the quarter's 22.2% effective tax rate (computed from IR figures) and assumes the full release sits in the adjusted basis. The exact figure may differ somewhat, but the magnitude says the beat is mostly not a better core business.

Versus Q2: that +30% beat was framed as structural MCR improvement. Q3 is also a beat, but the supporting evidence is much weaker — revenue turned negative, MCR rose QoQ, and the beat leans on a release.

3. Segments: Optum Health revenue -10%, but profit recovers from the floor

Two directions at once: volume is shrinking (Optum Health revenue -10% YoY on fewer value-based-care patients; MA members 7.605M, -9.8% YoY; Medicaid -10.3%; Optum Rx adjusted scripts 393M vs 414M, -5%) and margins are recovering (Optum Health operating margin 3.2% vs 0.2%, 2.6% adjusted; UnitedHealthcare 3.3% vs 2.1%). This is shrinking to earn more, not growth.

Membership: the quarter saw about 165K employer-client attrition and about 85K from Medicaid eligibility changes; MA and complex Medicaid populations are down 935K since year-end 2025. Q2 sub-segment revenue for Health/Insight/Rx was not retrieved, so no QoQ for those lines.

4. Guidance: raised, but the implied Q4 is much lower

EPS guide path: >$17.35 → >$18.25 → $19.50–20.00 → at least $20.50, raised three quarters running. But: (1) the first three quarters of adjusted EPS are Q1 $7.23 + Q2 $6.38 + Q3 $4.69 = $18.30 (Q1 from the prior note), so Q4 implied is at least about $2.20 (derived), under half of Q3; (2) this release does not restate revenue, MCR or operating cash flow guidance (Q2 gave ~$24.0B for cash flow, while nine-month operating cash flow is already $25.1B) — "not stated" cannot be read as maintained or raised; (3) there is no 2027 financial outlook either, only "further commitments expected in 2027."

5. Cash flow, balance sheet and capital return

Operating cash flow of $5.1B (1.2x net income) versus $11.1B in Q2 (prior note), about -54% QoQ; nine-month total $25.1B ($18.6B a year ago). Quarterly capex was not disclosed; nine-month capex was $2,421M ($2,674M a year ago) — quarterly FCF is not available and not estimated (shown as — in the site's earnings table).

DCP 47.0 days (Q2 47.0, year-ago 46.2): it stopped falling, so the warning flagged in the Q2 note did not worsen. DSO 16.9 days (Q2 17.7). Debt-to-capital 41.7% (Q2 41.2%) against a year-end target of about 40.0% — this quarter moved the wrong way. Nine-month buybacks $5.0B, dividends $6.2B.

8. Valuation: forward PE about 17.8x; the beat was not rewarded

📈 Forward PE about 17.8x (Futu forecast valuation, as of 2026-10-14); on the same source the trailing PE is 24.28x at the 57.2th percentile of the past year. Price $377.76 (10/13 close, Futu snapshot); 20 analysts, average target $485.59 (Futu).

⚠️ The Q2 note's 16.4x came from GuruFocus — a different source, so do not read it as a PE increase. On the guide floor, I compute $377.76 / $20.50 ≈ 18.4x (derived).

Cycle read (invest-playbook): managed care is a mid-intensity cycle driven by claims cost and federal rates; it is now in a "profit repair, shrinking scale" phase where quarterly MCR is dominated by reserves and seasonality. With lower earnings quality and falling revenue, 17–18x prices the recovery already — it is not cheap.

10. Risks and the other side

Good numbers, but…

① The beat leans on a release: $610M roughly equals the whole EPS beat; a release is not repeatable each quarter, and implied Q4 EPS of about $2.20 may reflect exactly that (inference, to be checked on the call).

② Scale is shrinking: revenue -0.4% YoY, MA members -9.8%, Optum Health revenue -10% — the profit repair sits on a smaller business, and nothing here says what drives 2027 growth.

③ Narrower guidance: revenue, MCR and cash-flow guidance are all missing; only an EPS floor remains.

The bull case's counter-evidence: ① operating cost ratio improved 0.7pp YoY and Optum Health's margin went from 0.2% to 3.2%, so part of the repair is structural; ② full-year EPS guide raised three quarters in a row, and DCP held at 47.0 days, so Q2's reserve warning did not worsen; ③ nine-month operating cash flow of $25.1B already exceeds the full-year figure guided at Q2.

★ The point of disagreement: is the ~$2.20 implied for Q4 a conservative floor, or an admission that Q3's earnings level is not sustainable?
